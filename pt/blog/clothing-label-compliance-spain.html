Spain Apparel Labelling: Spanish Composition, Regional Language and the Plastic Tax

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 4, 2026

Spain is the gateway to the Spanish-speaking market: local retail chains, fast-fashion headquarters and Latin American buying teams make many of their decisions here. Its labelling rules stack in three layers — EU Regulation 1007/2011, the 1987 Royal Decree on textile labelling (RD 928/1987), and the plastic packaging tax plus producer responsibility that came with the 2022 waste and contaminated soil law (Ley 7/2022). For a Chinese trims supplier the three most common failures are English fibre names, care symbols drawn to domestic habit, and packaging that was never declared for the plastic tax.

What follows covers composition, care and sizing, language, the packaging tax and textile producer responsibility, then closes with a pre-shipment checklist.

1. Three Layers: EU Regulation, RD 928/1987 and Regional Consumer Law

The EU regulation sets the frame, RD 928/1987 sets Spanish enforcement and the language requirement, and the autonomous communities — Madrid, Catalonia, Andalusia and others — add detail on consumer information. Prepare to the strictest layer: the cost is almost identical, and you satisfy both head-office document review and store-level checks.

2. Composition: Spanish Fibre Names and Percentages

Composition goes by weight percentage in descending order; a single fibre reads 100 % algodón. Names must be the Spanish statutory terms, not English. Very small components in a blend can be grouped as otras fibras (other fibres), but elastane and decorative fibres stay separate — as in Germany, do not fold 3 % elastane into other fibres for convenience.

Spanish customers often want composition, care, origin and size on a single care label, Spanish on the front and English or French on the back for onward shipment to Latin America. Allow four lines or more in the layout; do not discover at sampling that the text will not fit, because a care label typically offers a few square centimetres of usable area.

3. Care Symbols and Sizing: What Retail Actually Enforces

Care symbols follow ISO 3758, adopted in Spain as UNE-EN ISO 3758, with all five basics: washing, bleaching, drying, ironing and professional care. Spanish law has no separate mandatory symbol list, but consumer law requires the instructions needed for proper use, and retailers routinely put complete symbols into the buying contract. Sizes run ES 36–48 or the EU system, with childrenswear marked by both age and height.

💡 Orders that tranship to Latin America often need one Spanish label to serve Spain, Mexico and Colombia at once. Ask for the final country of sale before fixing the artwork: relabelling after arrival costs far more than the label, and the labour usually exceeds the label price itself.

4. Language: Spanish Is the Floor, Regional Languages Are the Bonus

Castilian Spanish is the floor for consumer information. Catalonia, the Basque Country and Galicia may require their own language under regional consumer protection rules, especially for packaging and instructions going to local stores. A two-sided hang tag or care label with Spanish plus the regional language adds limited cost and puts you on the compliance list of regional chains.

5. Packaging: The Plastic Tax and Producer Responsibility

Ley 7/2022 levies a special tax on non-reusable plastic packaging, charged on the weight of non-recycled plastic at the current rate of EUR 0.45 per kilogram, covering packaging produced in or imported into Spain. Poly bags, mailers, shrink film and plastic handles are in scope; paper hang tags and paper bags fall outside this tax but remain within packaging producer responsibility.

At the same time, RD 1055/2022 requires packaging producers to register and join a producer responsibility organisation, paying by material weight. When a customer asks for data, break grams down by material and state the recycled content: the recycled share directly reduces the tax base, and that figure is worth more than total weight. Suppliers who cannot provide it are usually asked to absorb the difference.

6. Textile EPR: The Variable of the Next Two Years

Spain is advancing separate collection of textiles and an extended producer responsibility scheme under Ley 7/2022, with apparel and home textiles to be phased in. The direct effect on trims suppliers: customers start asking for recyclable design and evidence of recycled or recyclable content, and the material choice for hang tags and care labels enters the assessment. Building mono-material, wash-resistant, low-composite structures into your standard range now is calmer than rebuilding after the questionnaire arrives.

7. Eight Checks Before Shipment

  • Every fibre name is the Spanish statutory term, with no English left over.
  • Percentages total 100 and run in descending order, with elastane listed separately rather than folded into other fibres.
  • All five care symbols are present and match what the garment really tolerates.
  • Sizes follow ES or EU, and childrenswear carries both age and height.
  • The final country of sale is confirmed and the language and label differences for Latin American transhipment have been checked.
  • Material weights and recycled content for plastic packaging are ready to enter the declaration.
  • Manufacturer and EU responsible person are complete and legible with name, address and contact.
  • Test reports cover formaldehyde, banned azo dyes, heavy metals and nickel release on metal parts, and are still valid.
