Trims in Cold Weather: Brittleness, Adhesive Failure and Winter Material Selection

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 5, 2026

Half of the trim problems on winter outdoor apparel are invisible during a room-temperature inspection. Soft PVC bags turn brittle at -15°C, self-adhesive labels lift at the edges in cold storage, care-label coatings crack and lose ink when folded, and paper hang tags that absorbed moisture warp once the cold arrives. All of it shows up after the goods have shipped or hit the shelf. This article covers what cold actually changes in a material, two cheap tests that catch it early, and how to choose by category.

1. Cold Changes Three Parameters

The first is glass transition temperature. Below Tg a plastic shifts from ductile to brittle; soft PVC relies on plasticiser to push Tg below zero, so the less plasticiser and the higher the recycled content, the higher the embrittlement temperature. That explains why two bags both specified at 0.05mm behave differently at -18°C: one folds, the other whitens at the crease.

The second is the adhesive. Water-based and standard hot-melt glues lose initial tack near 0°C; nothing shows at application, but a few days of transport vibration in the cold lifts the edges. The third is moisture content: board swells in humid air and contracts in the cold, so punch holes and die-cut edges become stress concentrators, and that is where warping and cracking start.

2. Six Typical Failures and Where to Head

The table runs from symptom to the materials where it concentrates to the direction of the fix, so it can be used directly when a winter complaint arrives. Note the last two rows: cold-related failures on metal hardware and elastic cord are usually blamed on another process, when the cause sits in the material.

Failure Symptom Where it concentrates Direction of the fix
Bag cracking Whitening at the fold, splits in transit Low-plasticiser soft PVC, thin LDPE, high recycled content Increase thickness, switch to LDPE or CPE, cap recycled content
Label edge lift Corners lift, the whole label eventually peels Water-based glue, hot melt with a high Tg Move to a low-temperature acrylic adhesive and state the minimum application temperature
Care-label coating cracks Ink loss after folding, incomplete lettering Over-thick coating, poor ink adhesion Thinner coating, flexible ink, add a fold test
Hang-tag warping Edges curl upward, the punch hole cracks Single-coated board, humid storage Double-coated or laminated board, control moisture and storage humidity
Elastic cord fatigue Slack after cold storage, no return to original length Cord built mainly on natural rubber latex Blend in polyester elastomer, or upsize to compensate for lost recovery
Plating loss on hardware Darkened edges, blistering after salt spray Thin-wall die castings, plating too thin Thicken the plating and run salt spray and cold cycling together

3. Two Tests That Are Enough: Cold Fold and Warm-Up Observation

The cold fold is the cheapest option: hold the sample at -20°C for four hours, then fold it 180° within five seconds of removal and look for whitening or cracking along the crease. Films can follow the low-temperature brittleness logic of ASTM D1790; where trims have no common standard, writing '-20°C, 4 hours, 180° fold, no cracking' into the specification gives supplier and client the same sentence, which removes most argument.

The second is warm-up observation: hold care labels and woven labels at -20°C for 24 hours, then bring them back to room temperature for two hours and check for stiffness, ink loss, coating cracks and dimensional change. Coating and label-stiffness issues that a cold fold misses usually surface here. Both tests together take under two days and cost a handful of samples.

💡 Tip: a domestic freezer sits around -18°C, so one overnight run gives a first screen. Formal confirmation still belongs in a lab or with a supplier report under the same conditions; a clearly written condition matters more than instrument precision.

4. Choosing by Category: Seven Positions and Their Cold Behaviour

The table covers the seven trim positions that come up on winter orders. It is not a recipe but an order of trade-offs: first how severe the failure would be, then what cost is acceptable, and only then hand feel and appearance.

Position Cold risk Recommendation
Poly bag Cracking LDPE or CPE, at least 0.04mm, with recycled content capped in the contract
Self-adhesive label Edge lift Low-temperature acrylic adhesive that still holds at -20°C
Paper hang tag Warping, hole cracking Double-coated board at 350gsm or more, laminated if needed
Woven label Stiffening, neck irritation Lower density and softer yarn, soft-edge cutting, less stiffening finish
Care label Coating cracks, ink loss Thin coating, flexible ink, with a fold test report attached
Elastic cord Loss of recovery Polyester elastomer blend, or upsize to compensate
Metal hardware Plating loss, darkening Thicker plating, tested with salt spray plus cold cycling

5. Put the Conditions in Writing, Not in Verbal Promises

Cold performance is most easily lost when a material is swapped: film from supplier A on one run, supplier B on the next, same thickness, same hand feel, and it cracks at -20°C. The specification should carry the grade, thickness, recycled content and test conditions, and any material change needs a fresh sample approval rather than a reference to the previous batch's approved card.

The reverse also holds: if the purchase order says only 'winter packaging bags', the supplier chooses by experience and neither side can prove anything when it fails. Writing down the minimum service temperature, the test method and the acceptance criterion costs half an hour and saves a rework run plus an air freight bill.

  • Specification states minimum service temperature, test method and acceptance criterion, for example -20°C, 4h, 180° fold without cracking
  • Approve samples under cold conditions, not only by hand feel at room temperature
  • Material or supplier changes reported in writing, with fresh sample approval before mass production
  • Run one cold fold at incoming inspection and file the result with the batch number
