Trim Carbon Footprint: Answering Brand PCF Questionnaires

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 22, 2026

Sustainability questionnaires now carry far more carbon items: what does one hang tag emit, is the woven label yarn recycled, does the bag contain recycled content? Apparel trims — hang tags, woven labels, care labels, packaging — are light per piece but high in volume and variety, which makes them the block most often missed in a brand's per-garment footprint.

This guide covers three things: which accounting basis the buyer is asking about, which data a trims factory already has on hand, and how activity data multiplied by emission factors yields a result that can be recalculated and explained.

1. Which Accounting Basis Is Being Asked For

Basis Typical question How a trims factory answers
Organisation level Annual emissions, renewable electricity share, reduction targets Annual figures from plant electricity and fuel bills, with the boundary stated
Product level kg CO₂e per 1,000 woven labels or per hang tag Calculate per functional unit and give the result with all assumptions
Material level Emission factors for paper, yarn and recycled content Cite published database factors, naming version and source

2. The Four Hot Spots in Trims

  • Materials: virgin versus recycled pulp, polyester and cotton yarn, coatings and adhesives — usually the largest share.
  • Energy: electricity and fuel for printing, weaving, die-cutting and laminating.
  • Finishing: foil stamping, lamination, UV and dyeing — the more passes, the higher the figure.
  • Transport and outer packaging: cartons, pallets and mode — air freight can be dozens of times sea freight.

3. Three Data Tiers a Trims Factory Can Offer, by Credibility

You do not need a full life-cycle assessment on day one. The first tier is material mass times a published database factor, the easiest start. The second uses your own activity data — electricity, fuel, consumable mass — times a factor, which lifts credibility noticeably. The third relies on supplier-specific factors, environmental product declarations or third-party verification: the easiest for a brand to accept and the slowest to obtain.

A rule of thumb: trims footprints follow the material. Reporting accurately how many grams of which material you used beats agonising over the workshop electricity bill.

4. Getting It Right: Functional Unit and Allocation

The brand counts pieces, the factory produces batches, so a functional unit bridges the two. Per 1,000 pieces or per 1 kg of finished goods is common; state the weight per piece and the brand can convert it to its own volumes. For plant energy, allocating by output or mass is simplest; where one line runs several orders at once, allocate by machine hours or output share and write the assumption into the report.

5. Three Traps When Answering the Questionnaire

  • Percentages without absolutes: a thirty percent reduction with no baseline cannot be verified and invites questions you cannot answer.
  • Unproven wording such as carbon neutral or eco-friendly: scrutiny of green claims is tightening everywhere, and unsubstantiated environmental claims carry rising risk.
  • Quoting numbers straight off a supplier brochure: once you are asked for the source and method, there is nothing to show.

6. The Practical Route: Fold Carbon Data into Existing Records

Field Source Frequency
Electricity (kWh) Meter or utility bill Monthly
Fuel consumption Gas meter or purchase order Monthly
Main material mass (kg) Material requisition Per batch
Output (pieces or kg) Production report Per batch
Shipping mode and destination Shipping documents Per batch

With this table in place, answering how much carbon 1,000 woven labels carry becomes arithmetic that can be recalculated, not a guess. When the brand asks again next year, you swap the factor version instead of collecting data from scratch.
