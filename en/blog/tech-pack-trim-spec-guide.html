How to Write Trim Specifications in a Tech Pack

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 19, 2026

Main fabric arrives with a swatch card, a g/m² figure and a shrinkage test. Trims often arrive as one line of text: 'woven label, black, 40 mm'. The factory builds to its own house standard, the buyer inspects against an imagined one, and the sample comes back three times before anyone notices the two were never describing the same thing. This guide breaks the trim block of a tech pack into six fields and shows what to actually write for hang tags, woven labels, care labels and cords.

1. Six Fields: Miss One and the Sample Comes Back

Field What to write Most often missing
Trim code A unique code that traces back to the PO Name only, no code
Construction and material Yarn, substrate, weave, folded ends, thickness 'Polyester' tells nobody anything
Dimensions with tolerance Finished size plus a ± tolerance, and what is measured Die size instead of finished size
Colour reference Colour code, ΔE tolerance, light source 'Dark grey', two words
Placement and quantity Which seam, which part, how many per garment 'At the neck' and nothing else
Compliance fields Per market: fibre content, care, origin, responsible party Deferring compliance to 'the customer will provide'

2. Trim Codes That Survive a Purchase Order

A workable structure is type-colour-key dimension-construction-revision, so WL-BLK-40-F2 means woven label, black, 40 mm wide, folded at both ends, revision 2. The length does not matter. What matters is that the same string appears on the tech pack, the purchase order, the packing list and the carton label. Mixed stock in the warehouse and mislabelled garments on the line are usually two departments using two names for one thing.

3. What to Actually Write for Four Common Trims

Trim Parameters to state How it is measured
Hang tag 350 g/m² board, 0.45 mm caliper, lamination, 3 mm hole, 120 mm cord loop Finished size, excluding die bleed
Woven label Warp and weft yarn, weave, density, 40 mm width, cut or folded edge, 60 °C wash Cut width, excluding the fold
Care label Nylon taffeta, 20 mm width, print method, care symbols per ISO 3758, 40 washes Text and symbol height on the face
Cord Material, 1.5 mm diameter, 150 mm cut length, knot type, migration test Cut length, not loop length
A dimension stated in the wrong convention costs more than a missing one: die size on a hang tag yields a tag 6 mm smaller than expected, and a woven label width that includes the fold adds 10 mm at the neck. One clause after each number — what exactly is measured — usually saves an entire sampling round.

4. Tolerances and Measurement Method

Tolerances should follow what the trim process can actually hold, not what the garment spec says about fabric. Printed hang tags: ±1 mm on finished size and ±5% on g/m². Woven labels: ±2 mm on width, ±3 mm on length. Care labels: ±2 mm on the cut. Cords: ±5 mm on cut length. State the sampling method alongside — five pieces per lot, laid flat for five minutes, measured with a stated instrument. A tolerance without a measurement method is not a tolerance.

5. Compliance Fields: One Row per Market

Compliance is not a field to defer to the customer; it is part of the specification. European Union: fibre composition under Regulation (EU) 1007/2011, care symbols following ISO 3758, and since GPSR (EU) 2023/988 an EU responsible party plus traceability information on the label or packaging. United States: care labelling under 16 CFR 423, fibre content under the FTC textile rules, and a CPSIA tracking label on children's items. Japan: 家庭用品品質表示法 requires 混用率, 取扱表示 and 原産国. Korea: 전기용품 및 생활용품 안전관리법 requires 품질 표시 — 섬유 혼용률, 취급 표시 and 원산지.

6. Revision Control: One Spec, One Revision Letter

Change a spec by adding a revision, never by editing the old one: void the previous revision but keep its sample, add a letter to the new one, mark what changed, and switch only after written buyer approval. The cheapest practice in the real world is a signed limit sample with every revision — the supplier builds to it, inspection judges against it, and the text only explains the delta.

The most common cause of resampling is not an impossible process; it is one spec carrying two revision letters — one quoted from, one ordered from, one inspected against. Put the revision letter in the filename and in the PO and the problem disappears.
