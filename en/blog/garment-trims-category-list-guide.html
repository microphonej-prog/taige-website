Types of Garment Trims: Why You Should Sort Them by Function, Not Material

By TAGE Packaging &nbsp;·&nbsp; Updated November 24, 2026

Garment trims are usually bought from a list somebody inherited. That list mixes material with function — metal, plastic, paper — so nobody notices that the care label and the poly bag were approved by different people on different dates. Sorting by function instead of material fixes this: one group, one owner, one set of numbers to sign off, one failure mode. This guide lists the eight functional groups that appear in every BOM, the trims that belong in each, and the single number that should never be missing from the specification.

1. Eight Functional Groups, Not Eight Materials

A BOM sorted by material looks tidy and hides responsibility. A BOM sorted by function tells you who signs off. The eight groups below cover what a garment actually needs; anything outside them belongs either to the garment itself or to the retail fixture. There is a second benefit: trims in the same group usually share one colour standard and one approval meeting, so a missing line means reopening the whole group.

Group Typical trims The number to write down
Closure and fastening zippers, buttons, snap fasteners, hook-and-eye tape, hook-and-loop pull-off strength in N
Structure and reinforcement fusible interlining, piping, shoulder pads, back-neck tape, elastic waistband g/m² plus shrinkage %
Identification and labelling woven main label, care label, size label, heat transfer label, barcode sticker fibre content wording plus care standard
Hanging and retail hang tag, string or plastic fastener, price ticket, security tag, euro hole tag size in mm plus attachment type
Elastic and tape woven and knitted elastic, webbing, ribbon, binding tape width in mm plus recovery %
Decoration embroidery, patches, lace, fringe, sequins, hotfix stones stitch density or stones per cm²
Packaging poly bag, paper bag, mailer, header card, tissue paper, desiccant thickness in µm or g/m²
Functional and safety reflective tape, FR trim, antibacterial finish, needle-detection requirement the standard the claim rests on

2. Identification Labels: The Group Mistaken for Printing

Labels look like printing and are quoted like printing, but they are legal documents. In the EU, fibre composition must be stated under Regulation (EU) 1007/2011 and care symbols follow EN ISO 3758; in the US, content and care rules come from the FTC textile regulations, and children's items add a tracking label. The practical consequence: this is the only group where editing a sentence changes compliance, so it should never be edited by whoever is laying out the hang tag.

  • Main label: brand mark and size, carrying no compliance claim
  • Care label: fibre content plus the five symbol families for wash, bleach, dry, iron and professional care
  • Size label: the size system the market expects, not a translation of one
  • Price ticket: a retail tool, usually printed locally so dead stock does not travel twice
  • Barcode sticker: GTIN plus batch, verified to an acceptable grade before mass printing

3. Closure and Structure: How a Cheap Trim Gets Expensive

A zipper that fails takes the garment off the rail; an interlining that shrinks 3 % takes the whole lot out of shape. Both are decided by numbers that are easy to put in a specification and easy to skip. Ask for pull-off strength in newtons for every closure, and for g/m² plus shrinkage at the fusing temperature for every fusible. A 40 g/m² interlining and a 60 g/m² one look identical in a swatch book and behave completely differently after one wash at 40 °C.

A rule of thumb for a first order: any trim that carries load (closures, straps, hang tag strings) gets a numeric strength value on the purchase order; any trim that changes dimension (elastic, interlining, tape) gets a tolerance in mm and one test wash.

4. Packaging: The Only Trim the Customer Grades

Every other trim is inspected by the factory. Packaging is inspected by the person who opens the parcel, and that judgement becomes a review before the quality report arrives. Two numbers decide most of the experience: bag thickness in micrometres, which settles whether the bag feels like a bag or a film, and the printed mark, which is what makes a parcel look dispatched rather than merely shipped.

5. Three Items Missing From Almost Every First BOM

We read incoming BOMs for a living, and the same three lines are missing again and again.

  • Spare button bag with its own label: nearly free to add, and the first thing a customer reaches for
  • A second care label language: France, Spain, Japan and Korea expect the wording in the local language, and English alone will not pass
  • Desiccant and bag venting decided together: one without the other either traps moisture or lets it in

6. From List to Specification: Six Fields Are Enough

A trim line can be quoted without a follow-up email once it carries six fields: reference and revision, material with grade or g/m², size in mm with tolerance, colour reference with an acceptable Delta E, packing quantity with carton count, and the acceptance test with the standard it rests on. Add the approver's name and the date, and the list becomes a control document. That is also the moment a trim order stops being a conversation and becomes comparable between suppliers.
