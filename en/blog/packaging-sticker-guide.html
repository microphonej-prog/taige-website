Packaging Stickers for Apparel Brands: Materials, Adhesives and Placement

By TAGE Packaging · Updated November 12, 2026

A poly bag sealed with a printed sticker is the first physical object a customer touches after opening the courier box. It is also the cheapest branding surface in the trim package: a 40 mm circle costs a fraction of a hang tag and still carries the logo, the thank-you line and the size.

This guide covers the four jobs a sticker actually does, the face materials that decide how it looks, the adhesives that decide whether it stays on a PE bag, the printing options, where it must not go, and the recycling rules that now shape the material choice.

1. The four jobs a sticker actually does

2. Face materials: looks, humidity and recyclability are decided here

  • Coated paper 80–150 g/m²: the workhorse face — good print, low cost, but it is not water resistant.
  • Kraft 80–120 g/m²: visible fibre and a natural look for organic-positioned brands, equally humidity sensitive.
  • White PP 50–80 µm: waterproof, tear resistant and the most reliable on a PE bag — the default for seal stickers.
  • Clear PET 36–50 µm: use it when the garment must show through, but print a white backing first or the colours turn grey.
  • Metallised or holographic PET: maximum shelf impact, but it cannot travel with paper packaging in the recycling stream — keep it for gift boxes, not volume packaging.

The face decides three things: how the print looks, whether it survives a damp warehouse, and whether it can be recycled together with the packaging it sits on.

3. Adhesives and the surface they land on

Most sticker failures are adhesion failures, not print failures. PE and PP bags have low surface energy — around 31 mN/m — so a low-tack or removable adhesive simply slides off in a cold truck.

  • Permanent acrylic: the general-purpose choice, stable from about -20 to 80 °C, works on PE bags and cartons.
  • Removable: good on cartons and garments, peels clean, but do not use it on a PE bag.
  • Low-temperature grade: for cold chain and winter warehouses, still tacky below 0 °C.
  • State adhesion in N/25 mm: at least 3 N/25 mm for PE bags, around 1.5 N/25 mm is enough on carton.
  • Wipe oil and dust off the surface first; final adhesion is reached after 24 hours, so do not run the drop test the same minute.

4. Printing and colour

  • Digital: short runs, variable data, no plate cost — the sensible choice below about 1,000 pieces.
  • Flexo or letterpress: lowest unit price at volume, worth it from about 5,000 pieces.
  • Clear PET and clear PP need a white ink backing, and the opacity of that white decides whether the colour is right.
  • Match colour to a Pantone reference, not to a verbal impression, and expect variation between rolls of the same order.
  • Gloss or matte varnish on top: matte resists scuffing and looks more premium, but it greys down dark colours — proof it first.

5. Placement rules and recycling compatibility

Two rules decide placement: the sticker must not hide information the buyer needs, and it must not contaminate the recycling stream of the packaging it sits on.

  • Seal across the bag opening fold, half on the front and half on the back, so opening the bag destroys the sticker and the seal actually proves tampering.
  • Never cover the suffocation warning — both US and EU markets require it to stay legible.
  • Keep barcodes and recycling marks clear — the sorting line reads the barcode, the recycling plant reads the mark.
  • Paper stickers on paper bags and cartons; a PP sticker on a paper pack is screened out as a contaminant at the mill, so the recycling claim collapses.
  • The EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, PPWR (EU) 2025/40, turns recyclable design into a requirement; France separately requires the Triman sorting mark and sorting instructions on packaging, and Germany requires registration in LUCID under the VerpackG.
  • A compostable claim needs EN 13432 certification; a compostable sticker without a certificate is greenwashing, and both EU and French authorities check it.

In Japan, packaging must be sorted and marked by material under the Container and Packaging Recycling Act, and environmental claims fall under the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations — an eco or degradable claim needs evidence, with sizes always in mm. Korea requires material and separate-discharge marking under the Act on the Promotion of Saving and Recycling of Resources, with consumer-goods quality labelling under the Electrical Appliances and Consumer Products Safety Control Act.

6. QC, storage and ordering

  • Die-cut tolerance ±0.5 mm; for machine-applied rolls, match the wind direction and the dispensing direction to the applicator.
  • Storage: 20 °C and below 50% RH, with roughly a 12-month shelf life; reseal partly used rolls to keep humidity out.
  • Incoming inspection covers three things: adhesion on the actual bag material after 24 hours, register accuracy, and white ink opacity.
  • MOQ: 1,000–3,000 pieces for stock sizes and from 5,000 for a custom die, with samples in 5–7 days and production in 10–15 days.
The sticker is the last thing between your garment and the customer's hands. Specifying the adhesive by surface — PE bag, tissue, kraft box — prevents the most common complaint we see: a seal that lets go in transit and a bag that arrives open.
