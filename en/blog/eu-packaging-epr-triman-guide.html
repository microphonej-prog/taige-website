EU Packaging EPR and Sorting Labels: What to Print, Where, and Who Supplies It

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 28, 2026

European buyers have started adding a question before ordering: do we need a recycling logo on our packaging? That is not a layout question but EPR — extended producer responsibility for packaging. Whoever places packaging on the market of an EU country must register, report and pay fees there; the sorting logo and instructions printed on bags and boxes — France's Triman being the best known — are the visible corner of that obligation. For garment packaging it decides what gets printed, where, and who supplies the artwork.

1. EPR Is a Registration Duty; the Logo Is Its Surface

EPR makes whoever places packaging on the market carry the cost of collecting and treating it. Each member state implements it with its own law, so registries, reporting rules and logo requirements differ widely by country. The obligated party is usually the brand, the importer or the cross-border seller — in e-commerce often the seller itself — not the bag maker or the printer. But the logo must be printed on packaging we produce, so our role is to print it correctly, not to register on the brand's behalf.

A second pressure comes from the marketplaces: major cross-border platforms already treat packaging EPR registration numbers for France, Germany and others as listing or compliance requirements, and items without them can be restricted. Brands therefore lock packaging artwork earlier, and the logo's position and colour get frozen earlier too.

2. Quick Market Overview: Different Registries, Different Logos

The table below is directional only: for each country, follow the local producer responsibility organisation or official guidance on details, timing and which packaging needs a logo.

Market Who registers and pays Typical logo on packaging Practical note for brands
France Brand or importer, registering with the national REP scheme for a unique ID Triman logo with sorting information (Info-tri) Colour and minimum size follow the official brand rules; a one-colour version suits clear bags
Germany Anyone placing packaging on the market, registering on the packaging register and joining a system Mostly registration and licensing; usually no mandatory graphic logo Keep the registration number and licence contract ready; platforms often ask for them
Spain Producers must register and file annual declarations Sorting marking follows the local scheme's guidance Spanish-language labelling is often required: check wording and logo together
Italy Producer obligations under the national packaging consortium Environmental marking by material, including material codes Paper and plastic are marked separately; check how laminated bags are classified

3. 'Is a Hang Tag Packaging?' Ask the Buyer, Do Not Assume

This is the argument that comes up most in practice. One reading treats a label attached to and delivered with the product as part of the packaging; another treats the hang tag as a product information carrier (printed matter) outside packaging responsibility. Definitions and platform practice are not harmonised, and the same shipment can get different answers in France and Germany. The safe route: get written confirmation from the buyer — country, artwork file, version — then decide whether the logo goes on the bag or the tag. If both readings are possible, keep the logo on the bag or box and leave brand and visual content on the tag.

4. Getting the Logo Printed: Five Production Questions

  • Colour and size: official rules fix colours, clear space and a minimum size; white or black monochrome is common on clear bags. Do not go below the minimum, or sorting equipment reads the logo less reliably.
  • Which face: the lower right of the front or the bottom of the back is common. Avoid vent holes, warning text and barcode quiet zones, and do not break the existing layout to squeeze it in.
  • Printing method: both offset and screen printing work, and fewer colours cost less. At volume the logo can share the plate with existing artwork, adding almost nothing; at small volume an extra colour raises the unit price noticeably.
  • Material matters: on clear OPP and PE bags, check ink adhesion and migration risk; kraft and white card are more stable. On darker recycled substrates, increase contrast for pale logos.
  • Version control: logo files and registration details get updated, so leave a low-cost change zone in the layout. When a brand changes its logo or logo version, old stock should be used up by a deadline or scrapped as agreed, so old and new never ship together.

5. Seven Things to Confirm Before the Packaging Goes to Print

  • Which countries and channels this shipment goes to (logos differ by country, and platforms add their own rules)
  • Who the obligated party is: brand, importer or the cross-border seller
  • The logo file and version: vector artwork, colour values, minimum size, clear space
  • Position and area of the print, and its relation to vent holes, warnings and barcodes
  • Whether the registration number, sorting instructions or material code also need printing
  • How the new logo fits the existing layout, and whether new impositions or plates are needed
  • The changeover plan and how to handle old stock, so old and new never ship together
