France's Textile EPR and the Triman Mark on Care Labels

By TAGE Packaging · Updated November 25, 2026

France's textile EPR scheme asks the label printer to register nothing, yet every obligation ends up on the care label. Since the AGEC law (Loi n° 2020-105) reshaped extended producer responsibility, each textile item placed on the French market carries an eco-contribution declared by the brand and, in most cases, a Triman mark with a sorting notice (info-tri) printed on the product. We make custom care labels in Dongguan for brands selling into France, and the same questions return every season: where the mark goes, how small it can be, and whether it still reads after five washes. This guide answers all three from the label side.

1. The Duty Sits With the Brand, the Change Lands on the Label

The obligated party is the producer, meaning the company that first places the item on the French market, usually the brand rather than the factory. Registration with an approved textile producer responsibility organisation such as Refashion, and the per-item eco-contribution declaration, sit on the brand side. What arrives at the factory is only the marking: Triman and info-tri on the product, plus the fibre composition required by Regulation (EU) 1007/2011. French consumer law also requires consumer-facing information to be in French, which is why a French-market care label rarely shares one artwork with the German version.

  • Producer = brand: registration and per-item declaration, not the label supplier.
  • Marking duty: Triman and info-tri belong on the product, in practice the care label or the brand label.
  • Language: French for the French consumer, so the French and German versions cannot share one artwork.

2. Three Rules That Decide the Label Layout

Rule or scheme What it requires Effect on the label
AGEC law (Loi n° 2020-105) and Code de l'environnement L541-10 Brand registers for EPR and declares the eco-contribution per item The product must carry Triman and the info-tri sorting notice
Regulation (EU) 1007/2011 Fibre composition and percentages, with tolerances for blends The care label needs two lines for composition, clear of the care symbols
Code de la consommation (French consumer code) Consumer-facing information must be in French A separate French artwork, roughly 25% more characters than English

3. Where the Mark Goes: Three Positions and a Millimetre Budget

The default position is the care label, because it is already mandatory and stays with the garment to the end. The alternatives are the woven brand label and the size label, and both are tighter: a 20 mm woven label with an end fold leaves about 16 mm of printable field, enough for the Triman alone but rarely for the sorting sentence. A third option prints the notice on the hang tag, except the tag is removed at purchase, so the mark leaves the product — a point brand auditors have started to check. Our recommendation: Triman on the care label, and the full info-tri on both the care label and the hang tag.

4. What Has to Fit on a 30 x 60 mm Label

On a 30 mm x 60 mm label with 1.5 mm bleed on each side, the printable field is about 27 x 57 mm. Six elements compete for it: fibre composition with percentages, care symbols, size, origin, Triman plus the sorting sentence, and the brand's own code. The conflict we see most is care symbols and Triman fighting for the same corner, since both need to stay legible. Our layout puts two lines of composition on the left, five care symbols in EN ISO 3758 order on the right, and a band 8 mm high along the bottom for Triman and info-tri.

5. Legibility After Five Washes: Tape, Ink, Edge

Legibility is a print engineering question, not a design one. We run colour fastness to domestic washing to EN ISO 105-C06 and control the wash procedure to EN ISO 6330; a label that loses half its contrast at 40 °C in one cycle is a return, even when the fibre percentages are exactly right. Three variables decide it: the tape (coated nylon and polyester satin absorb ink differently), the ink (resin thermal transfer versus water-based is the widest gap), and the edge finish (ultrasonic cut versus woven selvedge fuzz differently after washing). Recycled polyester tape is available, but bring the GRS transaction certificate — otherwise recycled content can live in your supply chain file, not on the label.

Triman is a legal marking, not a certification logo. It says the item has a defined sorting and collection route in France; it does not say the garment is recycled or recyclable. Using it on a hang tag as an environmental selling point walks straight into green claims territory.

6. Cost and Lead Time: What Actually Moves

The eco-contribution never appears on a label quotation. What moves the price is three things: the screen or plate for one more colour, a slightly wider tape so the Triman stays legible, and an MOQ of 3,000–5,000 pieces per design. Adding one colour and 8 mm of width typically shifts a 30 mm care label by a low single-digit percentage; the real cost is the reprint when the sorting sentence does not fit after sampling, and that reprint usually costs more than widening the whole run.

7. Six Checks Before the Artwork Goes to Press

  • Is the buyer EPR-registered in France? If not, the marking is only decoration.
  • Is the info-tri wording the current version? These notices change without a press release.
  • French runs about 25% longer than English; set the white space against the French version.
  • Is the Triman at least 8 mm wide? Below that it becomes three arrows and no shape.
  • Keep care symbols in EN ISO 3758 order; do not rearrange them for visual balance.
  • Will you verify appearance after washing? We attach the EN ISO 105-C06 report by default.
