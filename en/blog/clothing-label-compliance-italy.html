Italian Clothing Labels: Italian Text, Made in Italy and CONAI

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated October 23, 2026

Italian clothing label compliance is rarely about fibre content. The difficulty sits in three local rules: the Italian language, the legal boundary of Made in Italy, and the CONAI environmental label on packaging. The first two decide whether a label passes retail intake; the third decides whether poly bags and paper bags are accepted at the warehouse — and that is where Italian rejections have clustered over the past two years.

This guide covers five things: the scope of Italian-language marking and its fixed terminology, the four items on a permanent label, the difference between Made in Italy and 100% Made in Italy plus the red line for subcontract manufacturing, how the CONAI packaging mark is applied, and sizing consistency across three places. For factories producing for Italian brands and sellers shipping to Italy directly.

1. Italian Is the Language Required by Law

Italy's Consumer Code (Legislative Decree 206/2005) requires consumer information to be given in Italian. Fibre composition still follows EU 1007/2011, but the terminology must be the standard Italian set: composizione, lavaggio, prodotto in, importato da. English-only labels are frequently rejected outright at department store and multi-brand intake.

In practice, print Italian and English side by side with Italian first. If the client wants a single language, make it Italian — the market does not accept English-only as readily as the Nordics do. Avoid literal translation: Machine wash cold is not Lavare a macchina freddo but Lavare in lavatrice a freddo.

2. Four Items on a Permanent Label

  • Fibre composition (composizione): descending by weight, using standard Italian fibre names.
  • Care instructions (lavaggio / cura): ISO 3758 symbols plus Italian wording, in consistent symbol order.
  • Origin (prodotto in): the actual country of manufacture, e.g. Prodotto in Cina — an Italian brand does not make the product Italian.
  • Responsible party (importato da): name and address of the EU importer or the Italian entity answerable, traceable to a legal person.

The four can share one care label or be split between a main label and a care label. Italian multi-brand buyers are more sensitive than Nordic ones to how the care label feels and where it sits — a stiff label at centre back neck is often moved to the side seam or hem, so settle it at sampling.

3. The Legal Boundary of Made in Italy

Italian law No. 55 of 8 April 2010 provides that using the Made in Italy designation requires design, manufacture and processing to take place mainly in Italy. Performing only the final operations there — attaching labels, pressing, packing, inspection — is not enough. This matters most in subcontracting: an Italian brand made in China can only carry the real country of manufacture.

100% Made in Italy is a different tier: a voluntary certified mark introduced by law No. 166 of 2009, requiring all processing in Italy and verification by an authorised body. It is not a decoration a company prints for itself; without verification it cannot be used. Italian customs and the Guardia di Finanza routinely pursue both marks, and false marking is a criminal risk, not merely a labelling defect.

💡 Tip: if the client's hang tag artwork says Made in Italy and you manufacture in China, do not print it. Stop and get written confirmation — in many cases the client reused an old file. Changing one word to the real country is far cheaper than a detained shipment.

4. The CONAI Packaging Environmental Label

Since 1 January 2023, Italy requires packaging placed on the market to carry an environmental label stating the material code and the sorting instruction — PAP 21 with Raccolta carta for paper, PE 4 with Raccolta plastica for plastics. This covers hang tag bags, poly bags, paper bags and outer cartons. Many trims suppliers watch the garment label and forget that the packaging they deliver is caught by the same rule.

What this means in practice: reserve space on the poly bags and paper bags you supply to Italian clients, and let the client confirm the code and sorting text against their own CONAI filing rather than guessing. Multi-material structures — paper-plastic laminates, metallised film — are where codes are most often wrong, so insist on written confirmation.

5. Terminology and Size Consistency

Italian womenswear commonly uses IT 38–52, which does not match EU 34–48 numerically. The size on the hang tag sticker, the main label and the care label must agree. Size confusion is one of the top return drivers in Italy, especially when cross-border sellers print the EU size straight onto the tag.

6. Four Mistakes That Actually Happen

  • English labels with Italian stickers. A sticker counts as temporary marking, multi-brand buyers reject it, and if it falls off in store the garment is effectively unlabelled.
  • Reusing an old Made in Italy file. The client's old artwork is unchanged, the factory prints it, and the goods are treated as falsely marked on arrival.
  • Packaging missing the CONAI mark. No material code or sorting instruction on bags means the client cannot receive them and the whole lot is rebagged.
  • Three sizes that disagree. IT 40 on the tag, M on the main label, EU 38 on the care label — the shopper buys by the EU number, the garment does not fit, and the brand absorbs the return.

7. Seven Checks Before Sampling

  1. Who writes and who proofs the Italian text — align terminology on composizione, lavaggio, prodotto in, importato da.
  2. Made in Italy and 100% Made in Italy: confirm the manufacturing country and reply in writing, never by verbal understanding.
  3. Go through each packaging item for CONAI marking: hang tag bag, poly bag, paper bag, outer carton.
  4. Size system: unify tag, main label and care label on the IT size, adding an EU conversion where useful.
  5. Legibility after washing: is the Italian still readable after three washes? Be careful with coated paper labels.
  6. Warning text: small parts on childrenswear, suffocation warnings on poly bags — add them per the client and EU practice.
  7. Document consistency: origin and responsible-party address matching across label, invoice and packing list.
