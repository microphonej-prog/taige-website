Woven Label Color Matching Guide: Pantone, Shade Control & Colorfastness

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated August 30, 2026

Woven label color matching is where clothing brands and suppliers most often disagree. The same Pantone number looks completely different printed on paper, dyed into fabric, or woven into a label — and shades can drift between batches. This guide explains how Pantone works on woven labels, shade control standards (Delta E), colorfastness grades, and the sampling approval flow to minimize rework.

1. Why Woven Label Colors Are Harder to Match

  • Woven colors come from yarn, so only pre-dyed yarns are available — no blending like ink
  • Weave structure (plain, satin) and thread density change how colors read
  • Yarn luster differs (polyester is shiny, cotton is matte), so the same color number looks different
  • Dyed-yarn stock is limited; new shades need yarn lab dips, usually adding 3–5 days

2. Using Pantone on Woven Labels

色卡类型适用场景注意
Pantone 纺织色卡（TCX）织唛对色的首选棉布材质印刷，最接近织物观感
Pantone 配方指南（GP）印刷吊牌、包装设计纸张印刷色卡，直接用于织唛会偏差较大

Best practice: give your supplier the TCX number, a reference image and a physical color sample. Combining all three greatly improves first-pass color approval.

3. Shade Control: Delta E & Approval

  • Delta E quantifies color difference; the lower the value, the closer the match; visible differences usually start around Delta E 1.5–2
  • Industry practice: keep woven labels within Delta E 1.5 (measured under D65 light, 10-degree observer)
  • Bulk orders are sampled under AQL; color/appearance defects are usually accepted at AQL 2.5 or stricter
  • Compare colors under standardized lighting, never ordinary fluorescent tubes that cause misjudgment
💡 Tip: if your brand color is critical (evergreen logo colors), ask the supplier to reserve dyed-yarn lots and run random shade checks on bulk production to minimize batch drift.如果品牌对颜色极其敏感（如常青款LOGO色），建议下单时要求供应商预留色纱批次，并在大货中随机抽检对色，把批次偏色风险降到最低。

4. Colorfastness Grades

Woven labels sit against skin and face repeated washing, so poor colorfastness causes bleeding. Three tests matter most: washfastness (grade 3–4 or better), rubbing fastness (dry 4 / wet 3 roughly), and perspiration fastness (3–4). Domestic and export standards differ slightly; always confirm against buyer requirements or the agreed test standard.

Polyester labels generally outperform cotton on colorfastness; dark and fluorescent shades are hardest — always verify with actual samples.

5. Sampling Flow & Batch Consistency

  1. Provide TCX number + reference image + physical color sample
  2. Mill dyes yarn as needed and weaves a strike-off; yarn lab dips usually take 3–5 days
  3. Approve the lab dip under standard lighting and confirm in writing (email confirmation is fine)
  4. Sample bulk against AQL, keep sealed shade references for re-verification
