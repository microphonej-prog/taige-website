Childrenswear Trims Small Parts Safety: Cylinder, Pull Tests and Documentation

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 13, 2026

Small parts are the fastest way for a childrenswear trims lot to fail: one button, rivet or zip pull that drops whole into the small parts tester stops the shipment. Recall notices repeat the same line, a decorative button detached and a young child may swallow it. This guide covers the test criteria, how pull and torque tests are run, what documentation to keep, and five design moves that pass without adding cost.

1. The criterion: a cylinder decides the shipment

The CPSC small parts cylinder is 31.7 mm across and 57.1 mm deep — 16 CFR 1501 — and applies to products for children under three. EN 71-1 uses the same cylinder for toys intended for children under 36 months. The test is not a judgement of size: the part must be dropped in under its own weight, with no pressure, and it must fit entirely. Attached components are abuse-tested first — pulled and twisted — and then tested, which is where many factories fail: they test the finished state and skip the post-pull state.

2. Which trims count as small parts

Component Main risk Usual practice
Decorative buttons, snap studs Loose stitching; the stud enters the cylinder whole Backing washer, double ring of stitching, 90 N pull or better
Rivets, eyelets, grommets Fabric tears and the whole piece comes off Add a reinforcement patch behind, choose a longer-leg prong
Zip sliders and pulls A removable pull fits whole in the cylinder Use a fixed pull or an extended pull that cannot enter
Hot-fix stones, beads, sequins Adhesive fails and single stones detach Move infant pieces to embroidery or print; keep stones for 3 years and up

3. Pull and torque: force values, method and the usual misunderstanding

CPSC abuse testing scales force by age group: 10 lbf (about 44 N) for 0–18 months, 15 lbf (about 67 N) for 18–36 months and 3–6 years, applied together with a torque test. EN 71-1 requires 90 N tension and 0.45 N·m torque, held for 10 seconds, on components of toys for children under 36 months. China's GB 31701 sets a minimum 70 N attachment strength for infant garments. The usual misunderstanding is that holding the force is a pass: the part must still not become a small part after the pull, and must not present an accessible sharp point.

4. Three more checks that arrive with the same inspection

  • Sharp edges and points: unbuffed metal stampings and burred button rims fail outright
  • Cords and drawstrings: EN 14682 limits hood and waist cords on childrenswear up to 14 years, and cord ends must not carry metal toggles
  • Magnets: two or more swallowed magnets attract each other and can perforate the bowel; both the US and EU treat them as high-risk, so avoid magnetic closures on infant wear

5. What documentation to keep, and what the buyer will ask for

The US market wants a Children's Product Certificate and a permanent tracking label carrying manufacturer, production date and batch, and place of manufacture. The EU wants a declaration of conformity plus the EU-based responsible person, in line with the traceability duty in GPSR (EU) 2023/988, and the label must carry the age warning and any required safety wording. The defect most often written up in an audit is documentation that does not match the goods: retention samples, test report and purchase order must all carry the same lot number.

6. Five design moves that add no cost

  • Move a decorative button from two holes to four and lock-stitch twice; it beats heavier fabric
  • An 8 mm reinforcement patch behind a rivet costs cents and often lifts pull strength by 20 % or more
  • Make the pull one-piece or long enough that it cannot be mouthed; cheaper than changing the whole zip
  • Reserve hot-fix stones for 3 years and up; move infant pieces to embroidery or print and step off the test path
  • Write the age band into the hang tag and spec: 0–3 and 3-plus follow different rules, so stop applying the strictest one to everything

7. Pre-shipment checklist

  • Zero components enter the cylinder under their own weight after the pull test
  • Pull strength reaches 90 N in the EU, 70 N for infant garments in China, the age-banded values in the US
  • No burrs on metal parts, button rims chamfered, no catch when a finger is drawn across
  • Three retention sets, the test report and the purchase order all carry one lot number, retrievable on demand
Small parts safety does not cost money in the lab — it costs money in tooling. Once the button mould is cut, the labels printed and the goods sewn, a failed test means scrap or a downgraded domestic sale. Fix the age band and market first, then the trims construction, and talk about price last.
