Hem Tags: The Woven Label at the Hem, and How to Specify It

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 21, 2026

A hem tag, also called a side-seam tag or hem label, is a short woven strip sewn just above the hem and beside the side seam. It runs 15 to 40 mm wide and shows 10 to 20 mm once folded. It carries no legal duty, yet it does the one thing brands most often forget: it makes someone remember who made the garment in the ten seconds they turn it inside out. Direct-to-consumer labels treat it as a second business card, fast fashion treats it as decoration, workwear treats it as a use cue. Done well it is a memory hook; done badly it is a stiff edge that rubs skin.

1. Where It Goes: Three Garments, Three Positions

On tees and sweatshirts the common position is the left side seam, 2 to 6 cm above the hem, folded so it faces inward and is only seen when the garment is turned. Hoodie cuffs use the same treatment. Denim and workwear put it inside the back waistband or beside the coin pocket, where 40 mm is acceptable because heavy fabric and coarse thread swallow a narrow tab. Knits and fine fabrics are better served by the inside centre line 10 cm below the back neck, away from where the neck rubs, with softened edges. Once you fix the position, draw it on the tech pack dimension sheet so three factories do not each invent their own.

2. Four Specs: Width, Fold, Cut Edge and Density

Widths of 15 to 20 mm suit a small wordmark, 25 to 30 mm suit one line of copy, and only 35 to 40 mm holds two lines of small type. There are three folds: end fold, the default, with 10 mm turned at each end; centre fold, for tabs that hang from a ring; and loop fold, used on waistbands and webbing so stitching never reaches the face. Cut the ends ultrasonically or hot, never with a cold blade: a cold-cut woven edge frays and shows whiskers after about five washes. On construction, satin reads cleanly but wears, damask wears better and suits workwear, and 12 to 16 warp and weft threads per centimetre is the usual band.

3. Three Things It Cannot Replace

The first is fibre composition and care. Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 requires composition on a durable label attached to the garment. Japan's Household Goods Quality Labelling Act requires composition by blend ratio, care instructions and country of origin in Japanese. Korea's Quality Management and Safety Control of Industrial Products Act requires blend ratio, care marking and origin, with care symbols under KS K 0021. Taiwan's Commodity Labelling Act requires composition, washing method and origin in Chinese. A slogan on a hem tag is safe; putting composition and care on it means asking a 20 mm strip of cloth to carry a durable label duty, and ink that fades after a few washes becomes a claim. The second is size: size belongs on the durable label or the hang tag, in cm and go numbers under JIS L 0001 in Japan, and as hose numbers 55, 66, 77 under KS K 0050 in Korea. The third is traceability of the manufacturer or importer: GPSR (EU) 2023/988 requires a responsible economic operator and traceability information on the product.

4. Sewing It On: Five Settings That Decide If It Puckers

Eight to ten stitches per inch is the usual band for woven labels: denser stitches stiffen the tab, sparser ones let the edge float. Hold a 3 mm seam allowance, because anything past 4 mm turns out after washing. Needle size 11 to 14 follows fabric weight, and knits want a ball point to avoid cutting yarn. Use polyester thread with the same shrinkage as the shell fabric, not cotton: cotton shrinks more and the tab goes wavy after a wash. The fifth setting is placement. Keep the lower edge of the tab parallel to the hem and hold side-to-side variation to plus or minus 2 mm, which is the one item you can actually measure at inspection. Pull test at 50 N on a 20 mm sample, then five washes at 40 °C to confirm no colour loss and no fraying.

5. Five Things to Send Before You Order

First, the copy and its type height. Chinese characters carry many strokes, so keep the smallest character at 1.5 mm or more; 1.2 mm is the floor for Latin capitals. Below that, satin weaves the shapes into a blur. Second, colour: give a Pantone number, not a photograph, and always sample when reversed type sits on a dark ground. Third, fold and direction: which way the first letter faces and whether the face turns inward or outward once folded, written as one sentence plus a sketch. Fourth, the sewing method: single needle, double needle or bartack, because factory defaults differ. Fifth, quantity and lead time: custom woven labels usually start at 3,000 pieces per design, sampling runs 7 to 10 days and bulk 12 to 18 days, and peak season means ordering two weeks earlier. Send all five at once and the quotation stops taking three rounds.

The value of a hem tag is not its size, it is whether it appears in the right place. Of the six faces of a garment, the shopper looks at the neck first and the hem last, which is exactly when the decision to keep it is made. A 20 mm strip that states the name cleanly, keeps its edges soft and weaves its letters true does more than one more graphic on the front.
