Colombia Clothing Label Compliance: Spanish Text, Fibre Content and Importer Details

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 18, 2026

Colombia apparel labelling compliance comes down to two hard rules: Spanish language and permanent attachment. Every consumer-facing detail — fibre content, country of origin, importer information and care instructions — must be in Spanish and either sewn in or durably attached so it stays legible after washing.

This guide is written for clothing brands sourcing from China and their Colombian importers. It breaks down the mandatory label items, acceptable ways to state fibre content, care symbols, extra requirements for childrenswear, and ends with a checklist you can copy straight into a quotation request or sampling brief.

1. The Legal Framework and Who Enforces It

Colombia's consumer protection statute (Estatuto del Consumidor, Law 1480 of 2011) requires sellers to give consumers clear, truthful, sufficient information expressed in Spanish. For apparel, fibre content, care instructions, country of origin and the responsible party (manufacturer or importer) are mandatory disclosures — and they cannot live on the hang tag alone.

  • Regulator — the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio (SIC) enforces consumer protection and can order non-compliant goods withdrawn from sale and impose fines
  • Market checks — both customs and market inspectors may pull garments for label checks; English-only labels and missing fibre content are the most common findings
  • Permanence — composition and care information must be sewn in or durably attached; a removable hang tag is extra information and cannot replace the care label

3. How to Write Fibre Content So It Passes

  • Descending order, total 100% — list fibres by weight from highest to lowest and make the percentages add up to 100%
  • Tolerances — the industry norm is roughly ±3% labelling tolerance; for functional or speciality fibres, declare measured values so a lab test cannot contradict the label
  • Spanish fibre names — algodón (cotton), poliéster, elastano (spandex), viscosa (rayon), nailon or poliamida (nylon), lana (wool), lino (linen)
  • Do not skip components — lining, rib, lace, filling and removable liners each need their own declaration; multi-part garments are listed component by component
  • Typical mistakes — saying blend without percentages, using codes such as PE or PES, care label and hang tag disagreeing, or quoting fabric weight where a percentage belongs

4. Care Instructions and Wash Symbols

Care symbols normally follow the international system (ISO 3758): washing temperature, bleaching, drying, ironing and professional care. Colombian shoppers are less symbol-literate than European ones, so pair symbols with a short Spanish line.

  • Ready-to-use Spanish lines: Lavar a máquina a 30°C; No usar blanqueador; Secar a baja temperatura; Planchar a baja temperatura; No lavar en seco
  • Care text must match how the fabric actually behaves: shrinkage, colour fastness and coated or laminated fabrics that cannot take high ironing temperatures — otherwise after-sales claims and returns land on the brand
  • Printing must survive washing: coated woven tape and wash-resistant inks outlast standard printing across repeated home washes (see our care label wash durability guide)

5. Extra Requirements for Childrenswear

  • Cords and drawstrings: neck and hood drawstrings and over-long waist cords create strangulation risk; most markets restrict them following cord-safety practice such as the EN 14682 framework
  • Small parts: buttons, fasteners, tag pins and trims should pass pull-force tests so they cannot detach and be swallowed
  • Where buyers require it, we support third-party testing for saliva colour fastness, formaldehyde, azo dyes and phthalates by supplying samples and documentation
  • Add an age range on the care label or hang tag (for example 2-3 años) so retail staff and parents can check it at a glance

6. Choosing the Label Format: Neck Label, Care Label or Hang Tag

  • Neck label (woven) — brand mark and size; the Spanish product name can be woven in as well
  • Care label — composition, origin, responsible party and care text; this is the primary carrier of mandatory data, and coated satin printing keeps it legible after washing
  • Hang tag — price, barcode, QR code, brand story; excellent for marketing, but it is not where mandatory data belongs
  • If existing labels are English-only, you can sew in a Spanish secondary label while keeping the original — as long as the Spanish information is complete, durable and sensibly placed

7. Sampling and Production Checklist

  1. Confirm the target market and the final Spanish wording from your importer — legal responsibility for compliance sits with the importer
  2. List composition percentages for every component (lining, rib, lace, filling)
  3. Decide label format and placement: centre back or side seam, waistband, hem
  4. Finalise care symbols and the Spanish wording, aligned with actual fabric test results
  5. Approve samples: woven labels, care labels and hang tags usually ship in 3-7 days — wash one piece once before you sign off
  6. Bulk lead time is typically 10-25 days depending on quantity and finishing; proofread neck label, care label and hang tag Spanish text together to avoid three inconsistent versions
💡 Tip: regulations and enforcement practice change. This guide is a working framework — the current rules in the importer's country and the customs paperwork always prevail. We typeset and print the Spanish wording your buyer confirms; we do not give legal advice.
