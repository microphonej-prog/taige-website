Folding Methods for Garment Packaging: Retail, E-commerce and Gift Box

By TAGE Packaging &nbsp;·&nbsp; Updated November 9, 2026

A garment that arrives creased has already lost the unboxing. Folding is the step where the bag size, the carton count and the impression on opening are all fixed at once, and it is usually the step nobody writes down. Two suppliers given the same verbal instruction will produce two different panels, and the difference only shows up when the poly bags arrive.

This guide sets out the three fold standards used in apparel supply, the arithmetic that turns a folded panel into a bag and a carton size, the interleaving that keeps fabric from marking, and the packaging obligations in the EU, Japan, Korea, Brazil and Spain that decide what has to be printed on the bag.

1. The three fold standards and the channel each one serves

Choose the fold by channel, not by habit. A shop-floor garment needs a panel that sits flat and lines up with the hang tag; a parcel needs a compact block that survives handling; a gift box needs layers that open without creases.

  • Retail fold with a backing board: garment folded to a fixed panel, board inserted, pin or clip optional. The board is what keeps the corners square on a shelf.
  • E-commerce compact fold: garment folded to a smaller panel, air pressed out, slid into a mailer. Handling matters more than a perfect crease line.
  • Gift fold with tissue: garment folded around tissue, tissue interleaved between layers, then into a box with a further sheet on top.

Write the panel size into the tech pack in cm — for example 28 × 20 cm for a folded shirt, measured flat and closed — and state which edge the fold line sits on. Without that figure the bag order is a guess, and a guess that is one centimetre short turns into a cutting charge or a fold that is redone at the warehouse.

2. From folded panel to bag and carton size

Bag size is panel size plus ease: allow 1,0 to 1,5 cm per side, so a 28 × 20 cm panel needs roughly 31 × 23 cm. Carton size comes from the count times the compressed thickness, and the compressed figure has to be measured on a packed stack rather than calculated from one garment, because ten folded pieces press down to less than ten times one.

3. Interleaving and keeping creases out

Creases come from three things: fibre memory, pressure, and time in the carton. Acid-free tissue at 17 g/m² between layers stops dye transfer on dark and printed fabrics; a thin plastic sheet does the same job but is harder to recycle, and it is what most audits now ask you to replace. For wool and wool blends, fold along the grain and avoid a hard press on the seam line. For coated and polyurethane fabrics, interleave and skip vacuum compression, which leaves a shine that no iron removes.

4. What the packaging itself has to carry, market by market

Folding decides the bag; the market decides what is printed on it. In the EU, packaging falls under PPWR (EU) 2025/40, and in Germany under VerpackG with a LUCID registration — whoever puts the packaging on the market registers it. France adds the AGEC law waste-reduction duties and the Code de la consommation rules on mandatory mentions, and Spain applies Ley 7/2022 on waste and contaminated soils. Japan applies the 容器包装リサイクル法 with material marking, and Korea applies the 자원의 절약과 재활용촉진에 관한 법률 for separation marks plus the 품질경영 및 공산품 안전관리법 for product safety marking. Brazil requires information in Portuguese and an identified responsible party under the CDC (Lei 8.078/1990), following ABNT NBR practice on packaging.

Two consequences follow for the fold. First, the bag needs a material code and the responsible entity printed, so the print area is not optional decoration but a legal field — leave it in the artwork from the start. Second, the care label inside must stay readable without breaking the seal, because the buyer or an inspector will check clothing label requirements through the bag: under (EU) No 1007/2011 the fibre composition and the care symbols have to be legible, and a German Pflegeetikett must show the Faserzusammensetzung. That is why a clear label window, or a transparent bag panel, is part of the folding decision rather than a packaging detail added later.

5. Before the bag is sealed: a six-point check

  • Panel measures within 5 mm of the tech pack figure, on a sample of ten pieces, not one.
  • Fold line sits on the edge named in the spec, and the shoulder is not twisted.
  • Tissue between layers on dark, coated and printed fabric: 17 g/m², acid-free, two sheets on the face side.
  • Hang tag and size sticker are visible through the bag, not hidden behind the fold.
  • Bag carries material code, responsible party, and the suffocation warning where the market requires one.
  • Carton count and stack height match the packing list before the carton is taped, so the fold does not change the shipping weight.
Rule of thumb: fix the panel in cm once, in the tech pack, and let the bag and carton follow. Folding to fit whatever bags happen to be in stock is how a shipment loses its shelf presentation and its packing list at the same time.
