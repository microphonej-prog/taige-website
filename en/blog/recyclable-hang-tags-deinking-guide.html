Recyclable Hang Tags: How Lamination, Foil and Plastic Fasteners Break the Paper Loop

By TAGE Packaging · Updated November 25, 2026

A 350 g/m² hang tag is, on paper, one of the most recyclable objects on a garment: board, print, a punched hole. In practice it is often not recyclable at all — a 12 to 18 µm BOPP film, a hot-foil logo and a plastic fastener turn the board into a composite that most mills will not take. We make custom hang tags in Dongguan, and buyers no longer ask us to say the word recyclable; they ask for the mill report that backs it. This article is about what that report actually tests, and which design choices have to change.

1. What Recyclable Is Actually Testing

Paper recyclability comes down to two lab questions. Repulpability: can the fibre be dispersed and recovered at a usable yield? De-inkability: can the print be lifted off so the recovered fibre can go back into white paper? European mills work to published methods — PTS-RH 021/97 from the German Papiertechnische Stiftung, ATICELCA MC 501 in Italy, and the 4evergreen Circularity by Design evaluation protocol. A 300 g/m² tag printed with water-based ink and finished with a water-based varnish normally passes both. Add a plastic film and the first question has no answer.

Ask for the mill's repulpability report, not the printer's opinion. If the reply is a PDF with a leaf logo and no test method or reference number, there is no report.

3. Ink Choice Matters More Than Ink Colour

Offset, UV-cured and water-based flexo all print a hang tag; they do not de-ink the same way. In most mill trials water-based inks lift more cleanly than UV-cured inks, because the cured UV film is cross-linked onto the fibre. Soy ink is not a recyclability claim either: soy oil is often only the carrier, with a resin binder doing the work, and de-inking behaviour follows the binder. What moves the needle regardless of ink type is coverage — keep total ink coverage moderate and avoid a full-bleed black reverse on the recycled version of a design.

If the brand insists on a dark tag, print light on dark board instead of flooding the sheet: unbleached kraft with a one-colour logo reads as premium and still goes through the pulper.

4. Board Spec: Weight, Thickness, Hole

  • 300–350 g/m² coated one side is the standard tag stock; 400 g/m² and above for heavy premium tags.
  • Thickness is quoted in mm or µm. A 350 g/m² C1S board usually lands between 0.42 and 0.48 mm; take the figure from the mill datasheet rather than a rule of thumb.
  • A 4 to 5 mm hole is normal for retail hanging; below 3.5 mm the paper tears at the edge under load.
  • On a euro hook, reinforce with 0.5 mm board or a paper patch instead of a metal eyelet.
  • Kraft and recycled stock vary in shade between lots — agree the tolerance on a physical standard, not on a screen.

5. Cost and MOQ Reality

The foil-free, film-free version of a tag is usually cheaper, not more expensive: you remove a die, a foil pass and a lamination pass. The difference moves into the paper — at small volumes, recycled and FSC-certified board costs more per tonne than virgin C1S, and the FSC claim needs a chain-of-custody certificate held by the printer, not by the paper merchant.

  • MOQ for a custom die-cut tag is typically 3,000–5,000 pieces per design; below that, digital cutting is the cheaper route.
  • Removing foil also removes a one-off die cost, so short runs benefit most.
  • Water-based varnish instead of lamination saves a process pass but may need a heavier board for the same stiffness — cost both together.
  • Certified recycled board carries a per-tonne premium that is usually absorbed in the unit price at 5,000 pieces and above.

6. What You May Write on the Tag

In the EU, PPWR (EU) 2025/40 governs packaging, and a hang tag is not packaging — the bag it ships in is. The recycling logo therefore belongs on the bag; on the tag it may only refer to the tag's own fibre, and only with the mill report behind it. In the US, the FTC Green Guides apply to the tag as advertising. Write the claim narrowly: FSC-certified board, water-based inks, no plastic film, with the grammage in g/m² and the sheet thickness in mm on the specification.

7. Before Artwork Is Released

  • Board: mill, grade, g/m², FSC or recycled claim with certificate number.
  • Finishing: no film, no foil, water-based varnish — written on the job sheet, not just in an email.
  • Attachment: cotton string or paper cord, no plastic fastener.
  • Shape: do not die-cut narrower than 8 mm at any point; it snaps in the mailer.
  • Claim text: cleared by the brand's legal team, with the mill report reference next to it.
