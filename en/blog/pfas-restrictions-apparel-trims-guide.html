PFAS Restrictions and Apparel Trims: From Deadlines to Fluorine-Free Options

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated October 11, 2026

PFAS — per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances — is the fastest-moving restriction in today's apparel supply chain. The EU concentration limits for PFHxA apply to clothing, accessories and footwear from 10 October 2026, while California's AB 1817 and New York's apparel law have been in force since 1 January 2025. Neither measure stops at fabric: any water-, stain- or grease-repellent coating on hang tags, woven labels, webbing, zips or packaging bags can pull a whole shipment into a non-compliant position. Trims are a small line on the invoice, yet the hardest part to replace — mapping fluorine exposure early is far cheaper than reacting when a customer asks for test reports.

1. What PFAS Are and Why Trims Get Pulled In

  • A very broad family: PFAS covers thousands of fluorine-based substances rather than one chemical, and rules usually judge a product on two tracks — intentionally added PFAS and total organic fluorine content
  • Function drives the residue: water, stain and grease repellency, heat resistance and easy-clean surfaces have traditionally been delivered by fluorinated finishes — the better the performance, the more likely fluorine is present
  • Judged as one product: rules assess the garment together with its accessories and packaging at the level of inseparable components, and trims are never an exception
  • Thresholds keep tightening: limits are moving from ppm to ppb, while routine analytical detection limits for fluorinated substances fall in step

2. The Seven Places Fluorine Hides in Trims

3. Regulatory Timelines: Which Deadline Bites First

MarketRuleKey dates and thresholds
European UnionREACH Annex XVII restriction on PFHxA and related substancesFrom 10 October 2026 for clothing, accessories and footwear for the general public plus food-contact paper and board: 25 ppb for PFHxA and its salts, 1,000 ppb for related substances, measured in homogeneous material; extended to other textiles on 10 October 2027
European UnionProposed EU-wide universal PFAS restriction under REACHThe Risk Assessment Committee opinion was adopted in March 2026; the Socio-Economic Analysis Committee final opinion is expected by the end of 2026, after which the Commission will draft the restriction; an 18-month general transition period has been proposed
California, USAAB 1817 on PFAS in textile articlesSince 1 January 2025 the manufacture, distribution and sale of textile articles with intentionally added PFAS — or at or above 100 ppm total organic fluorine in the product or components — is banned; the threshold drops to 50 ppm on 1 January 2027; outdoor apparel for severe wet conditions has until 1 January 2028
New York, USAEnvironmental Conservation Law §37-0121 on PFAS in apparelSince 1 January 2025 the sale of new apparel with intentionally added PFAS is banned; from 1 January 2028 this extends to outdoor apparel for severe wet conditions, subject to concentration limits set by the state DEC; retailers are advised to obtain a manufacturer compliance certification
Other US statesState laws in Colorado, Washington, Maine, Vermont, Connecticut, Rhode Island, Minnesota and othersDisclosure requirements started phasing in from 2025, such as Colorado requiring a made with PFAS chemicals notice on severe-wet outdoor apparel, turning into bans through 2026–2029, with Minnesota requiring reporting from 2026 and a full sales ban by 2032

These rules keep moving: before signing or shipping, confirm against the latest official texts and the requirements in force in the customer's market. This table is for prioritisation, not legal advice.

4. The Three Document Sets Customers Ask For First

  1. Material and supply chain declarations: a line-by-line BOM statement on whether fluorinated finishes, coatings or membranes are used, with category, supplier, process and signatory — far more useful than a one-line no PFAS claim
  2. Test reports: state the method, sampling location and batch. Use targeted analysis for known substances of concern and total organic fluorine to judge the overall fluorine level; a PFOA/PFOS-only report carries little weight in markets that judge on total organic fluorine
  3. Substitution and process notes: whether fluorinated finishes are used, how the fluorine-free alternative performs and survives washing, plus the cost and timing of switching — customers use this to answer their own brand clients

5. How to Test Without Wasting Money

  • Split into homogeneous materials: break the product into inseparable components and test each one, with reports issued per component — a hang tag, its string, the tag pin and the bag are four separate test objects
  • Two methods, two jobs: targeted analysis (LC-MS/MS, EN 17681 series for textiles) confirms known substances; total organic fluorine by combustion ion chromatography shows whether fluorine is present overall, covering unknowns
  • Reports must match the goods: photos of the sample, batch number, submission date and lab accreditation — CNAS/CMA or an internationally recognised scheme — or neither the customer nor customs will accept them
  • Screen the high-risk parts first: start with the two or three components most likely to contain fluorine, then decide on a full panel — much cheaper than ordering everything at once
💡 Tip: many unexpected detections come not from the main material but from release paper, sewing thread, mould release agents or upstream auxiliaries. Break the BOM down to its finest level and ask about auxiliaries item by item — that usually resolves the risk in a single round.

6. Fluorine-Free Alternatives and What They Cost

  • Fabric and webbing: fluorine-free repellents (silicone, wax, PU based) are a mature route with comparable initial performance; retention after repeated washing is usually a little lower than fluorinated systems and can be offset by higher add-on or a laminated layer
  • Components: zips and laminates can move from PTFE to fluorine-free TPU membranes and PU coatings, and waterproof bags can switch to heat-sealed construction with PU seals
  • Paper packaging: switch to acrylic fluorine-free grease barriers, or add a PE/PLA extrusion coating or an inner liner; food-contact uses must also satisfy food-contact material rules
  • Cost and time: replacement auxiliaries usually add only a small material premium; the real spend is trial production during the switch, wash-durability validation and third-party testing. Stabilising bulk production typically takes a few weeks and longer for high-performance outdoor categories, so plan it batch by batch
