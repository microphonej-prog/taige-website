Country of Origin Marking for Clothing: Getting Tag, Label and Bag to Agree

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 28, 2026

When a buyer asks whether you can put Made in China on the hang tag, the email looks like one line but it really asks three questions: where it goes, how it is worded, and who checks it before the order is placed. Country-of-origin marking is read by both customs and the end consumer, and it is the single item most often missed at the trims stage — and the most expensive to fix once the batch is printed.

This article does not repeat the full legal framework for labelling (look those up per market in the US or EU compliance guides). It focuses on the three printed surfaces a trims supplier actually controls — hang tag, care label and packaging bag — covering market differences, wording, production points and the checks to run before ordering.

1. Origin Marking Is Not the Same as a Certificate of Origin

Origin marking is a duty to declare: goods must state their country of production honestly, for customs identification, statistics and consumer information. A certificate of origin (CO, Form E, EUR.1 and similar) is a different track: it decides whether the shipment enjoys preferential duty rates. Neither replaces the other — holding a CO does not mean the packaging can skip the origin country, and printing the origin country does not replace the origin document.

For a trims supplier the rule is simple: mark the actual place of production. If a client asks for a different country, the brand must confirm legality and procedure with its customs broker; we print according to the artwork the client confirms in writing, and keep that confirmation on file.

2. Read All Three Surfaces Together: Hang Tag, Care Label, Bag

In one shipment the origin usually appears on three surfaces. When they disagree, that is the most common source of customs queries, marketplace reviews and consumer complaints — and all three are made in the trims factory, so the checking duty naturally lands at the trims stage.

Surface Common practice What to watch
Hang tag Bottom of the front or the company block on the back, next to the website and service line Artwork changes often and this line is the easiest to forget; keep the type legible
Care label and neck label Same or facing panel as fibre content and wash symbols, usually in the same block as origin Highest wash durability: woven or coated is safer; offset inks fade
Packaging bag (OPP or PE bag, paper bag, box) Small type on the back or bottom; EU and US buyers often specify the position One-colour print on clear film reads poorly: use a darker or bolder ink, and avoid seals and folds

3. Market by Market: One Sentence, Different Rules

  • United States: imported goods must show country of origin, and Made in USA is tightly restricted; wording, placement and exemptions follow FTC and customs guidance
  • EU and UK: non-preferential origin rules plus consumer information, enforced by member-state customs; label language follows local and catalogue requirements
  • Middle East: Saudi Arabia, the UAE and others often expect Arabic alongside English, ideally on both packaging and hang tag
  • Latin America: Brazil, Argentina, Peru and others mark in Portuguese or Spanish, often together with the importer's name and tax number
  • Japan and Korea: English marking is generally acceptable; where local law prescribes a language, add that text as required
  • Marketplaces: the origin country field in the back office must match the printed surface, as audits check the physical item

4. Wording and Production Points

  • Wording: use standard phrasing such as Made in China or Product of China; do not invent abbreviations or hybrid wording, and keep other information out from between product name and origin
  • Consistency: When the origin appears on more than one surface, keep the wording identical, including capitals, hyphens and spacing
  • Wash resistance: For care labels prefer woven or wash-resistant coating; if printing is required, run three to five wash cycles before final approval
  • Layout: Keep the type on hang tags large enough and keep it clear of bleed and trim lines so it is not cut off in the finished piece
  • Clear bags: use a dark or bold single colour on clear film, or move the text onto a paper card, sticker or paper bag where it reads better

5. Five Mistakes We See Repeatedly

  • Disagreeing surfaces: the tag says Made in China while the bag says Designed in Italy — easily read as misleading
  • Omission: the client updated only the care label artwork, the hang tag kept the old file, and the finished goods carry no origin
  • Cut away: the origin line sat near the bleed or trim, so the finished tag keeps only half a line
  • Wrong language: packaging for Middle East or Latin American clients carries English only and is rejected at inspection
  • Third-country requests: where the brand asks for another country for customs reasons, obtain written confirmation before the press runs to avoid later disputes

6. Pre-Order Checklist

  1. Confirm the origin country as the actual place of production, once, including spelling
  2. Check that all three surfaces — tag, care label, bag — carry it
  3. Is it ordered clearly next to care, fibre and size information
  4. Does the destination market need a second language or Arabic
  5. Does it match the invoice, packing list and certificate of origin
  6. File the approved artwork and reuse the same version on reorders rather than re-typing it
💡 A practical habit: build one fixed information block — origin, company name, website, service email — and let the tag, the care label and the bag share that same text. Change it once and all three stay consistent.
