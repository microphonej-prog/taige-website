Metal Labels and Metal Hang Tags Guide: Materials, Finishing and Attachment

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 11, 2026

Metal labels and metal hang tags have become a popular premium trim for apparel, bags and high-end brands. Compared with paper tags and woven labels, metal feels more durable and substantial; the weight in the hand communicates premium quality immediately, which is why luxury, denim and sportswear brands use them.

This guide explains the materials, surface finishes, attachment methods and compliance points of metal labels and hang tags, so brands can lock down the key parameters before ordering.

1. Types of Metal Labels and Their Applications

  • Metal hang tags: replace or combine with paper tags, common on denim, leather goods and streetwear
  • Metal nameplates: riveted or sewn onto leather patches, webbing or bag panels to highlight the logo
  • Zip pulls: functional and decorative, a common metal fitting on bags and outerwear
  • Metal charms and chains: combined with patches and tags to add a trendy, gift-box feel

2. Comparing Common Metal Materials

MaterialCharacteristicsBest For
Stainless steelCorrosion-resistant, strong, can be brushed or mirror polishedPremium tags and nameplates
BrassWarm tone, easy to etch, can be plated in gold or antique bronzeVintage and premium brands
AluminumLightweight and lower cost, can be anodised in colourMass-market apparel and e-commerce
Zinc alloyDie-castable into complex shapes, rich detail, controlled costCustom-shaped tags and zip pulls
Iron / tinplatePaintable and cheap to stamp, suited to budget tags

3. Surface Finishing and Decoration

  • Electroplating: gold, silver, nickel, antique bronze or black nickel — this sets the overall tone
  • Brushing / mirror polishing: brushed is subtle and refined, mirror is bright and eye-catching
  • Sandblasting / matte: a matt finish that resists fingerprints, good for large surfaces
  • Etching: chemical etching for fine graphics, in recessed or cut-through form
  • Screen printing / laser engraving / epoxy dome: add colour, precision and a tactile feel to metal

4. Attachment and Fixing Methods

  • Punched hole with cotton cord, plastic fastener or chain, for standalone hanging tags
  • Riveted onto leather patches, webbing or bag panels for a firm, secure hold
  • Metal plates with stitch holes can be sewn on for both decoration and reinforcement
  • Magnetic or adhesive backing suits finished goods or gift boxes that must not be punched

5. Ordering Process, MOQ and Compliance

  • MOQ depends on process: standard stamped metal labels usually start in the hundreds; zinc die-casting involves a mould, so larger runs are advisable
  • Sampling usually takes 3–7 days; a new mould or etching plate extends the lead time, so allow extra time
  • Compliance: skin-contact metal must meet nickel release limits (EU REACH), and childrenswear must respect heavy-metal limits such as lead and cadmium (e.g. US CPSIA)
  • Inspection focus: plating evenness and colour deviation, burrs and sharp edges, coating adhesion (salt-spray or rub tests), and logo clarity
💡 Tip: Metal tags weigh noticeably more than paper tags, so factor freight into export costing by air. For childrenswear and skin-contact garments, always deburr edges and confirm nickel release and heavy-metal compliance to avoid risk.
