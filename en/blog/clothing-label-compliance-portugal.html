Portugal Apparel Labelling: Portuguese Text, Packaging EPR and Green Claims

By TAIGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 5, 2026

Portugal is one of the few European textile markets that both produces and consumes: the Porto and Braga cluster in the north is among the densest in Europe, while retail in Lisbon and Porto sells large volumes of garments imported from China, Turkey and Morocco. That dual identity decides the single most important thing on the label — whose name goes in the responsible-party field.

This guide covers four blocks — who is responsible, Portuguese fibre names, packaging EPR and environmental claims — and closes with six pre-shipment checks.

1. First Work Out Who Is Responsible: Local Producer, Importer or Subcontractor

The same garment sold in Portugal carries a different responsible party depending on how it enters the market. Getting this wrong is harder to fix than a wrong fibre name, because the whole label run has to be redone.

Route to market Responsible party on the label
Made in Portugal Producer name and address, origin as Fabricado em Portugal
Garments imported from China Portuguese importer name and address, origin as Fabricado na China
Private-label production The brand is the responsible party; factory details go on separate documents per contract
Direct cross-border e-commerce An EU responsible person is required; GPSR expects a verifiable name and address

2. Portuguese Fibre Names and Legal Spelling

The Portuguese version of Regulation (EU) 1007/2011 has its own statutory names: accents are mandatory and English words are not a substitute. One missing accent is a defect on an inspection table in Lisbon.

  • The four you meet most: cotton algodão, polyester poliéster, viscose viscose, elastane elastano.
  • Natural fibres: wool lã, linen linho, silk seda, listed in descending weight order in blends.
  • Synthetics: polyamide poliamida, acrylic acrílico, polypropylene polipropileno.
  • Regenerated cellulose: modal, lyocell liocel — brand names never replace the legal name.

Two frequent traps: bamboo fibre can only be declared as viscose — bamboo is a raw-material source, not a legal fibre name — and algodão orgânico is a descriptor; the composition column still reads algodão with a percentage.

3. Care, Sizing and Origin: Consumer Information in Portuguese

  • Care: ISO 3758 symbols plus a short Portuguese line such as lavar à máquina a 30 °C, with symbols and words saying the same thing.
  • Sizing: retail generally uses EU sizes, though some local brands still label Portuguese sizes — confirm with the client which one goes on the label.
  • Origin: Fabricado na China must match the customs origin; changing the label means changing the documents too.
  • Responsible person: GPSR wants a name plus an address; naming a warehouse inside Portugal is not traceability.

4. Packaging EPR: Ponto Verde and the Unified EPR Regime

Portugal brings packaging, electronics and other waste streams under a unified extended producer responsibility regime (DL 152-D/2017), with packaging handled through authorised bodies such as Ponto Verde. Companies declare by material and weight and pay what the market calls the Green Dot tariff, so the more packaging you place, the more design choices matter.

  • The Green Dot is licensed, not free to print: use rights come from the authorised body, so never place the mark in artwork unless the client holds the licence.
  • Declaration basis: record by material (plastic, paper, composite) and per-unit weight; laminated bags must be split by layer.
  • Textile EPR: Portugal is moving towards extended producer responsibility for textiles; brands with volume should prepare material and weight data now.
  • Reducing beats switching material: one step down in thickness or dropping an inner card usually cuts both the fee and the freight better than a biodegradable swap.

5. Environmental Claims: What reciclado and reciclável May Say

With the EU Empowering Consumers Directive (EU 2024/825) being transposed, unsupported generic green wording counts as an unfair commercial practice. Portuguese shoppers are particularly sensitive to greenwashing, so one extra claim on a label can buy you a complaint.

  • Facts, not adjectives: write contém 30% fibra reciclada certificada instead of a blanket amigo do ambiente.
  • Certification must be checkable: name the body and the certificate number instead of dropping a green leaf icon.
  • Recyclable needs a scope: reciclável should state that it is recyclable in Portugal's collection system; be careful with composites.
  • No homemade icons: self-designed green icons are high risk in the EU; use the official marks supplied by the certifier.

6. Configuring the Four Trim Families

  • Hang tag: Portuguese retail often runs Portuguese plus English; brand on the front, composition and care on the back, QR code to a Portuguese page.
  • Neck label: satin weave matches what local premium brands are used to; choose high density so accents stay clean.
  • Care label: folded multi-page with Portuguese plus ISO 3758 symbols, tested beyond five washes when the client asks.
  • Packaging: paper or CPE bags with material code and recycling guidance; the Green Dot needs a licence and void space is tightening under PPWR.

7. Six Checks Before the Goods Leave

  1. Is the responsible party right: producer for local making, importer for imports, EU responsible person for e-commerce.
  2. Portuguese fibre names carry all accents, and bamboo is declared as viscose, not bambu.
  3. Care symbols match the process, and EU versus Portuguese sizing has been agreed with the client.
  4. Origin matches customs, and any label change comes with a document change.
  5. Packaging material and unit weight reconcile with the EPR filing, and the Green Dot is licensed.
  6. Every green claim is evidenced: a specific percentage, a certifier and a number, no generic adjectives.
