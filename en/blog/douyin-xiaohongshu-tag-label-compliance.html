Douyin and Xiaohongshu Apparel Tags and Labels: A 7-Point Compliance Checklist

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 21, 2026

On Douyin or Xiaohongshu an apparel order is won in a fifteen-second clip, but the shopper meets the hang tag and care label for the first time inside the courier bag. That moment decides whether the order comes back, and whether the platform treats the listing as misleading advertising. The product page, the live-stream script and the physical label all have to say the same thing: fibre content, size code, care method, country of origin and the product standard the garment was made to. This checklist pulls together the seven items to align before listing, the durability rules for the care label, and the failures that trigger penalties after shipment.

1. The Platform Checks One Thing: Page Matches Product

Douyin's product publication rules and Xiaohongshu's product note rules do not read your hang tag word by word, but they do require the listing parameters, material, composition, fit and standard, to match what ships. When a buyer complains that the material is not as described, the platform puts the listing screenshot next to the physical label. If they disagree, the case is handled as misleading advertising, not as an ordinary after-sales request. So the order of work matters: finalise the label content first, then fill the listing from the tag, never the reverse.

Ten minutes of cross-checking before listing: fibre content, size code, care method, country of origin, product standard. If any one of the five disagrees, fix the page first, do not ship and hope. Editing a page takes a minute; reprinting ten thousand tags takes ten days.

2. Seven Items That Must Appear on the Tag and Label

For textiles and apparel sold in mainland China, use instructions follow GB/T 5296.4-2012, fibre content follows GB/T 29862-2013, size designation follows the GB/T 1335 series, care symbols follow GB/T 8685-2008, and basic safety categories follow GB 18401-2010, which splits products into class A, B and C. A hang tag can carry brand story, price and a QR code, but these seven legal items cannot live only on the product page: a page can be edited, a label sewn into the garment cannot.

  • Product name and style code: identical on the page and in your internal system, so returns and reconciliation use one number.
  • Fibre content: declared under GB/T 29862-2013, with blends listed in order and by percentage. Cottony and silky are handle descriptions, not fibre names.
  • Size code: written as 160/84A, height plus body measurement plus figure type, per GB/T 1335. An M or L alone does not meet the requirement.
  • Care method: symbols per GB/T 8685-2008 in the fixed order of wash, bleach, dry, iron and professional care. A missing symbol is a missing declaration.
  • Safety class: class A for infants, B for direct skin contact and C for non-direct contact under GB 18401-2010. Marking a base layer as class C is a classic error.
  • Product standard number: for example FZ/T 73020 for knitted casual wear, or a company standard already filed on the platform.
  • Manufacturer name and address: the Chinese legal entity and its address. For contract manufacturing, name both the commissioner and the producer; a brand name alone is not enough.

Fibre content GB/T 29862-2013 Cotton with no percentage
Size code GB/T 1335 M or L only, no height or figure

3. The Care Label Is the Durable Mark: A Hang Tag Cannot Replace It

A hang tag hangs on a string and is cut off and thrown away. A care label is sewn into the garment and has to survive many washes. GB/T 5296.4-2012 sets clear durability requirements: the content must stay legible after normal washing, which is why fibre content, care method, origin and safety class belong on the sewn label, with the hang tag only supplementing and carrying brand expression. On materials, coated nylon is wash-resistant and stays sharp, polyester satin is soft and suits base layers and childrenswear, and cotton tape feels natural but prints less precisely. Keep type at 1.5 mm or above, and add 0.2 mm when reversed out of a dark ground.

4. Live-Stream Close-Ups and Multiple Markets: The Second Layer of Labelling

Holding a tag up to the camera in a live stream magnifies it for tens of thousands of viewers: an old tag version, a price over-stickered by hand or a handwritten note all get screenshotted. The rule is simple. The label that appears on camera must be the version that ships. For cross-border and export programmes a garment often has to satisfy two rule sets at once: in the EU, fibre composition is declared under Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 and GPSR (EU) 2023/988 requires an economic operator established in the Union plus traceability information; in the US, fibre content follows 16 CFR Part 303 and origin marking follows 19 CFR 134.

  • European Union: fibre names and percentages under 1007/2011, care symbols per EN ISO 3758, sizing optionally to EN 13402, with French and Spanish womenswear typically running taille 34 to 48.
  • Japan: the Household Goods Quality Labelling Act requires composition and handling in Japanese, care symbols per JIS L 0001, and sizes as 7, 9 or 11 go.
  • Korea: the Electrical Appliances and Consumer Products Safety Control Act puts composition, care instructions and country of origin on an attached label, with sizes as 55, 66 or 77 ho, not S/M/L alone.
  • United States: 16 CFR Part 303 covers fibre content and origin marking, states add chemical warnings such as California Proposition 65, and childrenswear also needs a CPSIA tracking label.

A hang tag costs a few cents. One misleading-advertising case plus a wave of returns costs thousands of orders worth of traffic. Writing these seven items, the durable label rule and the five checks into the specification keeps the risk inside the factory and the warehouse instead of amplifying it in a live stream. Before the next print run, make the label pass these five checks on its own; it is far cheaper than explaining afterwards.
