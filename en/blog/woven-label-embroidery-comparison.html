Woven Labels vs Embroidery: Feel, Cost & When to Use Each

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated August 21, 2026

Woven labels and embroidery are both common ways to brand garments, but they differ greatly in how they are made, how they feel and what they cost — choose wrong and the look misses your brand positioning, or the budget blows out. This article compares them across five dimensions so you pick the right process.

1. How Each Is Made

A woven label is created directly on a jacquard loom with yarns — the text and artwork are literally woven. An embroidery label is stitched onto fabric or tape by an embroidery machine, producing a raised, dimensional design.

  • Woven: thin, flat and smooth on both sides
  • Embroidery: layered threads, strong 3D feel and a crafted look; the back usually needs a lining
  • Woven excels at fine small type and tonal gradients; embroidery suits bold logos and large lettering

For skin-contact spots like necklines and side seams, woven labels win — thin and non-irritating. For display areas like jacket chests and cap fronts, the dimensional feel of embroidery carries the brand better.

3. Cost & MOQ Compared

Woven label cost is driven by loom time and color count; MOQ is typically 1,000+ pcs and unit price drops with volume. Embroidery is priced by stitch count and stitched area — flexible and workable in small batches, but cost climbs fast with dense, large designs.

  • Woven: best for large volumes and repeat orders, with an edge in fine detail
  • Embroidery: suits small batches and mid-to-high price points; strong with big logos and bold lines
  • Both get cheaper per unit at higher volumes — confirm with sample quotes

5. Using Them Together

Established brands often mix both: a woven main label at the neckline for comfort, embroidery on the chest or back to reinforce brand memory, and printed tape care labels — each plays to its strengths and total cost stays under control.

6. Sampling Advice
