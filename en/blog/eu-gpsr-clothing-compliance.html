EU GPSR for Clothing Brands: What You Must Label and Show

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 15, 2026

GPSR (the EU General Product Safety Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2023/988) has applied across the EU since 13 December 2024. It governs product safety itself rather than how a label is printed — yet for clothing brands most of the practical duties end up on care labels, hang tags and online product pages: where the EU responsible person is named, whether a batch can be traced to a specific production run, and what the listing must show. Get these three wrong and shipments or marketplace audits can stall.

This guide is written for brands and factories selling clothing into Europe: first the three core duties, then the pitfalls Chinese supply chains trip over most, and finally how to put it into practice on hang tags, care labels and packaging — plus a pre-launch checklist.

1. What GPSR Covers — and How It Differs From Textile Labelling Rules

Fibre composition rules in the EU come from Regulation (EU) 1007/2011, a labelling content rule; GPSR is a horizontal product safety regulation about whether the product is safe and whether it can be traced and recalled if something goes wrong. One does not replace the other — garments must satisfy both.

  • It applies to non-food consumer products sold to consumers in the EU, including direct online sales and goods imported from third countries
  • It applies to every product placed on the EU market, even when the brand itself sits outside the EU
  • Children's clothing cords and drawstrings are additionally covered by specific safety standards (e.g. EN 14682) within the same safety framework

2. The Three Core Duties: Responsible Person, Traceability, Online Disclosure

In practice the duties fall into three blocks — and all three feed back into trims and packaging layouts.

(1) The EU Responsible Person

Before a product is placed on the EU market there must be a responsible person established in the EU — usually the importer, an authorised representative or an EU fulfilment provider. Their name and address must appear on the product, its packaging or an accompanying document, or on the online listing where the physical layout genuinely cannot carry it. For brands shipping factory-direct from China this is the most frequent gap.

(2) Traceability: Batch, Model or Serial Number

The product or its packaging needs an element that identifies the specific item: a model, batch or serial number will all do. In apparel the batch or lot code is the most practical: it confines a problem to a traceable slice of production instead of a full-stock recall. This is where hang tags earn their keep — printed per style and per lot, they carry traceability by design.

(3) What the Online Listing Must Show

When selling through your own store or a marketplace, the listing must show the responsible person's name and address, the product identifier and any required warnings. This is frequently missed: sellers keep the data inside the parcel or in the back office rather than on the page a shopper sees before ordering.

3. The Five Pitfalls Clothing Brands Hit Most

  • A Chinese address only, no EU responsible person — this drives most customs requests and marketplace rejections
  • Composition and care symbols are printed, but no batch can be found — you cannot scope a recall when it matters
  • No responsible person on the listing — putting it only on the parcel or invoice misses the page requirement
  • Treating GPSR as a labelling rule — assuming a new care label version makes you compliant, while safety and notification duties are ignored
  • No notification or recall path prepared — discovering after an incident that nobody knows who reports to the safety gateway or how to reach buyers

💡 Tip: GPSR and textile labelling are two separate sets of rules. Updating composition and care symbols alone does not cover the responsible person, traceability or listing duties. Reserve layout space before you go to press — it is far cheaper than a reprint.

6. Three Things to Confirm With Your Trims Supplier

  • Can the tag layout reserve space for the responsible person and batch code while staying compatible with die-cutting or foil stamping?
  • Can the batch code be tied to the tag and care label print run and stay consistent across reorders?
  • Can compliance information be added to the poly bag or insert, and can the supplier issue material declarations for your records?
