A Carbon Number on a Hang Tag Is a Claim, Not a Report

By TAGE Packaging · Updated November 26, 2026

A 55 × 90 mm hang tag in 300 g/m² paper weighs about 1.485 g; switch to 350 g/m² and it becomes 1.732 g. Anyone can do that arithmetic. But when a brand asks to print the carbon footprint on the tag, what goes on the paper is no longer a weight, it is a public claim. Buyers, marketplace reviewers, customs and consumers all read it, and it has to survive three questions: how was it calculated, who verified it, and does it include transport. This guide covers where the number comes from, which evidence level may be printed, where the EU and national rules draw the line on environmental wording, and the data list a custom hang tag manufacturer can actually hand over.

1. Two Documents, Two Liabilities

The first document a brand receives each year is a carbon report: dozens of pages, with boundaries, assumptions and a third-party verification statement, filed between procurement and sustainability. The second is a hang tag: a few square centimetres that hold one sentence. Their legal nature differs completely. The report is an internal management document; if it is wrong, you correct it. The tag faces the market, and once printed it becomes an environmental claim, exposed to consumer law, marketplace delisting and buyer claims. The most common mistake is copying a figure from the report onto the tag without carrying across the boundary and assumptions that made that figure true.

2. Where the Number Comes From: ISO 14067 and the Functional Unit

The international reference for product carbon footprints is ISO 14067 (2018 edition), which sets the quantification principles, system boundaries and reporting requirements; China's corresponding national standard is GB/T 24067. It does not hand you a number. It first forces three answers, and those answers decide both the size of the figure and whether it can be compared with anyone else's.

  • Functional unit: per 1,000 tags, or per kilogram of paper? The first is easy for a brand to allocate per garment, the second is easy for a paper mill to price. The two are not interchangeable.
  • System boundary: cradle-to-gate stops at the factory gate; cradle-to-grave runs to disposal by the consumer. Hang tags usually use the first, but a brand claiming on the whole garment must use its own boundary.
  • Allocation: a tag carrying three co-branded logos still needs a rule, whether by printed area or by unit count. ISO 14067 requires the allocation basis to be stated; without it, a per-product figure cannot be claimed.

3. The Four Inputs That Actually Move the Number

A hang tag's footprint is not a mysterious constant. It is decided by four measurable inputs. The table uses the common 55 × 90 mm size: on the same artwork, moving paper from 250 to 400 g/m² takes a single tag from 1.237 g to 1.980 g, and that weight multiplied by the paper's emission factor is the bulk of the tag's footprint.

Input Typical range Effect on the figure What documents it
Paper grammage 250–400 g/m² 1.24–1.98 g per tag, linear ISO 536 grammage test report
Printing method Offset / digital / flexo Ink volume and drying energy, significant on small runs Ink MSDS plus press energy record
Tag cord material Polyester / cotton / paper / hemp A 15 cm polyester cord is roughly 0.3–0.6 g Spec sheet and material composition
Transport mode Sea / rail / air Air freight often pushes transport above production emissions for trims Bill of lading, mode and chargeable weight

4. Three Evidence Levels, Three Sentences You May Print

Buyers ask whether you can supply carbon data, but the honest answer is graded. Level one is measurement: bill of materials, grammage, meter readings and freight documents all in hand, so the figure can be recomputed. Level two is a database estimate, where the weights are yours but the emission factors come from public libraries. Level three is a sentence from a supplier with no boundary and no unit. Only the first two may go on a tag; the third belongs in an email.

  • Level one wording: 'This tag's footprint is calculated to ISO 14067, cradle-to-gate, functional unit 1,000 tags', followed by the figure and the calculation date.
  • Level two may be printed, but it must be labelled as an estimate from industry factors, and it cannot be set beside a level-one figure as if comparable.
  • Level three may not be printed. An 'around' or 'about' from a call or an email becomes, on paper, an unsupported environmental claim.

5. Where the EU and National Law Draw the Line on Environmental Wording

Two rule sets apply at EU level at once. One is the amended Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, (EU) 2024/825, which puts generic environmental claims, self-made eco labels and offset-based carbon neutrality on the prohibited list. The other is the packaging and product side: PPWR, Regulation (EU) 2025/40, and ESPR, which will turn recyclability, recycled content and durability into verifiable product requirements. A sentence such as 'this hang tag is carbon neutral' will, after 2026, most likely trip both advertising law and product law at the same time.

National law adds its own layer, so one sentence cannot travel everywhere. France reads it through the Code de la consommation and the AGEC law, with packaging statements also meeting Triman marking; Spain through Ley 7/2022 and Real Decreto 1055/2022, the latter bringing textile producer responsibility and packaging marking obligations into one frame; Japan through the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations, where an unprovable eco-friendly line is a misleading representation, and where a tag carrying wash instructions also falls under the Household Goods Quality Labelling Act; Korea through the Act on Fair Labelling and Advertising and the Act on Safety Management of Electrical Appliances and Consumer Products, both of which prescribe mandatory formats; China through the Advertising Law and GB/T 24067. One tag sold into five markets needs five versions of the sentence.

6. What You Print Matters More Than How Much You Print

The steadier practice is to print the invariant facts on the tag and keep the moving number behind a QR code or a data card. Facts include recycled paper content with the FSC licence code, grammage and size, cord material, design-for-recycling choices such as mono-material and no lamination, and weight reduction, for example 'this version is 18% lighter than the 2024 version, saving 0.25 kg of paper per 1,000 tags'. Those sentences can be checked against a bill of materials and do not depend on emission factors. The number itself, grams of CO2e per tag, belongs on a data page behind the QR, with the standard, boundary, functional unit and calculation date stated, which also gives the brand a basis for its Scope 3 conversion.

  • If the brand insists on printing a single figure, require the standard, boundary, functional unit and calculation date to be printed with it. Drop any one and the sentence no longer holds.
  • The page behind the QR must open publicly and match the tag; a QR that lands on a 404 leaves the claim unsupported.
  • Never ship two versions of the story in one carton: a data card with level-one figures and a tag with a level-three slogan invite direct comparison in a customs or marketplace check.
Three self-check questions for the buyer: what is the functional unit of this figure, does the boundary include transport, and who recalculates it when the paper changes? If all three have named owners, the number is ready for a tag.
