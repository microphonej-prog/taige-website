The Complete Hang Tag Die-Cutting Guide: Custom Shapes, Folding & Cutouts

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 1, 2026

Die-cutting is the most direct way to make your hang tags stand out on the shelf — with the same paper and printing, a distinctive silhouette completely changes how your brand is remembered. This guide details the common die-cutting types, die-board requirements, paper selection and cost factors, so you can decide whether and how to do it.

Die-cutting uses a steel-rule die to cut printed sheets into a preset outline in one pass. It goes far beyond rounded corners: die-cut hang tags can take garment silhouettes, circular badges, cut-out string holes or even multi-layer folding structures — a key process for differentiating your garment trims.

1. Common Die-Cutting Types for Hang Tags

  • Custom shape die-cutting — the whole tag is cut into a brand silhouette, such as a garment outline, circle or tag shape
  • Rounded corners — the most basic option, softens a square card at almost no extra cost
  • Cutouts & holes — openings inside the tag for string holes, window displays or decorative details
  • Kiss-cut & scoring — cutting halfway through or pressing a crease line for folding and tear-off designs

2. How to Choose Between the Four Techniques

TechniqueFeaturesBest For
Custom shapeFully custom outlineBrand display, limited editions
Rounded cornersSimple, durable, low costRegular bulk hang tags
Cutouts & holesAdds depth and functionString holes, window displays
Kiss-cut / scoringEasy folding, tear-offFolding tags, tear-open designs

3. Folding Hang Tags: Two Sides in One Card

  • Fold-over tags — folded over the garment with double the information space, ideal for two-sided brand stories
  • Tri-fold / multi-panel tags — like a mini booklet for product instructions and care guidance
  • Tent cards — stand up when folded, great for counters and trade-show display
  • Folding quality depends on the score line position, and paper weight determines whether the crease cracks

4. Die-Board & Artwork Requirements

  • Put the die line on a separate layer marked with a spot color — never mix it with the print content
  • Bleed at least 3mm and keep a 3–5mm safe margin; text near the die-cut edge needs extra clearance
  • For complex shapes, always sample the die first — sampling usually takes 3–7 days
  • Die tolerance is typically within ±0.5mm, which directly affects batch consistency

5. Paper, Finishing & Cost Factors

Art paper of 300gsm or above suits fine custom outlines; kraft and cotton papers give die-cut tags a more natural texture. Lamination protects cut edges from fraying, and foil stamping combined with a custom shape clearly lifts the premium feel.

Die-cutting cost consists of a one-time die-board fee, paper waste and cutting time. The more complex the outline and the smaller the tag, the higher the waste; folding adds a scoring step. Trial orders start at 2,000–3,000 tags, and the unit price drops noticeably once the die fee is amortized — always confirm with a quote.

💡 Tip: always sample die-cut hang tags first to confirm the outline, score lines and paper behavior before bulk production; a good manufacturer sends physical samples within 3–7 days.
