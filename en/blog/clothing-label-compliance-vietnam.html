Vietnam Clothing Label Requirements: What Decree 43/2017 Requires

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 13, 2026

Vietnam is the world's second-largest apparel exporter and, for many brands, both a sourcing base and a sales market. Any garment made, imported or circulating in Vietnam must carry a label that follows the local goods-labelling decree: Vietnamese language, responsible party and origin are all mandatory. This guide turns Decree 43/2017/ND-CP and its amendment 111/2021/ND-CP into artwork-ready specifications.

1. Legal basis and scope

Goods labelling in Vietnam is governed by Decree 43/2017/ND-CP, issued in 2017 and amended by Decree 111/2021/ND-CP, under the Ministry of Science and Technology (MOST). It covers goods produced, imported and circulating in Vietnam, apparel and textiles included. The core requirement is simple: the label must be true, clear, legible and consistent with the goods.

2. Vietnamese is mandatory, other languages may run alongside

Vietnamese is mandatory. English or other languages may be added, but the Vietnamese content must be complete, correct and consistent; where the two disagree, Vietnamese prevails. The two routine failures are an English-only label, and Vietnamese that translates nothing but the brand name.

3. The six items an apparel label must carry

Item Requirement Common error
Product name Generic name in Vietnamese, matching the customs entry Brand name only
Responsible party Importer or manufacturer name plus a Vietnamese address Overseas parent company only
Origin Marked per the applicable rules of origin Contradicts the declared origin
Fibre content Percentage per fibre, in descending order Writing cotton without a percentage
Size Local size or body measurement in cm European size with no explanation
Care ISO 3758 symbols or Vietnamese wording Missing symbols, or two contradictory sets on one garment

4. How to write size and measurements

Label both the local size name and a body measurement in centimetres — a 96 cm chest for adults, heights of 110, 120 and 130 cm for children. Give fabric weight in g/m² rather than thick, and all lengths in cm. Styles bound for the EU also need fibre content under 1007/2011, and a responsible person under GPSR (EU) 2023/988.

5. Care symbols and durability

Care can be expressed with ISO 3758 symbols or in Vietnamese words, but either way the marking must stay legible for the life of the garment. Use a sewn-in care label — woven or heat transfer — rather than a sticker. On dark and high-stretch fabrics, test legibility after five washes; heat-transfer labels often bloom white after high-temperature setting.

6. Who carries which duty

The responsible party in Vietnam is the importer or manufacturer: its name and address must appear on the label and it carries liability for accuracy. The supplier produces to artwork and supplies fibre-content test reports plus origin documents; the brand confirms the Vietnamese wording is unambiguous. Putting that split in the purchase order is cheaper than arguing later.

7. Pre-shipment checklist

  • Vietnamese present across the label, not English only
  • Importer name and Vietnamese address complete
  • Fibre percentages agree with the customs entry and test report
  • Origin marking matches what the rules of origin produce
  • Care symbols complete, with no second contradictory set on the same garment
The cost in Vietnam is not translation — it is a rejected shipment. Warehouse rent and delay at the border dwarf the price of redoing every label. Treat the Vietnamese artwork as its own design version rather than bolting text onto the English one.
