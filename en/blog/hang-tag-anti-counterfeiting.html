How to Secure Your Hang Tags: Holographic Foil, Variable QR Codes & Serial Numbers Explained

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 7, 2026

Counterfeiting eats into profits and channel diversion wrecks pricing — more and more clothing brands are upgrading the hang tag from a brand business card into an anti-counterfeiting gateway. The tag is right in the consumer's hand, which makes it the natural place for authenticity checks. This hang tag anti-counterfeiting guide covers holographic foil, security inks, variable QR codes and serial numbers — what each suits and how to combine them.

One conclusion first: security is not about spending more, it is about matching the combination to your brand positioning and channel risk — for most brands, one visible feature plus one variable code covers most needs.

1. Decide First: What Are You Protecting Against?

Fighting fakes, stopping channel diversion and running scan-based marketing are three different goals calling for different technology mixes. Picking features before defining the goal usually means spending more while missing the real problem.

  • Anti-counterfeiting — make fakes hard to copy: visible or covert features such as holographic foil, security inks and microtext
  • Channel control — territory management: give every tag a variable QR code or serial number, scan at dispatch to record flow, and trace issues back to batch and region
  • Verification & marketing — the consumer scans into a verification page, sees the genuine result, and lands on brand content such as membership sign-up or new arrivals

2. Common Hang Tag Security Features at a Glance

FeatureHow It WorksBest For
Holographic foil stampingThe image shifts with angle and light, instantly recognizable, costly to imitateLogos or security marks for premium brands that also want a quality feel
Security inksCovert inks visible under UV light, heat-sensitive inks and similar — hidden by designA second verification layer combined with a visible feature
MicrotextUltra-fine text or patterns readable only under magnification, hard for ordinary printing to reproduceHigh-security, document-style marks, usually combined with foil or serial numbers
Variable QR codes / serial numbersEvery tag carries a unique code printed digitally — one item, one codeTraceability, channel control and consumer verification — the most versatile option

3. One Item One Code: From Printing to Scanning

A typical one-code-per-item flow: the brand prepares a code data file (a unique code per tag, usually CSV or Excel) → the printer applies codes one by one with variable-data digital printing while printing the tags → codes are read and verified before shipment to catch duplicates or defects → the code list is returned to the brand system and activated → consumers scan and see the authenticity result and product record.

Details decide the scan experience: leave enough quiet zone and white background around the code; keep it away from punch holes, fold lines and string stress points; never overlap the code with reflective finishes such as foil, which can break scanning; prefer matte lamination and test it for real.

4. Choosing a Combination by Positioning and Budget

  • E-commerce and fast fashion — variable QR codes first: affordable, covering verification, channel control and membership in one
  • Mid-to-premium brands — holographic foil logo plus serial number: anti-fake value with a better unboxing feel
  • Categories at high risk of fakes — foil plus microtext plus one-item-one-code: make copying cost more than it is worth
  • Kids and babywear — safety first, security simple: no small detachable parts, inks and materials meeting the relevant safety standards

5. What to Check at Sampling and Inspection

  • Adhesion and rub resistance: foil and inks must survive shipping and shelf display without flaking or obvious wear
  • Scan success rate: sample with the same paper, inks and lamination as bulk, and the pass rate on sampled scans should be near 100%
  • Position and structure: keep the code clear of holes, fold lines and string holes so it never distorts under tension
  • Visual consistency: security features should blend with the overall tag design, not fight the brand identity
  • Defect-code terms: agree with the supplier on the acceptable defect rate, replacement quantities and outgoing inspection ratio
