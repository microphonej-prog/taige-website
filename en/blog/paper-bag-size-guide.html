Choosing Eco Paper Bag Sizes: Measuring, Size Chart & Custom Tips

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 9, 2026

Size is the easiest thing to get wrong when ordering eco paper bags: too small and folded garments will not fit; too large and the bag looks floppy, wasting paper and freight. Unlike poly bags, paper has no stretch — a wrong size usually means a redo. This article explains how bag sizes are expressed and measured, gives a reference size chart by garment type, and shows how size links to grammage, handles and print layout.

1. How Bag Sizes Are Expressed: Four Numbers, All Needed

A paper bag is a three-dimensional structure — width × height alone cannot build it correctly. Professional spec sheets use four dimensions: width (W) × height (H) × gusset (G) × bottom (D). W is the opening across, H is the drop from opening to bottom, G is the gusset depth between front and back (how thick an item fits), and D is the folded bottom (stability and load-bearing base).

  • Measure the product first: fold the garment as it will be displayed, take length, width and depth, then add 2–4 cm on each side for easy loading without looking baggy
  • Do not copy garment depth into the gusset: leave visual room — a gusset about 1.5–2× the product depth gives the crispiest silhouette
  • The bottom tracks the height: bottom depth is usually close to or slightly larger than the gusset — taller bags need wider bottoms or they tip over

The chart below gives common reference ranges — always confirm with a real pack test: two hoodies, one heavy fleece and one lightweight, can need very different bags.

3. How Size Drives Cost and Strength

A paper bag's cost is almost entirely its flat sheet area × grammage, plus handles, printing and forming. Adding 1 cm to width and height is barely noticeable, but growing the gusset and bottom inflates the flat area fast — that is why one size up costs visibly more. Grammage decides stiffness and strength: thick paper on a small bag is waste, thin paper on a large bag collapses. Size and grammage must be matched.

4. Custom Sizes & Print Layout Tips

  • Give finished dimensions, not intentions: state W×H×G×D clearly, or send a folded sample garment and let the factory measure and recommend
  • Keep print off fold lines: large colour blocks and patterns should avoid the gusset creases and bottom fold to prevent misalignment
  • Scale side print to the gusset: on slim bags, shrink side-panel text or it will fold into the crease
  • Sample before bulk: load a real garment, carry weight and walk — confirm size, strength and feel before scaling up
💡 Unsure how to specify? Send TAGE a folded sample garment or just give length, width and depth: we work back to W×H×G×D from how it packs, calculate sheet area and paper, deliver samples in 3–7 days and bulk in 10–25 days — size is only proven by trying.

5. Pre-Order Checklist

  • What fits inside, what are the folded dimensions, and is the allowance enough?
  • Are W×H×G×D all specified and consistent with the pack test?
  • Does the grammage match the size — no collapse or handle tear at the intended load?
  • Is the artwork supplied flat with bleed, safe margins and fold-line avoidance?
  • Are handle type, closure (open or folded top) and eco-certification requirements confirmed?
