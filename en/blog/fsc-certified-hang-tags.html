FSC Certified Hang Tags: Paper Source, Chain of Custody and Claim Wording

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated October 23, 2026

When a buyer writes that the hang tags must be FSC certified, the sentence can mean two different things: the paper is FSC-certified, or the printer holds FSC chain-of-custody (CoC) certification. Satisfy only one of them and the finished tag cannot legally carry the FSC label — the most common trap in export hang tag orders.

This guide covers four things: what material thresholds sit behind the FSC 100%, Mix and Recycled labels; why chain of custody has to sit with the printer; how to word an environmental claim on the tag so it survives a follow-up question; and what certified paper costs, how it affects lead time, and what to send before sampling.

1. Three FSC Labels, Three Different Material Thresholds

FSC is not one paper but three sets of material rules. Pick the wrong label and you either overspend or make a claim that does not hold. On export orders the safe route is to ask the buyer which label they want, then choose the paper backwards from that.

Label Material rule Typical hang tag use Watch out
FSC 100% All fibre from certified forests managed to FSC standards Premium brand lines with a clear environmental claim Fewer paper grades, limited choice of weight and hand
FSC Mix A mix of certified material, recycled material and controlled wood Standard hang tags, paper bags, booklets The most common label, but never claim the pulp is all certified virgin
FSC Recycled Entirely reclaimed material, mostly post-consumer, typically 85 percent or more Recycled-look tags, kraft-paper styles Colour leans grey-yellow and lot-to-lot variation is wider than virgin paper

2. Chain of Custody Sits with the Printer, Not the Mill

A mill's CoC covers only the raw material stage. A hang tag still passes through printing, foiling, die cutting and stringing, and each step has to keep certified stock segregated with volume balance. If the printer holds no CoC, the best certified paper still yields a product that cannot carry the FSC label — it ships as an ordinary tag.

  • Ask for three numbers: the mill's CoC certificate, the printer's CoC certificate, and the FSC trademark licence code starting with FSC-C. If they do not line up, some step sits outside the certified scope.
  • The label has rules: the FSC mark must carry the licence code, and proportions, clear space and minimum size are prescribed. Do not rescale, stretch or recolour it, and never place it where a dark background swallows it.
  • Subcontracting breaks the chain: if foiling, UV, stringing or hand assembly goes to an uncertified workshop, that batch falls outside the scope and the label no longer stands. Either use a factory certified through every step, or drop the subcontracted step or change the process.
  • Certificates expire: CoC is audited annually, and suspended or withdrawn certificates show up in the public FSC database. Check before you take the order instead of relying on last year's PDF.

3. Wording an Environmental Claim That Survives a Follow-Up

EU green-claim scrutiny is getting finer grained, and compliance teams at marketplaces and large retailers read tag copy word by word. "Eco-friendly tag" no longer carries weight; a claim has to trace back to a specific label and certificate.

  • A logo with no material statement is not a claim. The proper form is label plus material plus licence code, for example FSC Mix certified paper containing certified and recycled fibre, licence FSC-Cxxxxxx.
  • FSC, recyclable and compostable are three different dimensions. Do not fold them into one sentence. Paper can be both FSC and recyclable, but FSC says nothing about compostability, and mixing them reads as misleading.
  • Fewer adjectives, more verifiable facts. eco-friendly, 100 percent green, planet-friendly have no definition and collapse under a follow-up question. Source, content and certificate number persuade far more.
  • Do not turn a part-certification into a whole-pack claim. If the tag is FSC and the bag is not, you cannot write that the whole pack is FSC certified. Naming the material of each component reads as more professional.

4. Cost and Lead Time: the Paper Costs More, Not a Certification Fee

The premium on FSC paper comes from the fibre source and lot consistency; depending on grade and weight it typically runs about ten percent above the equivalent. Common weights in white card and kraft are usually in stock and barely move the lead time; special grades are made to order and need scheduling.

  • 250–350 gsm white card and standard kraft: mostly stock items; certified and ordinary paper ship on similar timing, and the difference lands in price.
  • Cotton paper, tinted stock, textured grades: made to order; approve the paper sample 7–10 days earlier than usual, otherwise the certified stock arrives with no lead time left.
  • Paper MOQ often exceeds tag MOQ. Small certified runs leave surplus paper; agree on the first order whether it can be held in stock and whether reorders draw from the same lot.
  • There is no FSC certification fee line. The cost sits inside the paper price. If a supplier quotes a separate surcharge, ask which certificate and which process step it maps to, to avoid paying twice.
💡 Tip: before accepting an FSC hang tag order, do three things — check the printer's CoC validity, match the licence code on the artwork against the certificate, and write grade, weight and label type into the order. Three lines prevent a full rerun.
