In Spain, a Garment Bag Owes Two Declarations Before It Ships

By TAGE Packaging · Updated November 27, 2026

A poly bag shipped to Madrid is not just packaging; it is a declared weight. Since 2023, non-reusable plastic packaging placed on the Spanish market carries a special tax of EUR 0.45 per kilogram of non-recycled plastic (Ley 7/2022), and Real Decreto 1055/2022 makes producers responsible for registering and reporting the packaging they place on the market. Neither duty belongs to the converter in Dongguan. Both are impossible without data only the converter holds.

That is the practical shift: the bag's specification sheet is now a compliance document. Bag material, gauge in µm, recycled content with its certificate, label substrate, header card and the weight of every component decide what your customer declares and what it pays.

1. Two Instruments, One Bag

Ley 7/2022 has applied since 1 January 2023. It taxes non-reusable plastic packaging by weight of non-recycled plastic at EUR 0.45 per kilogram, is self-assessed, and for imports is settled at customs clearance. The taxable base is not how heavy the bag is, but how much virgin polymer it contains.

Real Decreto 1055/2022, of 27 December, implements the EU packaging and packaging waste rules: producers answer for the packaging they place on the market, must register in the producer register and report annually by material and weight, financing the system either through a collective scheme such as Ecoembes or an individual one. Household and commercial packaging differ in the fee, not in the duty to declare.

2. The Arithmetic Behind EUR 0.45

The taxable base is kilograms of non-recycled plastic. A 30 × 40 cm LDPE bag at 25 µm weighs about 5 g; at 100 % virgin content the tax is roughly EUR 0.00225 per bag, or EUR 2,250 for a million bags a year. That is not a reason to change supplier, but it is a reason to stop guessing. Recycled content cuts the base directly, which is where documentation decides: EN 15343 is the standard for traceability and assessment of recycled content in plastics, and a certificate referencing it turns a percentage on a datasheet into a number missing from a tax return.

One trap: recyclable and recycled are two different columns. A mono-material PE bag is easier to recycle and cheaper in EPR terms in some schemes, but unless it actually contains recycled polymer it does not reduce the plastic tax base by a single gram.

3. What the Supplier Has to Hand Over

  • Material and gauge per component: bag film in µm, label face and liner in g/m²
  • Weight per 1,000 units, split by component — the declaration wants kilograms, not rolls per carton
  • Recycled content percentage with the certificate number (EN 15343 or equivalent), stating post-consumer or post-industrial
  • Whether the bag belongs to a real reuse system — the tax excludes reusable packaging, but only if the reuse is real
  • Additives: oxo-degradable or biodegradable claims need test reports, and oxo-degradable plastics are restricted in the EU
  • Adhesive and ink type, for the sorting and recycling declaration — water-based and solvent-based are not interchangeable

4. Cutting the Bill Without Cutting Protection

The cheapest poly bag is the one you do not need. Right-size to the folded garment first: 5 cm of extra width on a 30 cm bag is about 17 % more film for the same garment. Then down-gauge within the strength spec — but decide that after a drop test, not before. Then replace a laminated header card with uncoated paper, or move the branding to a sticker on the bag. Whichever route you take, one rule holds: every gram removed has to pass the transit test the customer already approved.

On paper alternatives: the Spanish market accepts paper bags for retail and premium e-commerce, and the plastic tax does not apply to them — but the EPR declaration still does, and a paper bag with a PE window or a plastic handle is still plastic in that declaration.

5. Marking and the Words on the Bag

RD 1055/2022 sets marking obligations for packaging, including material identification; in practice that means a resin code on the film (the EU identification system, Decision 129/97/EC) and sorting guidance for household packaging. Marked correctly, the bag passes a brand audit; marked wrongly, the first complaint is about the label, not the garment.

Claims carry their own risk. Wording such as eco bag or biodegradable, with no standard and no test report behind it, is exactly what Directive (EU) 2024/825 targets from 27 September 2026. Write what is measured: 80 % post-consumer recycled LDPE, EN 15343 certificate number — that is a sentence a customer can repeat in front of their own auditor.

6. Three Dates That Avoid a Fine

The work is annual and it clusters. Registration is one-off and must be done before the first placement on the market; the annual declaration follows the scheme's calendar, usually the first quarter for the previous year; the plastic tax is filed monthly or quarterly through self-assessment, or settled at import. Put those three dates next to your Chinese New Year production plan and the panic disappears.

Three questions for your supplier: how many grams per 1,000 bags, by component; which certificate backs the recycled percentage; and does the marking on the film match the material you are declaring?
