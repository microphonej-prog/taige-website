Southeast Asia Apparel Label Requirements: How Vietnam, Indonesia, Thailand, Malaysia and the Philippines Label Garments

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated October 22, 2026

Southeast Asian apparel labelling requirements come up more often every season: brands move orders from China to Vietnam, Indonesia or Cambodia, yet the trims are still specified to European rules. Only at customs or at the retail DC does it emerge that the label must be in the local language, with fibre content, origin and importer all present. Re-printing care labels misses the window, so the fix travels by air at several times the cost.

This guide covers three things: what each of the five markets requires, why the language question is really a layout question, and the six points to confirm in writing before you place the order.

1. Why Label Problems Multiplied in Southeast Asia

  • Orders moved, standards did not: a brand designs in Europe, produces in Southeast Asia and sells into a third country, so one garment faces both the producing country's labelling rules and the selling country's — and the two often differ
  • Retail checklists are stricter than customs: language, importer name and address, care symbols, size system — miss one and the delivery can be refused, while customs often only looks at the declared description and origin
  • Local scripts stress the process: Vietnamese tone marks and Thai characters with ascenders and descenders blur into a smudge at small print sizes on a screen-printed care label, and a woven label below a certain density cannot weave them at all

2. Five Markets at a Glance

Market Usual label language Information usually required
Vietnam Vietnamese first, other languages may follow Product name, fibre content, size, care instructions, country of origin, producer or importer name and address
Indonesia Bahasa Indonesia Fibre content, care method, country of origin, importer; some textiles carry additional local certification
Thailand Thai Fibre content, care method, origin, importer
Malaysia Malay or English Composition, care instructions, origin, importer
Philippines English or Filipino Composition, origin, importer

The table reflects common practice, not clause-by-clause law. For a specific shipment, get written confirmation from the local importer or the brand's legal team: rules get amended, and enforcement differs between customs and retail channels. Keep that confirmation in an email rather than in your head.

3. Language Is a Layout Problem, Not a Translation Problem

A care label is often only 30–40 mm wide, and it has to carry five languages and eight lines of information. That is a layout problem. Three things decide the outcome: the priority of information, the minimum type height, and the leading.

  • Rank first: composition, care, origin and importer must stay on the care label; brand stories, marketing lines and QR codes belong on the hang tag, not squeezed into the care label
  • Respect a minimum type height: for screen-printed care labels keep characters at 1.2 mm or more; on woven labels clarity comes from density, and lowering density to fit more text leaves the whole label muddy — it can even trigger false readings at needle detection
  • Set leading by the tallest script: Thai characters extend above and below, Vietnamese tone marks sit over vowels; leading set for Chinese or English will collide, so allow space per script

4. Composition, Care and Origin: The Three Fields That Go Wrong

  • Use the local fibre name: elastane appears as spandex or elastane depending on the market, and Southeast Asia generally follows the British form; recycled polyester must state the recycled origin and percentage — the bare letters rPET mean nothing to a shopper or an inspector
  • Pair symbols with words: ISO 3758 symbols are only partly recognised by Southeast Asian shoppers, so symbols plus a short local-language line is the safe option; do not invent your own order — wash, bleach, tumble dry, iron, professional care is fixed
  • Origin must match where it is actually made: sewn in Vietnam but with fabric and most operations in China does not automatically make it Vietnamese origin; copying Made in Vietnam from the previous version is a classic compliance failure, and fixing it means reprinting every trim

5. Six Points to Confirm in Writing Before Ordering

  1. Where it sells: local retail, a cross-border platform or re-export to a third country — three different label requirements
  2. Which entity appears on the label: the brand, the local importer or an agent — names and addresses must match character for character
  3. Language set and minimum type size: give the language list and the smallest acceptable type, do not let the factory guess
  4. Size system: local, European or US, and the same system must appear on the hang tag and the size label
  5. Care symbol version and wording: which version of the symbols, and which short local phrases accompany them
  6. Who signs off: brand legal, importer or a third-party auditor — name the signatory on the sample approval sheet so nothing changes after bulk
💡 Tip: keep label copy in one master sheet with a single column of country differences. Change only that column per revision, and four markets share one layout and one set of process parameters — half the sampling cost, and no risk of a Vietnamese version inheriting Thai type sizes.
