Second-Hand and Resale Apparel Labels Guide: Care, Size and Grade Tags

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated October 13, 2026

The first barrier in second-hand and resale apparel is rarely the sourcing — it is the labelling. By the time a garment re-enters circulation, the care label may be washed out, the size label gone and the hang tag long lost, yet every channel — resale platforms, rental companies, off-price warehouses, multi-brand stores — inspects and lists by exactly that information. Labels decide whether a used garment returns to sale at a decent price or ends up as dead stock.

This guide has five parts: the three labelling pain points that stop resale and rental most often, the six trim items that need to be restored, how to choose labels that survive repeated washing and dry cleaning, where compliance and platform requirements draw the line, and a six-step refurbishment and grading flow.

1. The Three Labelling Pain Points in Resale and Rental

  • Faded or missing care labels: repeated washing and dry cleaning wear the print off coated labels. With no visible composition or care instruction, customer service explains piece by piece and returns rise.
  • Size labels that no longer match the garment: the original label has fallen off, been altered, or the garment shrank in a wash, so the marked size cannot be trusted. Size disputes are the last thing resale needs: measurements are more reliable, yet the label still needs one coherent size system.
  • No traceable identity on the garment: channels need item number, size, condition grade and batch, and rental operators also need durability and care history. Without trims that carry this information, it all falls back on manual logging — which collapses at scale.

2. Six Trim Items to Restore on Resold Garments

Unlike relabelling, this is not a swap but a rebuild. The table below lines up the six items, their role in the resale loop and the point that matters most when they are remade.

Care labelStates the composition and care that still apply — the basis for trusting a used itemMake sure the new label matches the actual piece, especially after shrinkage, alteration or a lining swap

3. Choosing Labels That Survive Repeated Washing

Labels for resale and rental should be chosen by number of wash cycles, not by new-garment standards. In rental, one garment may pass through a dozen customers in a year, and label durability sets the rework frequency.

  • Woven care labels: the text is woven into the tape, which colours through the fibre itself — hard-wearing and rub-resistant, best for high-frequency rental and activewear; the trade-off is coarser detail and fewer colours.
  • Coated printed care labels: sharp print and high information capacity, ideal for composition tables and multilingual text; choose a rub-resistant coating and run a real wash-ageing test before bulk, to see how long the print holds.
  • Dry-clean and heat resistance: rental gowns, suits and coats mostly go to dry cleaning, so the label material must resist solvents; where pressing is routine, the label also has to take ironing temperature, or it curls and stiffens at the edge.
  • Attachment decides lifespan: backstitching both ends with a moderate stitch length outlasts glue or heat pressing. For resale, favour an attachment that can be unpicked again for the next maintenance round.
  • Colour and bleeding risk: a pale label on dark fabric can itself be dyed, and a dark label can bleed onto pale fabric. Testing fabric and label together in one wash costs less than explaining afterwards.

4. Compliance and Platform Rules: What Is Fixed, What You Decide

Second-hand and refurbished goods do not enjoy a blanket exemption in most markets. Basic elements — fibre content, care instructions, origin and manufacturer or importer details — still have to be available when the item is resold. The exact scope differs by country, so check the target market's official guidance instead of copying the old new-goods label.

  • Authenticity comes first: brand marks, security features and origin statements must not be blurred to make a sale. Resale channels tolerate counterfeit mixing very poorly — one incident can cost platform access.
  • Disclose refurbishment honestly: a replaced lining, replaced rhinestones or an altered size should be stated on the listing or label, never presented as new or unused.
  • The digital product passport direction: the EU is moving ahead with digital textile information and circular-economy requirements, where composition, origin and care data for a garment may have to be retrievable. Recording item number, batch and material data in structured form now is far easier than backfilling later.
  • Platform rules are often stricter than the law: condition-grade wording, how sizes are entered, whether old labels may show in photos, whether care information needs a photo — each platform differs. Checking the current rules item by item before listing saves a lot of takedown disputes.

5. A Six-Step Refurbishment and Grading Flow

  1. Intake inspection: log item number, size, source channel and initial grade, and check whether the care label is present and legible. This decides which trims have to be restored.
  2. Cleaning and settling: split garments onto the right route (wash, dry clean, local treatment), then let them rest and rebalance moisture before shaping, so no water marks are set in.
  3. Repair and relabel: restitch loose seams, replace zip sliders and missing fasteners, and restore care label, size label and internal ID in one pass to avoid a second round.
  4. Grade to a written standard: judge each piece against written grade definitions (appearance, wear, colour, missing parts) and shoot a reference photo set per grade, so two graders do not drift a level apart.
  5. Tag, photograph, list: attach the hang tag and grade tag, then put measured dimensions, care method and flaw positions into the listing — the more complete the information, the fewer the returns.
  6. Pack and ship: match packaging to category (dust bag, resealable bag, rigid box) and include care and return notes, so transit creasing does not become a dispute.
💡 Tip: design resale trims for reuse. Each time you relabel, bring the internal-ID position, attachment method and care records under one rule set — otherwise every piece needs individual handling by the third maintenance round.
