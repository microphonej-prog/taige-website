Care Label Size Guide: Standard Dimensions, Font Height & Layout

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 3, 2026

Care label size is one of the most underestimated details in garment trims: too small, and fiber content and care symbols will not fit or will disappear under the seam; too large, and the label feels scratchy on next-to-skin garments and distorts when sewn. Worse, problems with export labels often surface only at inspection or retail stage. This care label size guide covers specifications, font height and layout planning so you get the size right the first time.

1. Choose the Type and Attachment First, Then the Size

Care labels come in three main forms, each with its own sizing logic: woven tape care labels are limited in width by the loom, typically 15 mm, 20 mm or 25 mm (also 10 mm and 30 mm), cut to 50–90 mm in length depending on content, and suit straight seams such as side seams; coated/printed care labels and heat-transfer care labels have no loom width limit — they can be rectangles such as 35×60 mm or 40×70 mm, or very narrow strips for back-neck and waistband positions. The safest sequence is to confirm the attachment method and seam length with the garment factory first, then work back to the label size.

2. Font Height Standards: Compliant and Wash-Resistant

Every character on a care label survives dozens of washes, drying cycles and rubbing. Practical benchmarks: Latin letters (English, French, etc.) should be at least 1.5–2 mm high, Chinese characters at least 2–2.5 mm, and care symbols must follow ISO 3758 (GINETEX) proportions — never compress them to save space. On compliance: the US FTC requires care labels to be permanent and legible (16 CFR 423), and EU textile rules require fiber composition labels to be durable, legible, visible and accessible. Font height is not a design preference — it is the baseline for compliance and user experience.

💡 Tip: folding discounts the visible font height — a folded label doubles in thickness and text can distort on thick fabrics. If you need an effective height of 1.5–2 mm after folding, increase the design font size accordingly.

3. Content Determines Line Count, Lines Determine Size

Content ItemTypical LinesNotes
Brand name/logo1 lineCan sit on the front or merge with the main label
Fiber content1–2 linesUse two lines when there are more than 4 fibers
Care symbol group1–2 linesSymbols must follow ISO 3758 proportions
Origin/importer/RN number1–3 linesRN number can replace factory name/address in the US
Short care instructions1–3 linesE.g. do not bleach, line dry
Each additional language+2–3 linesReserve space for multilingual exports (e.g. EU)

The estimation is simple: list the content line by line and count the maximum number of lines. If content fits within 3 lines, a single layer or center fold is enough; beyond 3 lines, you usually need a longer tape or a multi-page booklet fold — otherwise you must shrink the font, which, as we just said, is not negotiable.

4. Folding Styles and Length Planning

  • Center fold: finished length is about half the cut length; the most common sewn form, ideal for labels with 3–6 lines
  • End fold: both ends fold inward before sewing, hiding raw edges — suits premium garments that need a refined look
  • Booklet fold: Z- or C-shaped multi-page folding; each extra fold roughly doubles the length — for multilingual export labels with long content
  • Length planning rule: cut length = visible finished length × number of layers + seam allowance; longer tape costs more and is harder to sew, so keep it just long enough

5. Size Recommendations by Garment Category

CategoryRecommended FormKey Points
T-shirts/shirtsCenter-fold tape, 20×50–60 mmSide seam; soft and non-irritating
Denim/workwearLonger tape, 25×70–90 mmMore content, frequent washing, needs durability
Children's wearCenter fold, 15×40–50 mmShort and soft to avoid rubbing skin
Outerwear/downBooklet or extended tapeMultilingual/long care text; ample lining seam space
Underwear/activewearHeat transfer or coated labelUltra-thin and tagless; verify font height strictly

6. Common Mistakes and Sampling

  • Mistake 1: using one care label size for every category. Outerwear and children's wear have very different size and softness needs — set specifications per category
  • Mistake 2: printing text right up to the seam. The seam allowance eats 3–5 mm — always keep a safe margin on all sides
  • Mistake 3: finalizing the artwork before deciding placement. Have the factory confirm seam length and fold type first, then finalize the layout

Sampling is the safety net for sizing issues: ask your supplier for physical samples within 3–7 days, attach them on the actual fabric at the real seam position, then launder 5 times per standard to confirm legibility, no shrinkage or distortion, and no fraying. Bulk tolerance is usually ±2 mm — put the approved-sample dimensions plus tolerance into the contract and production will stay on track.
