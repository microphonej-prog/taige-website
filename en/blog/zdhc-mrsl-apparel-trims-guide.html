ZDHC MRSL and Apparel Trims: From the List to Practical Actions

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated October 11, 2026

The ZDHC MRSL is the list brands name most often in environmental questionnaires, yet it governs the chemicals used in production rather than the finished product. For a trims factory this matters: hang tag printing, care label coating, woven label dyeing, bag lamination and metal plating all consume chemicals, so the customer's question about using ZDHC MRSL conformant formulations sits on top of a whole chemical management system. Separating ZDHC, the MRSL and a brand's own RSL is far more useful than rushing to obtain a certificate.

1. Three Things to Separate First: ZDHC, MRSL and RSL

  • ZDHC: the Zero Discharge of Hazardous Chemicals programme, launched in 2011 by a group of apparel and footwear brands and now an independent foundation whose members span brands, suppliers and chemical companies; it publishes the harmonised lists and tools
  • MRSL: the Manufacturing Restricted Substances List, which covers the input side — the dye, auxiliary, ink, adhesive, coating and cleaning formulations entering the factory — and restricts intentional use
  • RSL: the Restricted Substances List covering the output side — residues above the permitted level in the final product, including trims such as tags, woven labels and bags; usually issued by each brand and judged from finished-product test reports

2. Which Trims Processes Touch Restricted Chemicals

  • Hang tag printing and foil stamping: inks, fountain solution, wash-up solvents and foil adhesives, historically a hotspot for APEO, phthalates and aromatic solvents
  • Woven label dyeing and finishing: dyes, fixing agents, softeners and flame retardants, with azo dyes and formaldehyde the classic restrictions
  • Care label coating and sizing: coating resins, sizing agents and optical brighteners; wash-resistant qualities usually mean heavier coating and more chemistry
  • Bag lamination and bonding: solvent-based laminating adhesives and residual solvents in inks are the most frequently failed items in flexible packaging
  • Metal parts and plating: plating baths involve nickel release and hexavalent chromium, among the parts most often stopped by an RSL
  • Water, stain and grease repellent coatings: fluorinated finishes fall under both PFAS regulation and the MRSL, and are a category to substitute early
  • PVC and rubber parts: plasticisers in tag pins, grip strips and elastic cord are the main source of phthalate failures

4. Higg FEM and Cascale: The Box in the Brand's ESG Questionnaire

  • Who runs it: the Higg Index is stewarded and governed by Cascale, formerly the Sustainable Apparel Coalition, as a shared self-assessment and verification toolkit
  • Higg FEM: the Facility Environmental Module covering energy, water, wastewater, emissions, waste and chemical management — the one trims plants are asked for most
  • Higg FSLM: the Facility Social and Labor Module, aligned with the SLCP framework and, with FEM, forming the brand's annual health check of a supplier
  • Understand self-assessment plus verification: Higg FEM is an annual self-assessment whose weight depends on third-party verification; brands and retailers use a separate brand and retail module to aggregate supply chain data
  • Link to regulation: Higg FEM has been recognised by EFRAG as a valuable sustainability reporting tool for SMEs, in step with EU reporting direction — filling it in also helps answer compliance questionnaires

5. The Four Documents Customers Ask For First

  1. Chemical inventory and SDS sheets: list inks, adhesives, coatings, cleaning agents and dyes by process with brand, grade and approximate volume; this list, not a generic green statement, is where the review starts
  2. Proof of MRSL conformance: a Gateway product page screenshot or ID shows the conformance level and validity directly, and expiry status is visible on the platform
  3. Wastewater test reports: plants with wet processes test against the ZDHC Wastewater Guidelines, covering conventional and MRSL parameters with sampling points and dates
  4. Higg FEM self-assessment and verification: the annual self-assessment, verifier's report or platform record answering how your facility manages its environmental performance

6. Six Things You Can Put in Place Within a Year

  1. One central register: put every chemical in the plant into a single ledger by process, with grade, supplier, SDS version and stock, and fill the missing SDS sheets in one pass
  2. Lock the recipes: put supplier and grade for inks, adhesives and coatings into the work instructions, and re-approve any substitution — most failures come from the shop floor switching to a cheaper adhesive
  3. Ask for conformance proofs: request the level and validity from chemical suppliers, prefer higher levels, and write it into the purchase contract rather than chasing it later
  4. Control the wet processes: if dyeing, washing or plating are outsourced, make wastewater reporting and the chemical list entry conditions; if in-house, test annually
  5. Name one owner: appoint a chemical management owner for ledger updates, SDS training and expiry reminders — one person watching beats five people firefighting
  6. Build one dossier: combine the ledger, conformance proofs, test reports and verification records into a single chemical management file you can send on request, instead of rebuilding it every time
💡 Tip: what customers really want to confirm is not whether you hold a certificate but whether the formulations behind this shipment are compliant and traceable. Tying conformance level to batch number is far more convincing than a nearly expired certificate — and since platform requirements keep moving, always confirm against the latest ZDHC and Cascale texts.
