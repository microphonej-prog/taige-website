Green Claims on Hang Tags: What You Can Legally Print

By TAGE Packaging · Updated November 28, 2026

A hang tag is the smallest piece of print a brand owns, and the one most likely to be photographed, quoted on resale platforms and read by a customs officer. That makes it the most scrutinised 30 cm² of the garment: any environmental statement printed on it — recycled, compostable, carbon neutral, plastic free — is consumer-facing advertising, judged under advertising and consumer law rather than under fibre labelling law. As a custom hang tag manufacturer we are asked almost every week to print wording that no certificate behind it can support.

This piece skips the slogans and separates the two rule sets brands actually cite — the US FTC Green Guides and the EU green claims rules adopted in 2024 — then gives an evidence checklist you can run with any supplier and the local differences in France, Spain, Japan, Korea and China.

1. Fix liability first: a tag is an advertising medium

When a claim is challenged, the authority asks the brand for the evidence file and the supplier for the certificate. The brand is the advertiser and the printer is the producer of the medium: different exposures, both questioned. There is also a scope question that gets missed: if the front says 100% recycled, the same wording on the back, the string, the sticker or the outer bag is read as part of the same claim. Fixing only the front fixes nothing.

2. United States: the FTC Green Guides (16 CFR Part 260)

The Guides are not a penalty-carrying statute, but the FTC enforces them under Section 5 of the FTC Act against unfair or deceptive practices, and most states mirror them in consumer protection law. The sections that decide tag wording: §260.4 treats broad terms such as eco-friendly, green or sustainable as claims of every environmental benefit, which makes them almost impossible to substantiate; §260.7 requires the item to be recyclable where it is sold, and the 2012 revision deliberately kept no fixed percentage safe harbour — the test is whether a substantial majority of consumers can actually put it in a bin; §260.8 separates pre-consumer from post-consumer recycled content; §260.9 requires proof that the item composts in the environment where it is sold; §260.10 treats an unqualified degradable claim as deceptive.

Wording on the tag What the rule requires Real risk on a hang tag
eco-friendly, green, sustainable on their own §260.4: read as claiming every benefit, so each one must be proven almost impossible to substantiate: the risk is printed on the card
recyclable §260.7: recyclable where sold, with no fixed percentage foil-blocked tags with a plastic barb usually fail it
recycled content §260.8: pre- and post-consumer must be separated and proven up the chain a paper mill invoice is not proof of content; FSC or a transaction certificate is
compostable §260.9: composts in the place it is sold; usually EN 13432 or ASTM D6400 home and industrial composting are not interchangeable
degradable §260.10: an unqualified degradable claim is deceptive in itself delete it from the copy; there is no safe version

3. EU: generic claims are already banned

Many brands assume they can wait for the Green Claims Directive. They cannot. Directive (EU) 2024/825 amended the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC and applies from 27 September 2026. Annex I gains these banned practices: generic environmental claims without recognised excellent environmental performance behind them; claims that a product has a neutral, reduced or positive climate impact based on offsetting; and sustainability labels that are neither certified by a third party nor set by a public authority. From autumn 2026 the eco-friendly card stock route is closed.

Two concrete consequences for tags. First, a self-designed green leaf icon counts as a banned sustainability label from 2026, even if the board really is FSC certified: the problem is the label, not the material. Second, the words carbon neutral now require proof of reduction rather than offsetting; the workable substitute is a per-unit emissions figure, not a conclusion.

4. How five markets land it in practice

Market Main instrument What it means on the tag
France Code de la consommation (deceptive practices) plus loi AGEC n° 2020-105 Triman and Info-tri are packaging duties; a tag carries them only if it counts as packaging. Sizes in mm, paper weight in g/m²
Spain Ley 3/1991 on unfair competition plus Ley 7/2022 on waste and contaminated soil etiquetado must state the material, and generic terms are advertising too. Garment sizes in cm, paper weight in g/m²
Japan Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations, plus the Household Goods Quality Labelling Act Japan’s quality labelling rules require composition and blend ratio, and eco wording needs substantiation on file; sizes in mm, basis weight in g/m²
Korea Act on Fair Labelling and Advertising, plus the KFTC review guidelines on environmental labelling and advertising The labelling duty covers country of origin and blend ratio; sizes as Korean hose numbers or cm, specs in mm
China Advertising Law (false advertising) plus GB/T 24021 on environmental labels and declarations origin and material must be stated, and the eco wording on export documents must match domestic claims; specs in mm and g/m²

5. An approval sequence that survives an audit

  • ① Write the claim as a sentence with a verb: what the item is, what it achieves, and which document proves it. Recycled paper is not a sentence; the board contains 100% post-consumer recycled fibre, verified under FSC Recycled 100% is.
  • ② Match the evidence level: self-declaration under ISO 14021, then supplier declaration, then third-party certification such as FSC, GRS or an EN 13432 test. For a high-visibility, low-value item like a tag, going straight to third-party level is the cheapest route.
  • ③ Bind the document to the batch: the certificate must match the production batch and date; an expired certificate is no certificate. Put the certificate number on the reverse of the tag or on the carton so a buyer can spot-check it.
  • ④ Re-check quarterly: a change of mill, board weight or ink can invalidate the original claim, and the copy will not update itself.

6. Swap-in wording for the same sentence

What brands want to print What can be printed instead
100% eco-friendly hang tag FSC Recycled 100% board, cotton string, no plastic barb; licence code on request
carbon neutral hang tag CO2e per 1,000 tags available on request, with no offsetting claim
biodegradable hang tag certified industrially compostable to EN 13432; certificate number on request, not suitable for home composting
plastic free packaging paper card and cotton string, no plastic anywhere (only if true, and check the sticker and tape too)
If a buyer insists on eco-friendly because it is their brand voice, offer the ladder above and explain that from September 2026 it is a banned generic claim in the EU and, in the US, a claim of every benefit at once. Most legal teams change their mind once they see the date.

On cost and MOQ: changing a sentence or adding a certification mark is a print change, not a material change, so board weight and structure usually stay put and the cost difference sits in plate-making and proofing. Our hang tags start at 3,000–5,000 pieces per style; changing paper grade or adding a certification mark means a new proof, and the sample fee is credited against bulk while the lost calendar time is not. Whether an eco claim can be printed is not a printing question — it is whether the certificate you hold covers this batch.
