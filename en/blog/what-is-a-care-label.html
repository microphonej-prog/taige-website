What Is a Care Label? Legal Role, Symbols and Placement

By TAIGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 10, 2026

A care label — also called a wash label or instructions label — is the small label permanently attached to a garment that states fibre content, washing and ironing instructions and, in most markets, origin and the company placing the item on the market. It is a legal document, not a marketing surface: a hang tag can be beautiful, but it is cut off; the inspector reads the care label.

1. The four blocks of information a care label must carry

Most supplier formats carry four blocks in this order: fibre composition by percentage, care symbols or written instructions, country of origin, and the identity of the company placing the garment on the market. The order can change; a missing block cannot. Percentages must total 100%, and blend tolerance against the tested sample is normally kept inside ±3%.

The differences sit in one sentence: under EU Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 fibre names and percentages must be given in the language of the member state of sale, and Germany adds its Textilkennzeichnungsgesetz (TKG). Japan requires composition and handling under the Household Goods Quality Labelling Act, Korea requires quality marking including fibre blend under the Electrical Appliances and Consumer Products Safety Control Act, and Brazil requires Portuguese wording plus importer identification under the Consumer Protection Code (Lei 8.078/1990).

2. Symbols: which system to print

Market Symbol system Watch out for
EU / UK EN ISO 3758 symbols Wording in the language of sale
US / Canada Symbols or words (16 CFR 423) French required in Quebec
Japan JIS L 0001 symbols Composition and handling both required
Korea KS symbols plus Korean text Fibre blend in percent
Brazil Text first, symbols optional Importer or responsible party named

Never mix two systems on one label: a Japanese tub with EU dots and no temperature reads as nonsense to a consumer and to an inspector. The five EN ISO 3758 symbols — tub, triangle, iron, circle, square — cover washing, bleaching, drying, ironing and professional dry cleaning, which serves most markets; Japan and Korea need their own symbol sets plus local-language text.

3. Placement and size

Common retail placement is the left side seam, 7–10 cm above the hem, or centre back neck under the brand label. The only real test: a customer must read it without unpicking a seam. A 20 × 40 mm coated label at 0.15–0.25 mm thickness fits four lines of small text; a 15 mm ribbon label fits two, which is why most brands split composition onto the care label and size onto a separate size label.

4. Materials and durability

Coated nylon taffeta survives repeated washing and keeps print crisp; polyester satin is softer on skin but scuffs; cotton tape is cheapest and fades fastest. Test to the number of washes claimed on the label using EN ISO 105-C06 or AATCC 61, and check three things: ink bleeding into the white ground, edge fray, and colour transfer onto the garment. Childrenswear and activewear usually demand ten washes or more.

5. Five failures that cost more than the label

  • Percentages that do not total 100%, or drift more than ±3% from the tested sample.
  • Composition that disagrees with the hang tag — the care label is the one that counts.
  • Symbols without a temperature where the fabric needs one — wool with a tub and no 30 and no single bar, for example.
  • A care label sewn into a lining layer the customer cannot reach is no label at all.
  • No EU responsible person after GPSR took effect — the fastest way to be stopped by market surveillance.
Cost reality: a 20 × 40 mm coated care label costs a few cents each at 10,000 pieces; a recall or a customs hold costs thousands. Order the label with the garment, not after it is finished.
