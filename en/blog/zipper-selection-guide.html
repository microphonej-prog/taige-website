Garment Zipper Selection: Teeth, Size Numbers and Sliders

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated October 3, 2026

The zipper is the most complained-about and most underestimated trim on a garment: it decides whether the placket opens smoothly and how the garment feels in the customer's first minute. Selection really comes down to five variables — tooth material, size number, tape, slider and structure — yet each choice feeds into cost, wash performance and compliance paperwork. This guide covers the specification parameters, tooth material comparison, sliders and structures, matching to fabric, common defects and the acceptance checklist.

Keep one thing in mind: a zipper rarely fails on its own — almost every problem comes from a mismatch with the fabric, the care conditions or the sewing process.

1. Five Specification Parameters to Fix Up Front

ParameterWhat it means and typical values
Size number#3 at about 3 mm tooth width for shirts and dresses; #5 at about 5.25 mm as the outerwear workhorse; #8 at about 8 mm for heavy coats and down jackets; #10 and up for large pieces and bags
LengthSeparate total tape length from working length (bottom stop to top stop) and quote and inspect on the same basis so nothing is disputed on arrival
TapePolyester tape is standard; width and colour must match the fabric — confirm same-colour or contrast against a colour card and pre-test contrast pairs
SliderAuto-lock, semi-lock or non-lock; pulls come in metal or plastic, with shape and plating options and space for a brand logo
StructureClosed-end, open-end, two-way, invisible or water-resistant, chosen by position and use

2. Choosing Between Three Tooth Materials

Tooth materialCharacteristicsTypical use
NylonLight, soft, matte, dyeable and corrosion-resistant, with moderate strength and a friendly costOuterwear, activewear, dresses, childrenswear, bags
Resin (moulded plastic)Injection moulded with the widest colour range, no nickel release, higher hardness and a near-metal lookOuterwear, childrenswear, eco-positioned styles, contrast-colour design
MetalBrass, aluminium or alloy: the best hand feel and pull strength, heavier, and the plating needs attention for nickel release and salt sprayDenim, leather, workwear, denim bags

Metal zippers bound for the EU need the plating checked for nickel release (the EN 1811 migration limit) and for salt spray; plastic sliders and resin teeth carry no nickel risk, which is why childrenswear and next-to-skin styles often favour them.

3. Sliders and Structures: Function Drives the Experience

  • Auto-lock: locks as soon as the pull drops, so the placket cannot slide open on its own — the usual choice for coats and trouser flies
  • Semi-lock and non-lock: semi-lock needs a light push to release and feels lighter; non-lock suits pockets and decorative positions that should slide freely
  • Two-way: for long coats and sleeping bags, opening from both ends so it does not dig in when sitting
  • Invisible and water-resistant: invisible zippers hide the teeth behind the tape for dresses and side seams, while water-resistant versions add a sealing film for outdoor and rainwear

4. Matching the Fabric and the Garment

  • Pick the size number from fabric weight and placket thickness: #3 for light fabric, #5 for standard outerwear, #8 or above for heavy and down — an undersized number is the top cause of broken teeth and jamming
  • Shrinkage match: the tape's wash shrinkage must be close to the fabric's, or the placket waves and wrinkles after washing — harder to fix than any tooth material issue
  • Sewing fit: use a ball-point needle with the right presser foot, match thread to the fabric colour, and leave clearance at the foot so the tape does not jam or skip stitches
  • Childrenswear and compliance: no sharp edges or pointed pulls; for the US watch the lead and phthalate limits, for the EU the chemical limits and the pull strength of small parts

5. Common Problems and Fixes

SymptomUsual causeWhat to do
Stiff or jamming zipPoor tooth engagement or too-soft tapeChange the tooth material or stiffen the tape, then test smoothness and pull strength
Placket wrinkles after washingThe tape and fabric shrink at very different ratesRun washing tests in parallel and switch to a low-shrinkage tape
Plating wears or rustsThe plating fails salt spray or perspiration exposureRequest salt spray and nickel release reports and move to an eco-friendly plating
Broken teethThe size number is too small or the tooth material is too weakGo up a size number or switch to metal teeth and specify pull strength
Tape colour bleedingInsufficient colour fastness or batch-to-batch dye variationAsk for water and rubbing fastness reports, confirm against a colour card and keep a retained sample
Slider comes offWeak top and bottom stopsState the stop material and strength requirement in the order
💡 Tip: send the fabric swatch, placket thickness and care conditions — we run wash and smoothness tests on the real cloth before locking tooth material, size number and tape spec, so problems never wait for bulk.

6. Enquiry and Acceptance Checklist

ItemWhat to fill in
Use and positionGarment type, placket, pocket, side seam or fly, and how often it is opened
Size number and lengthThe size number such as #5, finished length and the measuring basis (total tape or working length)
Tooth material and tapeNylon, resin or metal; tape colour reference and whether it is a contrast
Slider and pullLock type, pull shape and finish, and whether a brand logo is printed
Care and complianceWash temperature, dry cleaning or not, nickel and lead limits, and whether third-party test reports are needed
Quantity and lead timeQuantity per colour and size, sampling and bulk lead time, and whether staged deliveries are needed
