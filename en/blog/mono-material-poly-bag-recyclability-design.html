Mono-Material Poly Bags: Recyclable Apparel Packaging Is Decided at the Resin

By TAGE Packaging · Updated November 25, 2026

An apparel packaging bag gets rejected on the sorting line not because it is plastic, but because three materials are bonded together: a PET window, a nylon barrier, carbon black pigment, a paper header card. Any one of them is enough for near-infrared sorting equipment to reject it. Mono-material packaging does not simply mean one plastic; it means film, pigment, adhesive, print and accessories all stay inside the same recycling stream. We make custom garment packaging bags and paper bags in Dongguan, and this guide covers the four design decisions, how the RecyClass and APR criteria are used, and how to word a recyclable claim so it does not become greenwashing.

1. Recyclability Starts at the Resin, Not at the Bag

Three things decide whether a poly bag enters the PE film stream: whether the material is mono (mainly LDPE and LLDPE, with no PET, PP, PA, PVC or metallised layer), whether sorting equipment can identify it, and whether a recycler will take it, which depends on recyclate quality and end markets. The first two are locked when the die and the material are chosen; fixing them after mass production means reformulating and starting again. The reference documents for mono-material design are design-for-recycling guidelines, not marketing sheets: the RecyClass Design for Recycling Guidelines, the CEFLEX design guide for flexible packaging, and the APR Design Guide in the United States. They express one logic with different scoring, so when a buyer asks which RecyClass class a bag reaches, the answer has to map to a specific structure, not to the word recyclable.

2. The Four Design Decisions

Design item Choice that recycles Choice that blocks it Trade-off
Film structure Monolayer or coextruded PE at 30 to 50 μm PET, PA or PVC lamination, metallised film, nylon window Barrier for recyclability; high-protection categories need a separate assessment
Pigment Natural, white, or an IR-detectable grey-black Carbon black pigment, which absorbs NIR so the sorter cannot see it Loss of opacity, replaced by an opacifying masterbatch
Adhesive and accessories PE-compatible hot melt, PE zipper, no accessories Acrylic pressure-sensitive adhesive, metal clips, paper header Replace the header with a punched fold, or design it to be torn off cleanly
Print and label Water-based or low-migration ink printed direct, under 50% coverage Full-surface lamination, metal foil, inseparable paper label Direct print reduces line speed through ink load and drying

3. Carbon Black, Calliper and Barrier: Three Overlooked Details

  • Carbon black is the most common fatal detail: under NIR sorting a black bag reads almost like background, so the plant never picks it out. Switching to an IR-detectable charcoal or deep green changes the look far less than expected.
  • Thinner is not automatically greener: below about 25 μm some plants treat the film as contamination, and stiffness drops while bag failure rises. The working range is usually 30 to 50 μm, chosen by garment weight and shipping mode.
  • Barrier layers depend on ratio and structure, so never self-declare: a small EVOH share is tolerated in some design guidelines, but the ceiling and the neighbouring layers must be assessed against that specific guideline and supported by a structure declaration from the converter.
  • The recycling mark is part of the design: a sorting logo printed in the wrong place, whether Triman with info-tri in France, the separation mark in Korea or the material mark under Japan's container and packaging law, sends the consumer to the wrong bin.

4. How to Word the Recyclable Claim

ISO 14021 governs self-declared environmental claims, and its core requirement is qualification: writing recyclable without saying where it is recyclable fails as a claim. A workable wording is recyclable where PE film collection exists, next to the relevant symbol, plus a clear statement whether the bag alone or the bag with its header is recyclable, because the recovery rates differ sharply. Recycled content is a separate claim: the PCR share must be traceable, with EN 15343 as the common basis for traceability and conformity assessment of recycled plastics, or RecyClass recycled content certification. GRS or RCS only enters when the claim attaches to a textile rather than to packaging.

Three sentences to avoid: 100% biodegradable, which PE is not; eco-friendly bag, which has no definition; and carbon-neutral packaging, unless there is an ISO 14067 calculation and offset evidence. Under the direction of the EU green claims rules, these are the first to be challenged for substantiation.

5. Market Notes

  • EU: the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation PPWR (EU) 2025/40 sets a timetable for recyclability and recycled content, tightening design requirements for flexible packaging year by year; chemicals remain under REACH (EC) 1907/2006.
  • France: packaging EPR under the AGEC law n° 2020-105 is operated through CITEO, the Triman and info-tri must be legible, and the Code de la consommation forbids misleading information, so a 30 cm x 40 cm size printed on the bag must match reality.
  • Spain: Ley 7/2022 introduced the tax on non-reusable plastic packaging, currently 0.45 EUR per kg of non-recycled plastic by weight, while Real Decreto 1055/2022 sets packaging and etiquetado duties; buyers often ask for a taxable weight schedule.
  • Japan: the Plastic Resource Circulation Act requires businesses to rationalise use and curb discharge, while the Containers and Packaging Recycling Act adds material marking duties; bag dimensions are stated in cm and calliper in μm, with 25 to 35 cm a common size for womenswear tops.
  • Korea: packaging falls under EPR items of the Act on the Promotion of Saving and Recycling of Resources and needs a separation discharge mark; environmental claims fall under the Act on Fair Labelling and Advertising, so a recycled content figure must have a traceable basis.
  • China: under the plastic pollution control policy and the dual carbon goals, degradable bags fall under GB/T 38082; for export orders, grammage and calliper are stated in g/m² and mm, and mixing 0.45 mm with 45 μm invites misunderstanding.

6. What to Send With an Enquiry

  • Garment type and unit weight, which set the calliper range from 30 to 50 μm and the bag format; a light top and a down jacket will not share the same film.
  • Whether a header card or a window is required; these are where mono-material most often breaks, so confirm first whether a punched fold or full transparency will do.
  • Colour and opacity needs: if opacity is required, specify an IR-detectable dark masterbatch rather than discovering later that carbon black keeps the bag out of the stream.
  • Target markets and marks: Triman, separation marks and material marks sit in different places, so settle the layout once or pay for a new plate.
  • Recycled content requirement and its traceability route: if a PCR share is wanted, name the evidence at the same time - EN 15343 traceability, RecyClass certification or a transaction certificate - and never print an unsupported percentage.
