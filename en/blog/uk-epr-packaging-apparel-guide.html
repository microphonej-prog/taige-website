UK Extended Producer Responsibility for Packaging: An Apparel Brand Checklist

By TAGE Packaging &nbsp;·&nbsp; Updated November 23, 2026

The UK packaging EPR scheme changed what a clothing brand has to know about its own bags. Not roughly, and not in aggregate: per material, per weight, per nation. A brand that ships 60,000 orders a year now needs the gram weight of every poly bag, mailer and carton it supplies — numbers that usually live nowhere in a fashion company, because packaging was always bundled into the price of something else.

This guide covers the obligation thresholds, the packaging versus product distinction that trips most brands, the six data points per material, a weight formula you can apply to a specification sheet today, and the filing errors that turn a manageable exercise into a penalty.

Who Reports, and Who Pays

Obligation under pEPR is triggered by two tests taken together: UK turnover and the tonnage of packaging you supply into the UK market. As a rule of thumb, larger producers above both thresholds report full data and pay disposal fees; smaller producers above the lower band report data only. The thresholds have been revised more than once since the scheme was announced, so confirm the current figures in Defra guidance before you file rather than relying on a number from a summary article — including this one.

Dual obligation is common. pEPR runs alongside the older packaging waste regulations and their PRN system during the transition. Being registered for one does not register you for the other.

Your Poly Bag Is Packaging. Your Hang Tag Is Not.

The definition turns on function: packaging is what contains, protects, presents or enables handling of the product. A poly bag, tissue paper, mailer, carton, tape, void fill and any label affixed to packaging all count. The hang tag attached to the garment, like the care label sewn into the seam, is part of the product — it is sold with the item and stays with it. That single distinction can change reported tonnage by several percent, and it is the line buyers ask about first.

Six Data Points Per Material

Reporting is built on a material-by-material table, not on an invoice total. For every packaging component you supply, collect these six fields and keep them with the specification sheet.

Field What goes in it
Material category Paper/board, plastic film, rigid plastic, aluminium, steel, glass, fibre composite
Weight per unit In grams, from the supplier specification or a measured sample
Units supplied Annual piece count, including spares and reships
Nation split England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland listed separately
Household or non-household Direct to consumer is household; palletised to a retailer warehouse is non-household
Recyclability class Mono-material, recyclable mix, or not recyclable, per the current classification

Getting Weight Data From Your Trims Supplier

You do not need a laboratory. A poly bag weight follows from thickness and area: grams per square metre is roughly the film thickness in µm multiplied by the material density, about 0.92 for LDPE. A 30 µm LDPE bag with two 250 × 350 mm panels therefore carries about 27.6 g/m² over 0.175 m², which is close to 4.8 g per bag. Cartons are simpler: board grammage in g/m² multiplied by the total surface area of the blank.

One supplier request covers most of it: ask for bag weight in grams and thickness in µm on every packing list, plus board grammage and carton blank dimensions. Suppliers who already publish this avoid a month of email archaeology at filing time. Where a component is composite — a paper mailer with a plastic window, a carton with a PP tape strip — report each material separately.

Fees, Modulation and What Actually Moves the Bill

Fees are charged per tonne by material and adjusted by recyclability, so the same weight in plastic film costs more than in paper or board. Rates are reset each year and vary by nation; take the current rate card from the scheme administrator rather than a number quoted in a blog post, and model the change rather than the level — a brand switching from a laminated mailer to an uncoated paper one usually sees a larger effect from recyclability class than from a few grams of paper.

  • Moving from a 60 µm to a 40 µm bag cuts film weight by a third, which shows up directly in both tonnage and fee.
  • Removing a plastic window or a laminated layer can move a component into a better recyclability class, which often outweighs the gram saving.
  • Right-sizing a carton removes paper and void fill at the same time, because a smaller box needs less of both.

Five Filing Errors That Cost Money

  • Counting hang tags as packaging. It inflates paper tonnage that was never packaging, and it is the first thing an auditor questions.
  • Ignoring tape, stickers and void fill. They are small per order and large per year; a 3 g tape strip over 200,000 orders is 600 kg.
  • Using the carton gross weight. Report the packaging only, and split composite cartons by material.
  • Treating everything as household. Wholesale pallets to retailer warehouses are non-household and carry different obligations.
  • No supplier specifications on file. Without thickness, grammage and dimensions in writing, a filing cannot be defended two years later.

What to Ask Your Trims Supplier

Ask for five values on the specification, every time: bag weight in grams, film thickness in µm, carton board grammage in g/m², carton blank dimensions in mm, and the material of any tape or sticker. If you sell into the EU as well as the UK, keep two data sets: pEPR for the UK and a registration per member state under the EU packaging rules. The material categories are similar, the thresholds and portals are not.
