Loungewear and Sleepwear Trims Guide: Fleece, Set Ratios, Gift Boxes

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 23, 2026

Loungewear and sleepwear is one of the fastest-growing apparel categories, and its trims needs differ from ordinary garments: it usually sells as a top-and-trouser set, uses flannel, coral fleece, polar fleece and modal heavily, is worn often and washed frequently, and increasingly moves through livestream and gift-box channels. This guide sets out how neck labels, care labels, size labels, hang tags and packaging should be specified for loungewear.

This guide works through five steps — category characteristics, neck labels and tagless options, care and size labels, hang tags, then packaging — and closes with a specification table you can copy straight into your quote request.

1. Why Loungewear Trims Cannot Copy a Standard Garment Spec

  • Sold as a set, so ratios must match: neck labels, size labels and tags for top and trousers usually ship in a 1:1 ratio, and a wrong completeness rate blocks the whole shipment — the ratio table must match the cutting ticket
  • Fleece lint sticks to labels: the short pile of flannel, coral fleece and polar fleece clings to white plain-weave woven labels. A dark or tonal satin neck label looks cleaner and stays presentable in photography
  • Frequent washing: loungewear is worn almost daily and washed weekly, so the care label should be specified for more than 20 washes; standard coated tape and pale print go chalky over time
  • Livestream and gift channels: a high share is bought as a gift or for self-use, so packaging has to hold up on camera. Keeping tag, insert card and gift box in one visual system turns unboxing into listing material

2. Neck Labels and Tagless Options: Fleece, Modal and Cotton

On loungewear the neck label has to solve three things at once: no prickle, no lint attraction, and wash durability. For fleece, use a thin satin woven label with ultrasonic-cut edges. Modal and lyocell feel slippery, so avoid stiff heavyweight woven labels. Cotton and rib knit take a standard plain-weave label. For close-fitting or thin summer styles, a tagless heat-transfer label works, but test wash durability and the effect of fabric softener first.

  • Fold affects feel: a centre-fold label lies flatter than a flat label and suits fleece; if both sides should show branding on a hanging style, specify double-sided or double fold
  • Colour follows the fabric: dark coral fleece pairs with charcoal or tonal labels, while light cotton grounds take white or ecru. Match neck label colour to an approved swatch, not a screen
  • Label languages follow the market: Chinese alone is enough for domestic livestream channels; exports to Europe generally need composition and wash symbols in local languages, per the selling country

3. Care Labels and Size Labels: Content and Set Combination

  • Elastane must be listed: loungewear almost always contains elastane, such as 95% cotton and 5% elastane; omitting the stretch fibre is the most common inconsistency found in spot checks
  • Symbols must match testing: if the label says machine wash at 30°C, it must survive machine washing. Where fleece cannot be tumble dried, say so in the symbols to avoid shrinkage returns
  • Two size labels per set: one inside the neckline of the top and one inside the trouser waistband, with identical sizing. Gift-box sets can merge size and care information into a single combined label to cut sewing operations

4. Hang Tags: Set Tags, Price Tickets and Gift Box Cards

  • One main tag per set: hang a single tag on the top and place a matching insert or size card in the trouser pocket or gift box — cheaper, and the two pieces do not pull against each other
  • Price tickets and fasteners: retail channels use a plastic fastener plus price ticket, while e-commerce often uses an adhesive price label or prints the price on the tag — punch positions must be designed separately
  • Gift box cards: ribbon-tie the card to the box or make a free-standing card. Both need a ribbon hole, and the card stock must not tear under load

5. Packaging: Gift Boxes, Zip Bags, Moisture and Unboxing

  • Gift boxes and drawer boxes: check that the inner tray matches the folded thickness of the garment, and keep tissue and tag in the same paper family for a coherent look
  • Zip bags with venting: sealed bags trap odour, especially with fleece; add micro-perforations or leave a gap at the zip. Reserve a barcode area so labels do not cover branding
  • Moisture and sea freight: loungewear made in the south and shipped by sea can take on moisture inside the container, so a PE bag with desiccant is standard; bulky winter fleece benefits from an outer sleeve to reduce compressed thickness

7. Sampling and Acceptance: Five Checks

  1. Wear and rub: confirm the neck label does not prickle, and on fleece watch whether label edges catch lint
  2. Five washes: check care label legibility, label edge fraying and seam integrity, and on fleece watch for noticeable shedding
  3. Staining and colourfastness: dark labels must not stain light fleece, and pale labels must not show through dark fabric
  4. Set ratio check: reconcile neck labels, size labels and tags for tops and trousers against the cutting ticket, and confirm completeness rates and shortage rules
  5. Gift box and camera test: fold the garment into the box and shoot a set of unboxing photos to confirm tag position, tissue layering and brand information read clearly
💡 Tip: with loungewear sets, the failures are rarely about printing — they are about ratios and lint.
