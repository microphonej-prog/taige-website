Garment Trims Cost Reduction: 8 Levers That Keep Quality

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 17, 2026

Cutting trims cost is not the same as pushing the price down. Price pressure only moves the cost into after-sales: faded care labels, snapped strings, bags that will not fit the carton — all paid back through rework, claims and lost orders. Effective cost reduction redesigns specifications, processes and order structure without touching function or compliance.

This guide sets out eight workable cost-reduction levers, plus five false economies that come back doubled in after-sales.

1. Separate Necessary Cost From Waste

Necessary cost comes from material and labour. Waste usually comes from four places: over-specification (GSM and finishes nobody asked for), order sizes that are too small (setup and plate fees dominate the unit price), too many SKUs (each version needs its own plates, material prep and retention samples), and fragmented ordering (logistics and follow-up costs double). Cut the waste first, then discuss materials.

2. Lever One: Rationalise Specifications

  • Hang tag GSM: 300–350 gsm art paper already carries most brand hang tags; heavier stock is only needed for a substantial feel or multi-process double-sided work
  • Size consolidation: bring hang tags down to one or two sizes so die-cutters and impositions can be shared, saving plates and waste
  • Woven label width: set the width from the content and the minimum legible type size; wider labels consume more yarn and look heavier
  • Care label layout: fit fibre content, size, origin and wash symbols within one length instead of stretching the label to absorb extra information

3. Lever Two: Process Substitution

One visual requirement usually has several possible routes with clearly different costs. Fix the functional bottom line first, then choose the process.

4. Lever Three: Imposition and Shared Plates

If different versions for the same brand (size labels, fibre labels, hang tag backs) share size and stock, they can run on one plate and share setup, plate and waste costs. The precondition is a common layout standard — which is why we suggest a specification review before each season change.

5. Lever Four: Quantities and Scheduling

Trims costs are highly volume-sensitive, but 'bigger is cheaper' has a limit: quantities beyond your stock turnover turn saved cents into tied-up cash and dead stock. A safer mix: order core styles against a quarterly forecast, test new styles at minimum quantity, and book standard reorders three to four weeks ahead. Peak season (August to November) and the run-up to Chinese New Year are the busiest windows, so reserving capacity early usually costs less than paying a rush premium later.

6. Lever Five: SKU Pruning and Standardisation

  • Standardise stock and GSM: share one or two papers across categories for concentrated purchasing and simpler waste and replenishment
  • Standardise strings and fasteners: every extra variant adds stock, and more risk of snapped strings or mismatched colours
  • Standardise bag sizes: one or two sizes can cover most categories instead of a new die for every product
  • Fewer colourways: merge close shades to share plates and dies and cut retention samples and storage work

7. Lever Six: Hidden Packing and Logistics Costs

Bag thickness, packing split and carton size all move the total cost: a few microns of film is invisible on a single order but real money at 100,000 units; splitting by store or size saves hours of sorting at the client's end; and cartons that do not match container space turn directly into freight and handling losses.

8. Five False Economies to Avoid

  • Cutting GSM or film thickness: the feel and stiffness drop, and the first touchpoint on arrival looks cheap
  • Switching to cheap coating or yarn: fading, fraying and breakage after washing, with rework and claims doubling the saving
  • Skipping testing and retention samples: nothing to compare against on a reorder and no evidence if a shade dispute arises
  • Cheaper strings or fasteners: breakage rates rise, and brand damage plus returns far exceed the saving
  • Rock-bottom prices to get it made: bulk does not match the sample, and remaking is the most expensive option of all

9. How to Execute: A 30-Minute Specification Review

  1. Pull the last twelve months of trims purchases and mark the top 20% of items by volume — start there
  2. Check each specification for parts nobody needs: GSM, size, processes, colour versions
  3. Merge versions that share size and stock and run them on one plate
  4. Confirm volume price breaks and price-lock windows, and share your quarterly volume forecast
  5. Write testing and retention sample requirements into the spec sheet: cost cutting must never remove quality evidence
