The Desiccant Sachet Is the Last Plastic Nobody Specifies

By TAGE Packaging · Updated November 27, 2026

A 60 × 40 × 30 cm garment export carton usually carries four to twenty desiccant sachets. They are not on the trims list, they have no purchase specification, few people weigh them, and yet they are three things at once: the last small component still built from laminated film, the consumable most often over-dosed, and the small piece of plastic most likely to slip into a paper bale. Treating desiccant as a trim with a specification will not move tonnes, but it is the item you can fix within one season.

1. Dosage First: Over-Dosing Is the Waste

Desiccant dosage is not a handful by feel. The working method calculates the water load from carton volume, the moisture held by the packaging materials, transit days and destination humidity, then converts it into desiccant units. German standard DIN 55473 defines the desiccant unit and the test conditions for sachets, and samples are conditioned to DIN 50014 standard atmosphere (23 °C / 50 % relative humidity) before measurement. The most common error is adding one more sachet for safety: it does not make the garment drier, it just adds one more item to dispose of per carton and a few grams nobody sees on the customs weight.

2. Sachet Material Decides the Waste Stream

Four sachet constructions carry the same word on the invoice and end up in completely different streams. The difference between uncoated paper and PE-coated paper is not moisture transmission but the film layer itself; non-woven and Tyvek-style high-density polyethylene sachets are strong and low-dust, but inside a paper bale they are contamination. Getting the supplier to write sachet material, grammage in g/m² and film presence into the spec is the whole job at this step.

Sachet material Where it goes One line for the spec
Uncoated paper Paper stream, repulpable Uncoated, no PE film, 60-80 g/m² paper
PE-coated paper Flexible plastic, in practice mostly energy recovery If a laminate is required, state film type and gauge
Non-woven PP Plastic stream; contamination inside a paper carton Only where strength is essential, with a removal instruction
Tyvek-type HDPE fibre Hard to recycle; low mass but not negligible Specify only for food-contact or severe humidity; paper elsewhere

3. Regeneration: What Can Be Reused, What Cannot

Silica gel is inert; drying it at 100-150 °C restores most of its capacity, which makes it suitable for internal warehouse loops where the same sachets travel with returnable totes and never enter a customer carton. Molecular sieve needs 200-300 °C, costs more energy and rarely pays back. Clay (bentonite) collapses structurally once wet and loses capacity on reuse, so do not count it as reusable. Indicator gels need a closer look: the classic blue-to-pink type uses cobalt(II) chloride, which carries a harmonised carcinogenicity classification (Carc. 1B) in the EU and is now refused outright by many brand RSLs. Non-indicating or alternative-indicator sachets are simpler to defend.

4. Sorting: Why a Small Sachet Spoils a Big Bale

When a carton enters paper recycling, the desiccant sachet is rarely taken out. Uncoated paper sachets disintegrate on the pulper and can be verified with repulpability methods such as PTS-RH 021/97, but PE-coated and non-woven sachets become screen rejects — small in mass, expensive to handle. The effective action is not material but process: write remove desiccant before baling into the warehouse work instruction, and print one removal line inside the carton. That costs less than changing material and works better than arguing later about who contaminated the bale.

5. Five Lines the Specification Needs

  • Dose per carton: in desiccant units to DIN 55473, not in sachets per box
  • Sachet material and grammage: uncoated paper, 60-80 g/m², no PE film; if laminated, state film type and µm
  • Indicator: whether cobalt chloride is present; if so, attach the SDS and check the brand RSL
  • Removal instruction: the line inside the carton and the warehouse step
  • Packing and labelling: outer bag material, product name and lot on the label for traceability and declarations

6. Cost, and the Regulation Question

On cost, uncoated paper runs slightly above PE-coated, but the saving is not only reputational: paper goes into the paper stream and the warehouse does not need a separate plastic sorting step. The trade-off is higher moisture transmission, so a 40-day sea leg or a rainy season pushes the dose up, and the two effects often cancel out. On regulation, desiccant is usually counted in the declared packaging weight in the EU, which puts its grammage into the brand's packaging tonnage and EPR fee; France folds this into the AGEC regime and the Triman marking declarations, and Spain requires packaging placed on the market to be declared under Real Decreto 1055/2022. That is why sachet material and grammage belong in the purchase specification rather than in the supplier's internal file.

Three checks: is the dose calculated in units or by feel; are sachet material and grammage in g/m² written into the purchase spec; and does the warehouse actually remove sachets before baling.
