Children's Clothing Label Safety: CPSIA, EN 14682 & Compliance Essentials

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 2, 2026

The biggest difference between children's clothing labels and adult ones is that they are also safety components: if the hang tag, string or care label contains harmful substances or small parts that can detach, the product can be recalled. The US CPSIA and EU EN 14682 are the two hurdles cross-border sellers most often face, and this guide walks you through every key point.

1. Why Children's Clothing Labels Face Stricter Rules

Children explore the world by mouthing and pulling at labels. That is why children's clothing label regulations cover two kinds of risk: chemical risks (lead and phthalates in inks, coatings and metal parts) and physical risks (choking from detached small parts, strangulation from long cords). The US CPSIA, the EU General Product Safety Regulation and EN 14682 together form the most common safety framework for children's clothing labels today.

2. US CPSIA: Lead, Phthalates & Small Parts

  • Lead content: no more than 90 ppm in paint or surface coatings and 100 ppm in substrate — metal eyelets, pins and other hang tag fittings must use compliant materials
  • Phthalates: eight phthalates are each limited to 0.1% in accessible plastic parts such as PVC hang tags and plastic loops
  • Small parts test: for garments designed for children under 3, label attachments must pass the 16 CFR Part 1501 small parts cylinder test to prevent choking
  • CPC (Children's Product Certificate): children's garments exported to the US must ship with a CPC stating the product, applicable rules and testing lab details

3. EU EN 14682: Cord Safety & Label Design

EN 14682 is the EU harmonised standard for cords on children's clothing, detailing the length, position and elasticity of cords on garments for children under 14. Hang tag strings and care label loops are not structural garment cords, but if designed to be worn or long enough to encircle the neck, they are reviewed by enforcement authorities too. The standard practice: keep hang tag strings under 7.5 cm and easily breakable, and sew care labels in place rather than hanging them.

💡 Tip: for children's clothing, use a breakaway cotton string with a paper tag, and avoid hard fittings like metal eyelets or pins; for care information, prefer soft woven tape sewn into the side seam — compliant and comfortable.

4. Safe Choices for Hang Tags, Inks & Materials

  • Paper: choose FSC-certified or food-grade board and avoid excessive optical brighteners; PVC hang tags carry high risk in children's markets — prefer paper or PET alternatives
  • Inks: use water-based or soy inks and request heavy metal and phthalate test reports such as EN 71-3 migration testing
  • Finishing: lamination and foil stamping are safe processes, but confirm films and foils do not rely on lead-based pigments; rounded-corner die cutting avoids paper cuts
