Where to Attach a Hang Tag: Position, Hole and Display

By TAIGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 7, 2026

Where a hang tag is attached looks like a small decision and actually settles three things at once: whether the customer reads the brand at first glance, whether the tag survives being tried on, and whether it is still there at return. On the same T-shirt, a tag at the centre of the neckline reads crisp, one at the side seam reads like a price ticket nobody removed; on tailoring, a tag at the cuff pulls the moment an arm lifts. Placement is not taste — it is a parameter you can write into the spec sheet.

1. One Principle: Readable at First Glance, Out of the Way

When someone picks up a garment the eye lands on the neckline, the chest, the waistband. A tag hanging at the edge of those three zones gets read; at the cuff, hem or pocket most people turn the piece over twice and put it down. Conversely, the closer the position sits to a moving part — wrist, waist, zip — the higher the risk of tearing it off or pressing a mark into the cloth. Positions satisfying both conditions come down to one or two per category.

2. Where Each Category Hangs It

Category First choice Why
T-shirt, hoodie Below the back-neck label, or top of the left side seam Brand visible on pickup; does not interfere with pulling it on
Shirt Second buttonhole, or inside the collar stand Looped on a button it stays put, and shows naturally when the collar folds
Trousers Right belt loop, or inside the front pocket mouth Reads outward on a rail and does not dig in when tried on
Skirts, dresses Centre back neck, or side seam at the waist On a long dress a neckline tag hides the line; for eveningwear a clear elastic loop works better
Jackets, coats Inner hanging loop, or inside pocket An external tag presses a mark into the cloth; premium lines sew it inside
Swimwear, underwear Plastic barb through the strap, or pinned straight to the care label Thin fabric will not take a punched hole, and fine straps carry little load
Socks, hats, accessories Barb through the polybag, tag outside the bag The product cannot be pierced, and scanning needs no unpacking

3. Hole Position and Load: Wrong Spot, Wrong Paper

  • Keep the hole centre at least 8 mm from the edge and 10 mm from the short side; below 6 mm even 350 g board tears on a rail
  • Keep the hole coaxial with the tag's centre of gravity: a corner hole with an off-centre body always hangs crooked, and a dozen crooked tags show on a display
  • Above 400 g, or on duplexed board, an eyelet is the reliable answer; match its bore to the cord diameter, or a thin cord wears through a wide bore
  • Never load a die-cut point: stress concentrates there and it cracks first in transit

4. Four Things a Tag Must Never Cover

  • Brand label and size: hung at the neck, the tag must sit below the label, never over the brand name or size, or the customer has to push it aside
  • Care label: readability is a legal requirement, and a tag resting on it hides composition and care
  • Craft details: embroidery, beading, embossing, panels are the reason for the price — a tag on top hides the selling point
  • Hardware and zips: looping a cord around a zip pull takes the hardest pull when trying on, and it is where teeth catch cloth

5. Retail Display and Security

When garments are packed on a rail, the ones whose tags face the same way and sit outside the shoulder line get handled noticeably more. Boutiques and department stores usually hang the tag on the right as the customer faces the rail, face outward, uncreased. A hard tag belongs on a thick part of the cloth or a dedicated spot, never on the hang tag itself — the tag gets pulled off and the security tag leaves with the garment, the most common hole in the system.

6. E-Commerce, Returns and Resale

  • Return policies usually hinge on the tag being intact and uncut, so choose a spot that survives unpacking: the neckline is safer than a cuff
  • A one-way loop lock suits high-value lines, threaded between brand label and hang tag so cutting is irreversible
  • Sets and layering: outer and inner each need their own tag, not one shared, or partial returns have no basis
  • Sticker alternative: on silk, lace or coated cloth that cannot be pierced, use a removable adhesive sticker under the same four no-cover rules

7. Shop Floor Rollout and Inspection

  • Put the position in the spec sheet: a diagram with hole-to-edge distance, which button, left or right — verbal instructions drift
  • At PP sample, shoot three photos: full front, close-up of the attachment, and inside to prove the care label is clear
  • Pull ten pieces: over 10 mm of drift is a rework, and two orientations in one batch means the station has no jig
  • Pull test: 2 kg on a single tag for 30 seconds with no slip and no tear at the hole is a common retail DC threshold
