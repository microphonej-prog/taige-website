Underwear & Intimate Apparel Label Guide: Trims Next to Your Skin Deserve More Care

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 5, 2026

Underwear, loungewear, sports bras and baby bodysuits are in direct contact with skin for nearly a third of every day. Labels that are acceptable on regular clothing become itchy, return-driving nuisances here. This underwear label guide explains how to choose main labels, care labels and hang tags for next-to-skin garments — so the wearing experience matches the quality of your fabric.

1. Three Pain Points of Next-to-Skin Labels: Scratch, Itch, Curl

Consumer complaints about next-to-skin labels cluster around three issues: hard label edges and thick seams rubbing skin red and itchy; labels that curl, stiffen or fray after a few washes; and print that fades until fiber content and care info are unreadable. The fix is a three-pronged approach: softer materials, simpler structures, and durable finishing.

2. Choosing the Main Label: Satin Woven, Taffeta Woven or Tagless Print?

For the main label at the back neckline of intimate wear, the most popular choice is a satin woven label: silky surface, soft sheen, and barely noticeable once folded and sewn into the neckline. Plain taffeta woven labels are thinner and cheaper but feel slightly stiffer, while heat-transfer tagless prints have no seams or selvedges at all — the common approach for sports bras and shapewear.

Satin wovenSilky, soft, elegant sheenGood (polyester filament is more wash-durable)Bras, briefs, loungewear, sleepwear

💡 Tip: the fold direction and selvedge finishing of a satin label decide whether the edge feels scratchy — always sew a physical sample and try it against the skin at sampling stage, not just look at a mock-up.

3. Care Labels: Concise Content, Soft Materials

Care label content for next-to-skin garments is usually short — fiber content, care symbols and size often fit in a few lines — so you can use narrower, thinner woven tape, or simply heat-transfer the information onto the seam allowance. The more folds a label has, the thicker and stiffer it gets; a small three-fold or even no-fold design keeps the side-seam label almost unnoticeable.

4. Size Labels & Hang Tags: Getting the Special Size Systems Right

Intimate apparel has one of the most complex sizing systems in clothing: EU sizes often run 70A–90E (underbust + cup), US sizes map to 32A–40E, while S/M/L and numeric codes also coexist. Confusing size marks directly inflate e-commerce return rates. For hang tags, heavier eco-friendly paper adds a premium feel, and custom die-cutting or foil stamping is common for bra gift sets and premium lines.

5. Safety & Eco Requirements Are Higher Next to the Skin

  • Dye and chemical safety: EU and US buyers often require next-to-skin garments and their labels to meet limits such as Oeko-Tex Standard 100, or to be tested for formaldehyde, banned azo dyes and similar per the buyer's testing protocol
  • Children's next-to-skin wear: baby and kids' underwear exported to the US and EU is subject to CPSIA, EN 14682 and more — see our children's clothing label safety guide
  • Eco materials: organic cotton tape, recycled-polyester woven labels and FSC-certified hang tag paper give sustainable intimate brands a credible story

6. Three Things to Do at the Sampling Stage

First, sew a real main label into a sample garment and try it against the skin. Second, run repeated machine-wash tests matching the target market's habits and watch for curling, stiffening, fading or fraying. Third, verify the care label's legibility and that the fiber content matches the actual fabric. For satin labels, choose a soft, dense yarn construction; for care labels, prefer soft thin tape; and consider heat transfer only when a truly tagless feel is required.
