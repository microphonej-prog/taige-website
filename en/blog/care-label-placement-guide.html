Where to Sew a Care Label: Placement Rules That Survive the Wash

By TAGE Packaging &nbsp;·&nbsp; Updated November 12, 2026

A care label sewn in the wrong place fails twice: the customer cannot find the fibre composition, and the label itself wears out before the garment does. Placement is a compliance decision, not a finishing detail. Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 requires the fibre composition to be stated on a label permanently attached to the garment, and in the United States 16 CFR 423 requires care instructions that stay legible for the life of the product.

This guide covers where to sew a care label on the main garment types, the stitch and fold rules that stop labels from twisting, the measurements that decide legibility, and how to put placement on the purchase order so the sewing floor cannot improvise.

1. The Four Positions That Cover Most Garments

  • Centre back neck — the default for t-shirts and knit tops, sewn 1–1.5 cm below the neckline seam. The label must lie flat against the inside back, never folded into the neck rib.
  • Left side seam — the default for shirts, dresses and skirts, placed 5–8 cm above the hem so it disappears when worn and stays clear of the side vent.
  • Inside waistband — for trousers, jeans and skirts, 2–3 cm from the centre back seam and below the waistband top stitch, so a belt does not rub across the label.
  • Inside lining or facing — for jackets and coats, sewn into the left front facing at the hem, where it can be read without turning the garment inside out.
  • Flat-lock or outside placement — for babywear and skin-sensitive products, where any label against the skin is a complaint waiting to happen.
  • Double position — brand label at the neck plus care label in the side seam, when the neck area is already taken by a woven main label.

3. What the Main Markets Actually Require

European Union: Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 requires the fibre composition in descending order on a label that is durable and legible; care symbols follow EN ISO 3758, which is not mandatory in every member state but is the de facto retail standard. France additionally requires consumer information to be given in French.

United States: 16 CFR 423 requires washing instructions that remain legible for the life of the garment, and 19 CFR 134 requires country of origin marking. A care label that fades after ten washes is a compliance failure, not a cosmetic one.

Japan and Korea: the Japanese Household Goods Quality Labelling Act requires composition, care handling and country of origin on the product, with JIS L 0001 care symbols as the reference set. Korea requires fibre mixing ratio and handling cautions under its consumer product quality labelling rules, and the label text must be in Korean.

One test decides whether a position is right: can someone holding the garment find the label in five seconds? If a customer has to turn a jacket inside out, the position is wrong.

4. The Measurements That Decide Legibility

  • Label size: 20 × 40 mm is the practical minimum for composition plus three symbols at 2 mm text height.
  • Text height: at least 1.8 mm for care text and 2 mm for fibre composition — below that, the text fills in after washing.
  • Distance from seam: 1–1.5 cm below the neckline seam, 5–8 cm above the hem, 2–3 cm from the centre back seam.
  • Seam allowance: keep at least 10 mm between the label and the overlock line so trimming cannot cut the label.
  • Fold allowance: add 5 mm at each folded end, otherwise the fold eats the printed area and the last symbol disappears.

5. Attachment: Stitches, Folds and the Five Defects to Inspect

  • Three-side sew for long labels: one short end stays open so the label flexes with the garment instead of puckering the seam.
  • Four-side sew for short labels: prevents curled corners, the most common reason a care label becomes unreadable.
  • Folded ends: double fold where the label touches skin at the neck; a single fold is enough inside a waistband.
  • Stitch density: 8–10 stitches per cm with a soft thread; a hard thread cuts the label edge after repeated washing.
  • Inspect for five things: twisted label, puckered seam, label caught in the overlock, text crossed by the stitch line, shrinkage after the first wash.

The last one is what buyers notice. If the label substrate shrinks more than the garment, run a pre-shrink wash or specify a washed tape — a 3 mm shrink on a 40 mm label wipes out the care symbols entirely.

6. How to Write Placement on the Purchase Order

Placement is reliable only when it is written down. Put five fields on the purchase order: position and distance from the seam, label size in mm, fold type, substrate with thread colour, and the durability test — three washes at 40 °C with a colour fastness check against the adjacent fabric.

Also state the language set the destination market requires. A care label that satisfies the EU does not satisfy Korea, and reprinting a label run always costs more than printing the right languages once.
