Peru Clothing Label Requirements: Spanish Text, Fibre Content and RUC

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 19, 2026

Peru clothing label compliance comes from two rules layered together: the Andean garment labelling rule covers fibre content, care instructions and country of origin, while Peru's own industrial labelling law requires the responsible party — importer, packer or distributor — to be identified by name, Peruvian tax number (RUC) and legal domicile. A missing RUC or the wrong responsible-party role is the first thing Peruvian importers ask you to fix at sampling stage.

This guide works through seven parts: the rules that apply, label fields and where they sit, fibre-composition thresholds, care symbols, how origin must be worded, the responsible-party details and enforcement, and a sampling checklist you can copy straight into an enquiry email.

1. Three Rules Apply in Peru: Andean Rule, National Law, National Standard

  • Resolución 2109/2019 (amended by 2173/2020): the garment labelling rule applicable in Peru since February 2021. It requires fibre composition, care instructions and country of origin on the permanent label, and states expressly that no conformity-assessment procedure applies to the garment label itself
  • Decreto Legislativo 1304/2016: Peru's own industrial-product labelling law, requiring the responsible operator — manufacturer, importer, packer or distributor — to be identifiable inside Peru, by name, legal domicile and RUC tax number
  • NTP 231.400:2015: the clothing and household-textile labelling standard marked mandatory in Peru's national standards catalogue. In practice Resolución 2109 is the directly applicable rule — confirm the current scope of NTP with your importer order by order
  • Regulator: INDECOPI supervises the market and, under Ley 29571 (Consumer Protection Code), can issue warnings, order corrective action or impose graded fines

3. Fibre Content: the 5% and 15% Lines

  • 5% or more: declare each fibre individually, in descending order
  • Below 5%, and collectively below 15%: may be grouped as otros (other fibres) instead of itemised
  • Functional fibres are the exception: name them even below 5% — elastane, cooling or antibacterial fibres. This is the point most often missed
  • A 100%, puro or todo claim: tolerates unavoidable foreign fibre up to 2%, or up to 5% on carded products
  • Blend tolerance: plus or minus 3% per fibre, so label values should sit where testing and specification overlap
  • Coating and lining: dealt with under a separate article of the rule and worded apart from the main fabric — confirm the wording with your importer at sampling stage

4. Care Instructions: Symbols Must Match ISO 3758

Peru allows care information as legends, standardised symbols, or both — but where symbols are used they must conform to ISO 3758:2012. The usual failure is not a missing symbol but a contradiction: a wool-cycle symbol on a garment that is really washed normally, which counts as a mismatch between label and product.

  • Keep the Spanish wording consistent: Lavar a mano, No usar blanqueador, Secar en plano, Planchar a baja temperatura
  • Latin American buyers work mainly over WhatsApp, so settle symbol treatment, language set and wash-cycle requirements in one go to avoid repeated artwork revisions
  • Label durability: Peru sets no pre-market certification for the label, yet buyers normally inspect against the standard of legible print after three domestic washes — choose care-label material and ink to that standard

5. How to Word Country of Origin So It Passes

  • Use Hecho en, Fabricado en or Elaborado en followed by the country, e.g. Hecho en China. English-only Made in China does not meet the Spanish-language requirement
  • Origin must match where the garment was actually made: labelling or packing in a third country does not change origin, and if fabric or sewing happens elsewhere the wording must match the customs paperwork
  • Keep origin wording identical across label, customs documents and carton marks so an inspection never meets three different statements

6. The Responsible-Party Fields: Easiest to Get Wrong, Costliest

In Peru the label is treated as the responsible party's sworn declaration rather than a certified document, so whatever it states must match the goods. Enforcement happens in the market: INDECOPI investigates and, under Ley 29571, can issue warnings, corrective orders or graded fines, with the ceiling for very serious infringements reaching 450 UIT.

  • Company name present but RUC missing, or the RUC is outdated because the importing entity changed from importer to distributor
  • Legal domicile given as an overseas head-office address instead of a registered Peruvian address
  • RUC only on the carton or hang tag while the garment itself carries nothing — the garment is what stays with the consumer
💡 Tip: Peru runs no pre-market certification, so the entire risk sits in whether the label is true. Lock the responsible party's name, legal domicile and valid RUC at sampling stage, and wash a fabric swatch with the care label attached — far cheaper than relabelling after arrival.

7. Six Items to Confirm Before Sampling

  1. Responsible party and RUC: confirm whether this shipment makes your buyer importer, packer or distributor, and lock name, legal domicile and valid RUC
  2. Composition: check percentages against the 5% and 15% lines, list functional fibres separately and allow the plus-or-minus 3% tolerance
  3. Care: align symbols with ISO 3758:2012 and add the Spanish wording, with no contradiction between the two
  4. Origin: fix the exact wording of Hecho en, Fabricado en or Elaborado en and match it to the customs paperwork
  5. Size and language: Spanish as the label language, sizes per the buyer's approved chart, and a clear split of work between permanent and removable labels
  6. Childrenswear extras: confirm small-part pull strength and cord requirements against the buyer's contract and destination rules rather than adult-wear defaults
