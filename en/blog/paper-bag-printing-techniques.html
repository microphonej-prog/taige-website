Eco Paper Bag Printing Explained: Choosing Between Offset, Screen, Foil Stamping & Lamination

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated August 31, 2026

Eco paper bag printing is the most common question when clothing brands customize packaging: should the logo be offset-printed, screen-printed or foil-stamped? Why do colors on kraft paper differ from the design file? This guide compares the mainstream paper bag printing techniques and covers paper weight fit, artwork requirements and acceptance tips.

1. Three Ways Bag Printing Differs from Hang Tag Printing

  • A bag is a three-dimensional structure — the print surface includes fold lines and handle areas that need space in the layout
  • Kraft paper is rough and absorbent — colors look less vivid than on coated paper, so adjust your expectations
  • Bags carry weight and handling — ink adhesion and rub resistance matter even more than on hang tags

3. Paper Weight and Technique Fit

Bags commonly use 120–300gsm paper: 120–157gsm suits light small bags, while 200gsm and above give better stiffness and load capacity. Kraft paper pairs naturally with screen printing or foil; for vivid colors choose white cardboard or add lamination. Heavier paper prints and folds more stably, at a higher cost.

4. Artwork File Requirements

  • Bleed: keep at least 3mm on all sides to avoid white edges after cutting
  • Fold lines and handle areas: mark them clearly and keep printed content away from folds
  • Color standard: specify Pantone codes and accept that the kraft base color affects how inks appear
  • File format: supply AI or PDF vector files, with text outlined and images embedded
💡 Tip: before full production on kraft bags, approve a small color proof — the same Pantone looks very different on kraft versus white board.牛皮纸袋正式生产前，先做小面积色样确认——同一潘通色号在牛皮纸上的效果与白卡纸差异明显。

5. Sampling Acceptance Checklist

  • Check whether ink cracks or shows white at the fold lines
  • Lightly scratch the printed surface with a fingernail to confirm ink adhesion
  • Test the strength and load capacity of the handle (paper twist, cotton cord or die-cut hole)
  • Confirm bulk color deviation stays within the agreed range (e.g. Delta E)
