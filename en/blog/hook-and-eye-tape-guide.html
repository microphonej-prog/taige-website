Hook and Eye Tape: The Two Square Centimetres That Take the Load

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 21, 2026

Hook and eye tape is one of the cheapest components in a garment and one of the most stressed. A bra back closes and opens several times a day for years. A trouser waistband hook takes the entire tension of the waistband every time the wearer sits down. The tape is the carrier, the hook and eye are the metal, and the stitch is the link that binds them. Define and verify all three, because the part that fails first is almost never the fabric.

1. Four Positions, Four Different Loads

A bra back uses 2 by 3 or 3 by 3 hooks on a 25 to 38 mm tape, so the load spreads over two or three hooks and a single hook sees a typical design value of 20 to 30 N. A trouser waistband hook and bar is about 20 mm wide with one hook, and it takes the whole waist tension in one place when the wearer sits: 60 to 90 N is the usual design value, the hardest working of the four. A skirt placket hook under an invisible zip carries almost nothing; what matters is thinness, 1.2 mm total or less, and lying flat. Sliders and figure-eight sliders on adjustable straps rub constantly, so coating abrasion matters more than pull strength. Four closures on one garment should not share one purchasing spec.

2. Take It Apart: Hook, Eye, Tape, Stitch

Hooks and eyes are formed from 0.6 to 0.8 mm wire. Too thin and the hook opens up after repeated cycles; too thick and the tape buckles. The tape is usually a polyester woven tape that has to stay firm across its width and not curl at the edges; under-wefted tape goes wavy after two or three washes. Use a 40/2 polyester core thread, 8 to 10 stitches per 25 mm, with backstitching at both ends. There are four coatings: nickel plated, the cheapest with the highest release risk; white nickel; nickel-free electroplating; and nylon coated. Nylon coating resists perspiration best and adds 0.1 to 0.2 mm of thickness, so for intimate apparel confirm the hand feel before committing.

3. Nickel Release Is the Real Compliance Line

REACH (EC) 1907/2006, Annex XVII, entry 27 sets the limit: metal parts in prolonged direct skin contact must not release nickel faster than 0.5 micrograms per square centimetre per week. Screening runs to EN 1811, and coated parts must first be worn and corroded per EN 12472, so a nickel layer under a lacquer is not exempt. For the United States, CPSIA caps total lead in accessible substrate of children s products at 100 ppm, and California adds Prop 65. Japan has no statutory nickel release limit, but Japanese buyers routinely cite EN 1811 in the specification. Korean children s products also go through safety confirmation under the Special Act on Safety of Children s Products.

4. The Sewing Parameters That Decide Whether It Holds

Use an 80/12 to 90/14 needle, 3.0 to 3.5 mm stitch length, backstitch 12 to 15 mm at both ends of the eye tape and a crosswise bar at the tape head. Sew the hook bar with a box-X and backstitch each corner. Three numbers do the acceptance work: closure pull, the force needed to open a mated hook and eye, commonly specified at 60 N or more for intimate apparel; seam strength on a waistband hook bar at 90 N or more; and cycle fatigue, where the closure must still mate after 3,000 to 5,000 openings with no permanent deformation. Add five washes with no hook opening or tape curl. Put those numbers in the spec and a lost hook stops being a matter of luck.

5. Five Lines for the Purchase Order

Item How to write it How to verify
Hook count and tape width 3 by 3 hooks, tape 30 mm plus or minus 0.5 mm Ten pieces per lot, hook spacing within 1 mm
Coating Nickel-free plating or nylon coated, with test report EN 1811 nickel release at or below 0.5 micrograms per square centimetre per week, after EN 12472 if coated
Pull strength Closure pull 60 N or more, hook bar seam 90 N or more Five pieces per lot on a tensile tester, record the peak
Fatigue 3,000 cycles, still mates with no permanent set Cycle tester with before and after measurement
Packing 25 m per roll, label printed with lot and colour Label matches contents, check three rolls
Five numbers for the specification: hook spacing within 1 mm, tape width within 0.5 mm, stitch length 3.0 to 3.5 mm, nickel release 0.5 micrograms per square centimetre per week, and 3,000 opening cycles.

A hook and eye costs less than a third of what a hang tag does, yet it decides a completely different class of problem. A closure that fails is a functional defect, and functional defects mean recalls or credits. A colour shift is an appearance dispute you can talk through. The difference in cost of handling is an order of magnitude. Put those five numbers in the purchase order and the metal stops being an accessory and becomes a controlled part, with purchasing, QC and the supplier all answerable to the same figures.
