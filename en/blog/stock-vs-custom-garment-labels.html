Stock or Custom Garment Labels: What You Can and Cannot Buy Off the Shelf

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 17, 2026

A stock garment label is a generic roll that is already printed: pre-printed size tapes, generic care-symbol tapes, blank satin or taffeta. It is cheap, fast, carries no setup fee, and is genuinely attractive to a brand in its first season. The catch is that generic means it contains nothing about you: no brand name, no correct fibre composition, no responsible party and no market language. This guide draws the line between stock and custom — which situations are fully served by a roll off the shelf, which ones turn a stock label into a false statement, and how the setup-cost-divided-by-price-gap break-even actually works.

1. What Stock Actually Means

Stock means fixed content: a set tape width (15, 20, 25 or 30 mm are the common ones), a generic symbol set, one size per position, sold by the roll. What it removes is artwork preparation and the screen or die setup fee, which is why the price gap is widest at low quantities. The cost is that every generic word on the tape becomes your statement: if a pre-printed composition does not match the fabric, that is a false label, not a temporary placeholder.

2. Four Places Stock Labels Break

Not every style needs custom. But hit any one of the following and the roll stops being a saving and becomes a risk.

  • Brand name and logo — there is no space on a stock tape, and adding one means a second operation (heat transfer, a small sewn tab) with its own cost and fastness questions.
  • Composition that changes by colourway — the same style in two colours can be 95% cotton 5% elastane in one and 100% cotton in the other, and a fixed stock sentence becomes false the moment it does not match.
  • Market-specific mandatory content — Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 requires the fibre composition on a permanent label with care symbols under EN ISO 3758; France adds French care wording and the responsible party, Spain requires Spanish wording, Japan requires composition, JIS L 0001 care symbols, the labeller and the country of origin, and Korea requires fibre content, KS K 0021 care symbols and origin. No generic roll satisfies all of them.
  • Size granularity — a stock size tape gives one size per label, so a style sold as S/M/L in one SKU, or multi-size packs, needs a custom size combination or a separate size sticker.

3. The Cost Comparison Buyers Get Wrong

Two quotations cannot be compared on unit price; compare landed cost per garment: (setup + unit price × quantity) ÷ quantity. Setup covers artwork, the screen or die and one proof. Stock setup is zero but may add a second operation. Break-even quantity = setup ÷ (stock unit price − custom unit price). That number is usually smaller than buyers expect, because digital printing pushes setup down and moves the break-even into the hundreds rather than the thousands. Do not forget the cost of being wrong: a composition error that forces a whole shipment to be relabelled costs tens of times the label price.

4. Which Route for Which Scenario

Scenario Stock enough? What must be custom Recommendation
Basic single-fibre style, one market Yes Brand name and the size tape Stock size tape plus heat-transfer brand
Blend fabric, three colourways No One permanent label per composition Custom, one file per composition
Export to the EU, Japan and Korea No Market language, symbol version and responsible party Custom, one file per market
Sample room, 30 sample sets Yes Size and brand Digital custom, short lead time
Industrial-laundry workwear No Wash-resistant ink and a wash test Custom, with a wash test first

5. The Hybrid Route: Blank Tape Plus Overprint

The compromise buyers overlook most often is to buy a generic care-symbol tape or blank tape and overprint the brand, size and lot by thermal transfer. One roll then serves several orders, the data changes per order, and no screen has to be opened per size. Three limits apply: the clear area must be big enough (1.2 mm cap height for the brand, plus a 3 mm margin); the ribbon grade must match the number of washes the garment will take; and the symbol version must match the market — EN ISO 3758 in the EU, JIS L 0001 in Japan, KS K 0021 in Korea — because mixing versions gives one garment two sets of instructions.

6. Revision Control: The Real Cost of a Label Change

A label is not a standalone file; it lives in four places at once — the artwork file, the purchasing BOM, the sewing specification and the factory's labelling manual. If one place is not updated, a garment ships with a label stating a composition nobody verified, which is the most common finding in market surveillance and also the cheapest one to prevent. The method is unglamorous: one file per composition, one version number per market, change all four places together, and print the version number and effective date on the purchase order. If the composition or the supplier changes, run a wash and rub test on three production labels before the version number moves.

Quick test: if the style sells into a second market, if the composition depends on the colourway, or if the brand has to appear, do not buy a stock roll. Stock's real job is samples and emergency top-ups, not production.
