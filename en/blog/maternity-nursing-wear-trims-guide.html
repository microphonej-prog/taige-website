Maternity and Nursing Wear Trims: Tagless Labels, Nursing Clips and Size Design

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated October 2, 2026

Trims on maternity and nursing wear have to beat ordinary womenswear on three points: a wider size span, more sensitive skin and fastening that can be done one-handed. This guide covers tagless constructions, functional trims such as nursing clips and hidden zips, how to word the care label and wash instructions, how to mark maternity sizes and multilingual content, and what matters in gift packaging, plus the six items to include in an enquiry.

1. Three Demands Maternity and Nursing Wear Place on Trims

  • A wide size span: one garment has to serve from pregnancy into the postnatal months, with the waist changing by twenty centimetres or more. Labels, hook-and-loop tape and adjusters must travel with that change, or the trims have to be re-sourced the following season.
  • More sensitive skin: tolerance drops during pregnancy and nursing, so stiff labels, metal hardware and rough seam edges cause redness and itching. Making everything that touches skin feel like nothing is the entry ticket for this category.
  • One-handed operation: with a baby in one arm there is only one hand free. Clip opening force, zip pull size and snap placement must be validated one-handed, not signed off after a two-handed try on a sample.

2. Making Skin-Contact Labels Feel Like Nothing

Nine out of ten labels on maternity and nursing wear sit inside the back neck or at the side waist, both directly against the skin. The order of selection should be: can it be attached without sewing, then will it scratch if sewn, and only then price.

3. Functional Trims: Nursing Clips, Hidden Zips and Snaps

  • Judge a nursing clip by opening force and noise. Too tight and it cannot be opened one-handed; too loose and it pops open while carrying the baby. Metal clips can rattle after washing or snag other laundry, so test the action before freezing the sample.
  • A hidden zip needs a pull big enough to grab and a fine, smooth tooth. For side-access nursing openings, a two-way zip or an extended pull makes one-handed use easy and keeps the garment wearable for postnatal check-ups.
  • Snaps and magnetic fasteners are quieter, but watch wash durability and magnet decay. Magnet strength falls with washing and hot tumble drying, and some markets restrict strong small magnets in transport and product safety, so check the country of sale first.
  • Give adjusters their full travel range up front. The waist and bust keep changing after birth, so hidden adjusters, extended button rows and two-way drawcords need enough slack — otherwise the garment is unwearable within the first year.

4. Care Labels and Washing: What Nursing Adds

  • Washing is both more frequent and stricter: leakages and contact with a baby mean the garment is washed almost daily. Beyond the symbols, add a practical line — mild detergent, no chlorine bleach, no fabric softener.
  • Keep detergent residue low. A baby's skin sits against the fabric, and build-up over repeated washes matters more than a single cycle, so washing before first wear is a standard line to print alongside the symbols.
  • Plan multilingual content and compliance together with the size information. Many EU states require the local official language, Canada requires English and French, and the pregnancy and nursing notes need proper translation too — not just the wash symbols.

5. Size Labels and Compliance: Marking Maternity Sizes

  • Maternity sizing differs from standard womenswear: the usual approach is to print the pre-pregnancy size with the word Maternity plus a trimester or recommended weight range, so customers do not order their everyday size by mistake.
  • Keep size and care labels away from the fullest part of the bump and the underbust line. Those two areas take the heaviest friction, and even a soft label gets pushed against the skin repeatedly and can leave marks after birth.
  • Country of origin and fibre content still follow the normal garment rules — do not drop them because the style is unusual. Fibre content on the size label must match the care label, since customs and platform audits check both.

6. Packaging and Gift Boxes: Details That Matter

  • A good share of maternity wear is bought by someone else or given as a gift. Leaving a writable To and From area on the hang tag looks better than a loose note and reduces unboxing for returns.
  • Think about post-birth storage: a reusable paper box, fabric pouch or storage bag is better received than a single-use plastic wrap, and it aligns with EU requirements on packaging reduction and recyclability.
  • Do not fix anything inside the box with metal. Pins and staples are a safety hazard around pregnancy and childcare; hold the garment with a card or paper band and add a care card inside.
💡 Tip: what goes wrong on this category is label placement and adjuster travel, not fabric. At sampling, wear the piece and repeat four moves — carrying a baby, opening the fastening one-handed, bending, sitting — then wash it five times to see whether the label curls and the hardware loosens. Clear four moves and five washes and bulk production rarely disappoints.
