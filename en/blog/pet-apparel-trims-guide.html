Pet Apparel Trims Guide: Labels, Care Tags, Sizing and Packaging

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 23, 2026

Pet apparel has grown from a novelty purchase into a serious clothing category, and its trims logic differs from human clothing: the wearer is the pet, the buyer is the owner, and sizing has to span every breed plus cats. On pet apparel, hang tags, woven labels, care labels, size labels and packaging must answer three questions at once — what it is made of, how to wash it, and whether the owner will want to photograph it.

This guide runs through five steps — how pet apparel differs from human clothing, neck labels and care labels, sizing and hang tags, safety and restricted substances, then packing and cross-border shipping — and closes with a specification table you can copy straight into your quote request.

1. How Pet Apparel Trims Differ from Human Clothing

  • Different sizing logic: dogs are usually measured by chest girth plus back length, cats by weight and neck girth. The size label must explain how to measure, or cross-border return rates climb noticeably
  • Washed far more often: pet apparel meets mud, grass and odour, so it is washed much more than human clothing. Choose the care label print method and material for frequent washing rather than copying a standard garment spec
  • More information to carry: beyond material and washing, owners ask whether it is machine washable, water repellent or has reflective trim. The back of the hang tag effectively becomes a second instruction card
  • Small runs, many styles: pet apparel launches by season, holiday and collaboration, and single-style volumes stay modest. Calculate trims MOQ per style; quoting like a bulk apparel order ties up stock

2. Neck Labels and Care Labels: What Pet Apparel Should State

The neck label carries brand recognition; the care label decides whether a return turns into an argument. On pet apparel, the care label usually adds a usage line beyond fibre content, wash symbols and country of origin — for example pet use only, or wear under supervision. Those lines protect the brand, and printing them costs far less than explaining afterwards.

  • Keep the neck label thin: a satin or plain-weave woven label in a thin, soft construction reduces friction against fur. Close-fitting vests can use a tagless printed label, but wash-test it first
  • Four items on the care label: fibre content, wash symbols, country of origin and a usage note. European and US pet brands often add warnings such as keep away from fire or do not tumble dry, in the languages of the selling market
  • Print method follows wash frequency: readability depends on repeated laundering, so specify a construction that survives roughly 20 washes. Matte coated tape outlasts standard coating

3. Size Labels and Hang Tags: From XS to XXL

Pet apparel sizing cannot stop at S, M and L. Add the body measurements each size covers and say where to measure. Putting the size chart on the back of the hang tag makes it likelier to be kept — and easy to reuse in the e-commerce listing.

Size namingLetter size plus chest girth and back length range, e.g. M (chest 40–46 cm / back 30 cm)

4. Safety and Restricted Substances: Three Owner Concerns

  • Chewing and scratching: small fittings must survive pulling, metal parts need deburred edges and coatings that do not flake, and thread ends must be trimmed so nothing becomes a swallowable piece
  • Skin contact: pet skin is more sensitive than human skin, so labels touching the body should follow ecological textile requirements, avoiding heavy metals, azo dyes and phthalates; for the EU also watch nickel release limits
  • Night visibility: raincoats and autumn and winter outdoor styles often carry reflective tape or printed reflective marks. Confirm reflectivity and how much survives washing rather than judging from the sample day

5. Packing and Cross-Border Shipping: From Hang Card to Zip Bag

  • Pet store hanging: retailers display on peg hooks or hang cards, so packaging needs a hang hole and the tag front should face out. Keeping card and tag in the same paper family reads more coherently
  • E-commerce zip bags: a resealable bag makes try-on and returns easier and can carry micro-perforations to reduce trapped odour. Reserve space on the bag for barcode and size labels so stickers do not cover brand information
  • Cross-border requirements: the EU and US markets generally require importer or responsible party details, and pet products are often asked to state the applicable body size and safety warnings. Agree the label wording for the destination before ordering

7. Sampling and Acceptance: Five Checks

  1. Fit and weigh: test on a real dog or cat to confirm the size and care labels do not rub or restrict movement
  2. Wash test: run at least five simulated washes and watch care label legibility, reflective brightness and seam fraying
  3. Pull and chew simulation: pull-test cord, plastic fastener and reflective tape so nothing detaches under load
  4. Colour and copy check: verify neck label colour against Pantone or the approved swatch and proofread composition and warnings word by word
  5. Packing and barcode: confirm hang hole position, zip bag fit and barcode scan rate before releasing bulk
💡 Tip: pet apparel reorders follow the holiday calendar. Confirm trims for Halloween, Thanksgiving and Christmas styles six to eight weeks ahead, and keep tags, neck labels, care labels and packaging on one purchase order to save a round of colour and copy approvals.
