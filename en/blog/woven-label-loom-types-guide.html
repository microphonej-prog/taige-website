Woven Label Looms: How Needle and Shuttle Looms Change Detail, Cost and MOQ

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 15, 2026

A quote that says only 'woven label, 30 × 50 mm, two colours' hides the variable that decides whether the artwork survives: which loom weaves it. A needle loom and a shuttle loom produce different products in every way that matters — minimum line width, selvage, MOQ and unit price. The same artwork that blurs on a needle loom holds a 0.25 mm hairline on a shuttle loom.

1. Two loom families, two products

A needle loom drives the warp from a needle bed and weaves 2 to 12 narrow tapes in one pass, usually 10–200 mm wide: fast, cheap per piece, and right for artwork that is settled — main labels, size tabs, care labels. A shuttle loom weaves a single tape with a true woven selvage on both edges: denser, finer, and the normal choice for the face label on a high-price brand. The test is simple: measure the finest line in the artwork. Under 0.3 mm on a needle loom quote, you will lose it.

2. The detail floor the loom sets

The detail floor is three numbers: minimum line width 0.25–0.3 mm, minimum cap height 1.2–1.5 mm, and warp/weft density in threads per cm. Below that height CJK characters collapse into blocks, with kana suffering worst — which is why we ask for artwork printed at 1:1 on paper instead of judged on a zoomed screen. Low density carries a hidden cost too: dark fine text on a white ground lets the ground yarn show through and reads as grey.

Item Needle loom Shuttle loom
Minimum line width about 0.3 mm about 0.25 mm
Selvage Cut afterwards: hot or ultrasonic True woven selvage on both edges
Width range 10–200 mm usually 10–50 mm
MOQ and style change Tapes combined: 1,000–2,000 per style Stop and re-thread: usually from 3,000
Relative unit price Baseline about 1.5–2×
Best for Size tabs, care labels, volume main labels Face labels, high-price brands

3. Cost and MOQ: where the loom shows up on the invoice

One needle loom run carries several tapes of different widths, so three sizes at 1,000 pieces each can be scheduled together and MOQ falls to 1,000–2,000 per style. A shuttle loom runs one tape and any change means stopping to re-thread, so MOQ usually starts at 3,000 and unit price runs 1.5–2× higher. Practical advice: face label on the shuttle loom, size tabs and care labels on the needle loom, quoted separately — that moves the number far more than one round of price pressure.

Our acceptance line: a 1:1 woven sample against the artwork, and any broken hairline, insufficient character height or frayed selvage means a re-sample, not a production run. Written into the spec sheet, that line costs far less than repairing artwork later.

4. On top of that, the label carries legal text

A woven label is not just cloth. Japan's Household Goods Quality Labelling Act requires fibre composition (混用率), care instructions and the labeller's name; Korea's Quality Management and Industrial Products Safety Control Act requires Korean-language marking and country of origin; in the EU, Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 covers fibre composition while GPSR (EU) 2023/988 requires a traceable responsible party. Fitting that text into a 20 × 50 mm label is a calculation you do before choosing the loom: dense small type argues for the shuttle loom.

5. Five things to settle before the sample is approved

  1. Print the artwork at 1:1 on paper and judge hairlines and type height there, not on a zoomed screen.
  2. Put the minimum line width and minimum cap height in the spec sheet, not in a verbal agreement.
  3. Decide the edge first: woven selvage, hot cut and ultrasonic cut look and feel different.
  4. Combine sizes and colours on one run; the same spec across styles is what brings MOQ down.
  5. Match the legal character count to the label area before you discuss layout, or you will discover the problem after sampling.

Choosing a loom is a trade: the shuttle loom buys detail and a woven selvage, the needle loom buys speed and a low MOQ. Put that trade in writing on the quote and the spec sheet and half the arguments disappear — especially the classic 'the sample was beautiful, the bulk went chunky', which nine times out of ten means the loom changed and nobody re-sampled.
