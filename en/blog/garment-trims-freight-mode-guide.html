Shipping Trims: Choosing Between Courier, Air, LCL and FCL

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 28, 2026

Trims are small and rarely high in value, yet freight cost and transit time decide whether a garment factory catches its vessel. The same batch of hang tags and woven labels arrives in three days by courier, in about a week by air, or in three to five weeks by LCL sea freight - at several times the cost in the other direction. Choose wrong and the money saved on freight is usually lost on the delivery date. This guide compares courier, air, LCL and FCL in one table, then works through volumetric weight, Incoterms, consolidating with the garment shipment, and the documents and mistakes that cause delays.

1. Four Freight Modes in One Table

Mode Typical transit Typical scale Cost profile Usual pitfall
International courier 3-7 days, door Up to tens of kg Highest rate, least work Oversize or overweight surcharges
Air freight 7-12 days to airport or door 100 kg to 1 tonne Several times sea rates Unclear destination charges and customs file
LCL sea freight 3-5 weeks 1-2 to about 15 CBM Charged by volume, low rate Destination charges exceed expectations
FCL sea freight 3-5 weeks, close to LCL Over about 15 CBM, or with the garments Lowest unit cost Poor fill wastes paid space

2. Work Out Volumetric Weight First

Most trims ship as volumetric cargo: hang tags are stacks of paper, woven and care labels are rolls of tape, poly bags are largely air. Courier and air charge whichever is greater, actual or volumetric weight, using length x width x height in centimetres divided by 5000, with 6000 on some routes; LCL is charged straight by cubic metre. A carton of hang tags weighing 15 kg at 0.12 CBM becomes 24 kg volumetric - which is why compressing the packing saves money directly.

  • Ask up front whether weight or volume applies and which divisor
  • Rolls beat flat packing on space, slightly less handy to pick
  • Expel air from bags before cartoning to cut volume
  • The larger the shipment, the stronger the sea cost advantage

3. EXW, FOB, CIF, DDP: Where They Differ

The Incoterm decides who books, who pays the main freight and when risk passes. These four cover most trims orders; check first that the destination clears smoothly, then agree who carries that leg.

Term Booking and main freight Risk passes What it means for trims
EXW ex works Buyer On delivery at the factory Buyer needs its own forwarder; domestic pickup can stall
FOB free on board Seller books, buyer pays the freight When goods are on board The most common for both LCL and FCL; export clearance is the seller's
CIF cost, insurance and freight Seller When goods are on board Seller pays freight and insurance to destination; local charges usually remain with the buyer
DDP delivered duty paid Seller On delivery at destination Easiest for the buyer, hardest on the seller's duty and compliance knowledge

4. Consolidating with the Garment Shipment

  • Pack to the garment factory's carton spec and marks, matching carton numbers to the packing list
  • If trims take a corner of the container, brace them to avoid crushing
  • Work backwards: trims must reach the warehouse days before loading

The real risk in consolidation is timing: the container arrives and the trims are still in transit. Build buffer into production, inspection and inland haulage, and confirm warehouse receiving hours and holidays in advance.

5. Shipping Documents and Handover

  • Invoice and packing list must match the goods: description, material, quantity, carton weight and volume
  • Origin and required statements per destination, never hand-written onto tags or cartons at the last minute
  • For air or courier, name the arrival notice and customs contacts in advance
  • Send carton numbers, gross and net weight and the bill number in one go

6. Five Mistakes Worth Avoiding

  • Under-declaring value to save duty: a customs check costs far more than the duty
  • Comparing only the main freight and ignoring destination and delivery charges
  • Peak-season space: prices and transit jump before holidays and year-end
  • Samples inside the bulk carton: a sample inspection holds the whole container
  • Courier channels restrict liquids or items with batteries; declare them early
💡 A practical habit: describe the shipment once - modest value, bulky, tight deadline - and ask for courier, air and sea options with arrival dates in the quotation. Then work back from the garment factory's loading date. You are comparing total cost plus risk, not a rate per kilo.
