First Trims Order for a New Clothing Brand: Checklist, Quantities & Sampling

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 7, 2026

Trim sourcing is the most underestimated part of launching a clothing brand: the garments are ready, then you find the hang tags have not arrived, the care label text is wrong or the bags are the wrong size — any of these delays the launch. This first-trims checklist starts from three questions — what to buy, how many, and in what order — to get hang tags, woven labels, care labels and bags right the first time.

1. The First-Trims Family: One Table to See It All

TrimPlacement & RoleTypical First Order
Hang tag (main tag)Hung from the neck or side seam; carries brand image, style, price, barcode and marketing infoAt least one per garment; design per style, optionally with a brand-story or QR card
Woven label (main/neck label)Sewn inside the neckline, usually with brand name and size — the label that touches the wearerSatin or taffeta; size info can be woven in or made as a separate size label
Care labelSewn in the side seam or hem; carries fiber content, care symbols and origin — compliance infoWoven tape or heat transfer; content checked against destination-country rules and printed complete
Poly bags / paper bagsPer-piece shipping bags or retail carry bags — protection plus imageChoose by channel: clear poly bags with a seal sticker for e-commerce, printed paper bags for retail

2. Fix Your Master Data Before Printing Anything

The brand name, style codes, sizes, fiber content, care symbols and origin printed on hang tags, woven labels and care labels must come from one master file. Build a single sheet first: brand name (including case and ® usage), style-code rules, size chart, composition per style, care wording and origin line. Lock it before layout to avoid the classic rework of one spelling on the tag and another on the care label.

3. Sizing Quantities: Trial-Order Math and Typical Starting Points

A practical formula: projected first-run pieces × (1 + 3–5% allowance) + marketing, replacement and archive quantities. For a first launch, common trial starting points are around 2,000–3,000 hang tags, 1,000+ woven labels and 300–500 care labels — if you are below the MOQ, ask the supplier about combining designs or sharing a layout. And resist the urge to over-order: redesigns, seasons and brand refreshes all obsolete old trims.

4. Sampling in 3–7 Days, Then Seal the Sample Before Bulk

  • Hang tags: check paper, color and finish — thickness and feel, spot-color shade, foil and lamination all get locked here
  • Woven labels: check weave and legibility — satin vs taffeta feel, whether small text weaves cleanly, yarn colors vs the artwork
  • Care labels: check content and wash durability — verify every symbol and word, machine-wash test to confirm no fading or smudging
  • Seal the sample: sign or photograph the approved sample as the reference for bulk matching and acceptance
  • Plan the rhythm: sampling takes 3–7 days, bulk typically 10–25 days after approval, plus shipping — work it backward from your launch date

5. One-Stop Supplier or Separate Sourcing?

Separate printers, weavers and label makers may quote lower unit prices in theory, but a first launch is a total-cost game: every extra supplier means another color-matching round, another delivery window, another freight bill and more coordination. Ordering from one factory lets hang tags and care labels share one sampling round, one color system and easier scheduling.

6. A Six-Step Action List for New Brands

  • ① Build the master sheet: brand name, style-code rules, size chart, composition and care wording finalized once
  • ② Fix the product list and quantities: apply the formula to your first-run pieces per style
  • ③ Inquire and sample: send the list to suppliers and confirm samples within 3–7 days
  • ④ Seal the sample and order: sign and archive it; the PO spells out specs, color numbers and acceptance terms
  • ⑤ Bulk and inspection: track production milestones across the 10–25 day run and check against the sealed sample before shipment
  • ⑥ Review and reorder: track actual usage per style so your second order is data-driven and sharper

Trims are a small share of a garment's cost, yet they are the most likely link to stall a launch. Sort out the list, the quantities and the rhythm once, and your first shipment will carry your own tags on time and to spec.
