Clothing Size Stickers: Where They Go, What They Stick To

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 21, 2026

A size sticker is the cheapest piece of information in the whole pack, and the first one a warehouse or a shop floor reads when it picks a garment. It sits on the outside of the poly bag, on the face of the hang tag, or on the folded garment itself, and it does one job: tell someone the size without opening anything. Three decisions make or break it: where it sits, which adhesive holds it, and what is printed on it. Get any of the three wrong and the sticker lifts, drops off in transit, or refuses to peel, and the warehouse ends up opening the bag anyway.

1. Three Positions, Three Different Demands

The bag face is the common one: a 30 × 15 mm strip in the corner of the bag, readable at a glance. It demands an adhesive that grips low-surface-energy film such as PE or OPP and still peels without lifting the print underneath. Second is the hang tag face, bottom right, 40 × 20 mm, usually matching the tag stock so it can carry a brand colour block. Third is the folded garment or a size card, 50 × 25 mm, with room for a conversion table, a barcode and colour codes. One format per shipment, or the warehouse workflow forks and errors follow.

2. Adhesive: The Part That Decides If It Arrives Intact

Paper-faced stickers take an acrylic emulsion and suit paper tags and paper bags. Film-faced stickers in PP or BOPP need a high-tack acrylic to bite into a poly bag at all. Use a removable grade when it has to peel cleanly: low initial tack, slow build. Use hot melt when it has to stay put for good, but note that hot melt loses initial tack quickly below 5 °C, so winter shipments and cold-chain warehouses should switch to a freezer-grade acrylic and keep the application room at 10 °C or above with the surface at 5 °C or above. Store rolls at 15–25 °C and 40–60 % relative humidity; shelf life is typically 12 months, and damp rolls show adhesive transfer, which is the edge lifting you see first.

3. What to Print, and What the Sticker Must Not Pretend to Be

The minimum set is the size itself at 2 mm cap height or more, bold, with clean black-on-white contrast. Add a colour code, a different ground or a small dot per size, and picking gets noticeably faster. If a barcode goes on at all, use the GS1 GTIN with a module of 0.264 mm or larger and 10X quiet zones on both sides, and keep it off the seal line and any fold, because a sticker lifts at exactly those places. Be careful with conversion tables: turning an Asian size straight into a European size promises an equivalence that does not exist. List the regional codes and add a line saying the measured centimetres govern.

One line for the boundary: a size sticker is logistics information, not a durable label. Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 requires the fibre composition to be attached to the garment on a durable label, so when the sticker peels off the composition and care instructions must still be on the garment. The two never substitute for each other.

4. What Each Market Expects on the Label Behind the Sticker

France: the Code de la consommation requires composition and care in French, with the French size scale of 36 to 48 and the comma decimal, for example 0,5 mm. Spain: Real Decreto 928/1987 governs textile labelling, with dress sizes written as talla 38. Japan: the Household Goods Quality Labelling Act requires composition by blend ratio, care instructions and country of origin in Japanese, with sizes under JIS L 0001 given in cm and in go numbers such as 9号 or 11号. Korea: the Quality Management and Safety Control of Industrial Products Act requires blend ratio, care marking and origin, with sizes under KS K 0050 as hose numbers 55, 66, 77 plus centimetres. Taiwan: the Commodity Labelling Act requires composition, washing method, origin and size in Chinese. Selling one garment into four markets means one multilingual durable label plus one local sticker.

5. Applying and Inspecting: Six Numbers for the Spec

Hand application suits small runs and mixed-size cartons; an automatic label applicator suits a single format at volume. The inspection numbers are the same either way: position within plus or minus 2 mm, no bubble over 1 mm across, 180 degree peel of at least 2 N per 25 mm after 24 hours, no ink lift under a 3M tape test, no edge lift after 72 hours at 40 °C and 90 % relative humidity, and a 12-month shelf life on the roll. Measure one more thing: whether the bag print survives the peel. That is the check everyone forgets and the one that comes back as a customer claim.

Put those six numbers in the purchase order and the sticker stops being a supporting actor and becomes an inspectable material. The saving does not come from shaving the unit price. It comes from a warehouse that does not have to open bags, a service desk that does not answer which size questions, and a return rate that stops being pushed up by size confusion. Thirty millimetres of paper sets the tempo of the whole dispatch line.
