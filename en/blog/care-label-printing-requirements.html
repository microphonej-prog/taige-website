Care Label Printing Requirements: Keeping Small Text Legible

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated August 20, 2026

The care label is the most information-dense, and most overlooked, small label on a garment. A tiny strip must carry fiber content, washing symbols, origin and size — and stay legible after dozens of washes. The fonts, colors and printing methods you choose directly determine how readable and durable it is. This guide covers every requirement.

For exported garments, care label printing is not just about looks — it is about compliance: both the US FTC and EU regulations require care information to be clear and durable.

1. Why Legibility Matters

Consumers follow the washing symbols, and inspectors and customs check the label content. A care label that is blurred, faded or rubbed off is as good as no label at all — at best it hurts the repurchase experience, at worst it gets a whole shipment rejected.

  • Compliance — the FTC requires care instructions to be clearly visible and permanently attached
  • Customer experience — illegible washing symbols are a frequent cause of returns and complaints
  • Brand image — a crisp label reflects the quality control level of the manufacturer

2. Fonts & Sizes

As an industry rule of thumb, minimum text height on care labels is around 1.5-2mm, preferably in a sans-serif font with even stroke weight. No country sets a single mandatory size, but the universal baseline is that an average consumer can read it easily.

FactorRecommendation
FontSans-serif fonts (e.g. Arial, Helvetica) with even stroke weight
Minimum sizeCharacter height around 1.5-2mm, still readable after scaling
Line spacingAt least 120% of character height to avoid lines touching
WeightAvoid hairline weights; use bolder type on dark materials

3. Color Contrast & Rubbing Resistance

Dark text on a white base offers the highest contrast and wears best. Dark garments often use light text on a dark base (e.g. white on black), where the rubbing resistance of the ink becomes critical.

  • Prefer dark text on white (black or dark grey) — maximum contrast and most consistent cost
  • Light-on-dark printing needs rub-resistant inks to avoid fading and blurring after wear and washing
  • Transfer labels' wash fastness depends on ribbon and film quality — always run a wash test

4. Printing Methods Compared

MethodLegibilityDurabilityBest For
Woven tapeWoven directly, crisp and stable charactersWash and abrasion resistantRegular garments
CoatedSmooth surface, fine print possibleScratch resistant, but may crack when folded long-termFast fashion, high volume
TransferHigh definition, rich colorWash fast, but sensitive to hot ironingPremium lines, multi-color designs

5. Compliance Content Checklist

  • Fiber content (listed in descending order of percentage)
  • Washing symbols (ISO 3758) with any necessary wording
  • Country of origin (e.g. Made in China)
  • Brand / style number and size (as required by the customer)
💡 Tip: At sampling stage, ask the supplier for a standard wash test (e.g. 40°C for 30+ minutes) and confirm the text stays crisp after several washes — it is the most direct way to judge care label printing quality.
