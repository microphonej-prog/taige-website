Care Label Attachment Guide: Placement, Sewing Methods & Heat Transfer

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 1, 2026

Where and how your care label is attached directly affects compliance and wearing comfort. A bad position can irritate the skin, a bad stitch can fray or curl; for exported garments, care label placement is also governed by target-market regulations. This guide covers the common attachment methods, sewing positions and key considerations.

There is no single answer for care label attachment — it depends on the fabric, category, amount of content and compliance requirements. Start with the two main directions: sewing and heat transfer.

1. Attachment Methods at a Glance: Sewing & Heat Transfer

  • Sewn care labels — the most common option, suitable for cotton, linen, denim and other sewable fabrics; strong and removable
  • Heat transfer care labels — printed on a heat-seal film and pressed on, ideal for next-to-skin or seamless styles like underwear and sportswear
  • Woven ribbon care labels — durable and foldable into multiple layers, ideal for export orders with lots of content
  • Printed care labels — thin and soft, suitable for categories with high comfort requirements
MethodProsLimitsBest For
SewnStrong, removable, versatileNeeds a sewing stepShirts, pants, outerwear

2. Common Sewing Positions

PositionNotesCommon Categories
Side seamIn one side seam, hidden and skin-friendlyT-shirts, hoodies, dresses
Back neckVisible and easy to find; mind neck comfortOuterwear, coats, shirts
Inside waistbandStandard position for pants and skirtsPants, skirts
Cuff / hemUsed for special categoriesKnitwear, loungewear

3. Placement Advice by Garment Category

  • T-shirts / hoodies — left side seam, folded edge inward and label face outward for easy reading
  • Pants / jeans — inside the waistband, away from the high-friction center back area
  • Underwear — prefer heat transfer or seamless side-seam labels, avoiding any hard edges
  • Kids' wear — consider scratch-free labels or printed labels, placed away from the back of the neck

4. Sewing Methods & Durability

  • Flat seam — the label is sewn directly into the side seam; simple and low cost
  • Overlock (merrow) — the label and fabric are overlocked together for a cleaner edge
  • Folded seam — the label is folded and sewn in, leaving a flat face with no raw edges
  • The label itself must withstand washing and heat, but thread strength matters just as much; multi-layer folded labels wear most at the creases

5. Export Compliance & Readability

US FTC and EU regulations both require care label content to be clear, durable and easy for consumers to find. When content is extensive, multi-layer folded care labels work well, but the attachment position must keep the label fully readable once unfolded.

💡 Tip: confirm care label placement with your client in writing before bulk production for export orders; test-sew during sampling to verify flatness and readability.
