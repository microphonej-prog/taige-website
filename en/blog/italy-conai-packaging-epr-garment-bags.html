In Italy a Garment Bag Is a Line Item Measured in Grams

By TAGE Packaging · Updated November 27, 2026

Ship apparel into Italy and the bag around the shirt is not free. Italy runs one of Europe's oldest packaging extended producer responsibility systems: whoever places the packaging on the Italian market pays an environmental contribution to CONAI, and since 1 January 2023 packaging sold to a consumer must also carry a material code and Italian-language disposal instructions. For a brand that is a cost line and a return form. For a trims supplier it is a data request that arrives with the first purchase order: material, thickness, weight per unit, recycled content, and the evidence behind each number.

1. Who Pays: CONAI and Its Six Consortia

CONAI is the private consortium of Italian packaging producers, and it works through six material consortia: steel (Ricrea), aluminium (CIAL), paper (Comieco), wood (Rilegno), plastic (Corepla) and glass (Coreve). The environmental contribution, Contributo Ambientale CONAI or CAC, is due when packaging is first placed on the Italian market. In practice the Italian importer or the brand pays, not the factory in Dongguan; but the rate depends on three numbers we supply: material, grammage, and weight per unit.

  • Flat garment bag in LDPE film: Corepla, plastic stream, charged by bag weight in grams rather than by area
  • Paper shopping bag, hang tag, paper card: Comieco, paper stream; if a hang tag carries a cotton cord, the cord is a separate component
  • Woven labels, care labels, ribbon: textile, so they stay out of packaging EPR tonnage, but clients routinely list them and the point needs stating in writing
  • PE/PA laminated mailer: multi-material, declared component by component, and at the sorting plant it usually lands in “other”, which is where the real cost sits
Do the arithmetic: a 30 µm, 25×35 cm LDPE garment bag weighs about 5 g. That looks like nothing until you multiply by 200,000 units, which is one tonne — and the CAC is charged by the tonne. Downgauging saves more, faster, than switching material.

2. The Environmental Label Is Mandatory, Not a Bonus

The legal basis is Article 219 of Legislative Decree 152/2006, Part IV, as amended by Legislative Decree 116/2020. Since 1 January 2023, packaging intended for the consumer must carry a material code and disposal instructions in Italian. The codes follow the alphanumeric system of Decision 97/129/EC: LDPE 4, PP 5, PAP 21 or 22. The wording has to be Italian: “Raccolta plastica”, “Raccolta carta”. The obligation sits with the producer placing the packaging on the market — in practice the brand or the Italian importer — and the penalties follow the same party.

Multi-material packaging has to be labelled component by component. A PE-coated paper bag is not a paper bag; it is paper plus PE, and both layers need a code. A hang tag with a cotton cord is paper plus a textile component. This is the most commonly skipped clause and the easiest thing for a customs officer or a client auditor to spot.

3. What the Declaration Needs, Row by Row

Item we make What to declare Unit
Flat garment bag, LDPE Code LDPE 4, Corepla class, net weight excluding the carton grams per bag
Paper shopping bag Code PAP 22, Comieco, grammage, plus the CoC certificate number if FSC is claimed g/m² and grams per bag
Hang tag with cord PAP 21 for the card, the cotton cord as a separate component, with cord length in the spec g/m², cord length in cm
Mono-material PE mailer Mono-material statement and recycled content percentage, backed by EN 15343 or an equivalent verification grams per unit

4. How PPWR Changes the Timeline

Regulation (EU) 2025/40 on packaging and packaging waste applies from 12 August 2026 and replaces the 1994 directive 94/62/EC with a single regulation. It does two things: it sets recyclability performance grades, with A, B and C bands phasing in from 2030, and it introduces one EU-wide label. Until that harmonised label replaces the national rules, Article 219 of D.Lgs. 152/2006 stays in force. For the 2026–2028 transition, a bag going to Italy has to satisfy today's Italian label and be designed for PPWR recyclability at the same time.

5. What We Hand Over With Every Shipment

  • Weight declaration: grams per unit by SKU, including handle, zip and liner, excluding the outer carton
  • Material composition: percentage by layer, and a plain statement of mono-material or laminate
  • Recycled content certificate: third-party verification only, EN 15343 or equivalent. A supplier letter is not evidence
  • Label placement: marked on the die-line so the label does not sit over a barcode or a seam
  • For paper items, the FSC chain-of-custody certificate plus the invoice reference, so the two can be matched

On cost, printing is not the lever. Three things are: film thickness and weight per unit, which multiply straight into the CAC; whether the structure is laminated, since a laminate has to be declared by component and falls out of the main recycling stream; and recycled content, where rPE pricing follows supply and the verification itself costs money. Itemise those three on the quotation and a client's compliance team can see where the money goes.

6. Three Mistakes That Cost Real Money

  • Printing the label on the outer carton only: the duty attaches to the packaging itself, and the carton is not a substitute
  • One label artwork for France and Italy: Triman is a French requirement, Italy wants a material code plus Italian wording, and one panel cannot satisfy both
  • Declaring the flat bag weight and forgetting the handle, zip or liner: all of it is packaging, and under-declaring leaves a hole in the client's return
One line to keep: in Italy the bag is a material billed by the gram. Thickness, structure and recycled content decide the client's total cost and compliance risk more than the print does.
