UK Clothing Label Compliance: How to Label After Brexit

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 15, 2026

UK clothing label compliance has diverged from EU rules. After Brexit, garments placed on the market in England, Scotland and Wales (GB) still need fibre composition, care instructions and country of origin — but the responsible party, address details and chemical regulations are now governed by UK law. Reusing an old EU label template is a fast route to customs holds, retailer audits or platform compliance checks.

This guide breaks down the mandatory information, care symbols and safety requirements for the UK market, and ends with a labelling checklist you can hand straight to your factory.

1. Four Changes to UK Clothing Labels After Brexit

  • Framework retained, but separate — GB kept the textile fibre composition labelling framework, so the core requirements largely carry over, yet amendments are now made in UK law and will gradually diverge.
  • Responsible party changed — GB requires the name and address of a UK-based manufacturer, importer or authorised representative. An EU-only address no longer satisfies this.
  • EU-only marks no longer apply — the EU flag and EU Ecolabel are not valid marks for the GB market; alternative marks are managed under separate schemes.
  • Northern Ireland is a special case — EU rules still apply there, so goods moving to NI via GB often need to satisfy both sets of rules; leave room for dual labelling.

3. Fine Print on Fibre Composition

UK composition labelling follows the same logic as the EU: descending content, statutory fibre names, accurate percentages. Manufacturing tolerance between tested and declared values depends on the fibre and test method (typically around 3%), so do not treat tolerance as licence to round numbers freely.

Fibres that are easy to label wrongly

  • Wool — blends must show each percentage; never present a blend as pure wool.
  • Elastane — keep the name consistent across styles instead of mixing several trade names.
  • Other fibres — when minor fibres together make up a small share, the permitted format allows grouping them, but the scope must be stated.
  • Recycled fibres — stating recycled content and its percentage satisfies labelling rules and answers UK retailers' sustainable sourcing briefs.

4. Care Symbols and How to Build the Care Label

The UK widely follows the international (GINETEX) care symbol system, with five groups in fixed order: wash, bleach, tumble dry, iron, professional care. A common care label pairs symbols with English text — symbols for fast reading, text to prevent misreading.

  • Match the label material to the wash method: for frequent machine washing, use woven or coated nylon labels so the print does not flake off.
  • Wash durability is verified by lab testing; mainstream apparel usually targets 3 to 5 home launderings without detachment or fading.
  • Multilingual versions (English, French, German, Spanish) are common for brands selling into both the UK and the EU — allow extra line spacing.

5. Chemicals and Product Safety: UK-Specific Rules

  • UK REACH — now separate from EU REACH, with registration and restriction lists gradually diverging. Check azo dyes, formaldehyde and heavy metals against the UK list.
  • Childrenswear safety — cords and drawstrings plus small-part pull strength are tightly specified; UK kidswear tags and attachment methods should be simplified accordingly.
  • Nightwear — flammability performance is regulated separately, and a visible safety warning is often needed on the tag.
  • Packaging and plastics — packaging waste responsibility (EPR) and the Plastic Packaging Tax affect declarations for plastic packaging; confirm recycled content in mailers and poly bags with your supplier early.

6. Trim Ordering Checklist for the UK

Build a three-piece set: main label (brand identity) + care label (composition, care symbols, origin, responsible party) + hang tag (size, price, barcode, compliance note). Confirming each item before ordering avoids a full reprint.

  1. Confirm whether the target is GB or GB plus Northern Ireland — this decides whether you need dual labelling.
  2. Provide the responsible party's English name and UK address text — we set it exactly as supplied, we do not fill it in for you.
  3. Take composition and care symbols from the customer's test report rather than inferring them.
  4. Agree sampling and bulk timing: sampling 3 to 7 days, bulk 10 to 25 days, with earlier slots in peak season.
💡 Tip: UK labelling responsibility sits with the business placing goods on the market, not with the printer. We typeset what you supply for composition, responsible party and symbols. For flammability or childrenswear categories, obtain third-party test reports before approving artwork to avoid scrapping a whole label run.
