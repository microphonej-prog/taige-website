Woven Label MOQ & Cost Explained: Every Yarn Counts

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 9, 2026

Why is the woven label MOQ so high, and why do prices vary so much? These are among the most common questions brands ask when requesting quotes. A woven label is built yarn by yarn on a loom, not printed — so its MOQ and cost logic differ completely from hang tags or care labels. This article breaks down the price structure, explains where MOQs come from, lists five parameters that drive unit price, and gives cost-saving strategies you can actually use.

To understand woven label cost, accept one premise first: the bulk of the unit price is not the material itself but machine time and set-up. A 2 cm neck label and a 5 cm side label consume similar yarn, yet loom occupancy and finishing can differ several-fold — the closer your spec is to a factory standard, the friendlier the price.

1. What Makes Up a Woven Label Price

A woven label quote normally covers four cost blocks — know where your money goes before negotiating: yarn, the base material that decides colour and hand-feel; weaving, charged by loom time, slower for complex constructions; finishing such as folding, edge finishing and stiffening; and indirect costs like packing and quality inspection.

  • Yarn: polyester is the default; cotton, nylon, viscose and metallic yarns each cost more, and many colours or batches inflate yarn stock
  • Loom time: satin and plain weave fast; large jacquard areas and high density weave slowly — machine time is the biggest cost variable
  • Finishing: folding, edge finishing, stiffening and cutting methods each add their own labour and waste
  • Packing and inspection: piece-by-piece checking and specified packing (bundling, bagging, labelling) are all priced in

2. Why MOQs Exist

A woven label MOQ is not picked out of thin air. Every new design and yarn lot requires warping, heddle threading and machine tuning — this set-up time and small-batch waste is fixed, so smaller orders spread it over fewer labels. Looms also have a standard width: one label uses only a strip of it, and the rest must be filled by weaving several labels side by side. Too few pieces make that width uneconomical, and factories naturally decline.

💡 TAGE's standard woven label MOQ starts at 1,000 pcs, and lower for simple specs such as satin neck labels; splitting trial and bulk orders, or re-ordering the same design, lowers the first-order bar — final terms depend on your spec and process.

3. Five Parameters That Drive Unit Price

ParameterWhy it affects priceCost-saving direction
Width and lengthDrives yarn per label and rows per loom width; oversized specs waste widthStick to standard widths such as the 10–20 mm neck-label range
Weave complexitySatin and plain weave fast; full-surface jacquard and high density slowReserve jacquard for the logo; use satin or plain for the background
Colour countEach colour is a yarn set; colour changes and yarn stock cost moneyKeep it to 2–4 colours per label — cleaner and cheaper
Finishing requirementsFolding, edge finishing, stiffening — every step adds labour and wasteSimplify where possible, e.g. standard folding over special edge finishing
Order quantityFixed set-up costs spread over volume; doubling quantity rarely doubles priceCombine styles into one order or buy a full season's volume — avoid frequent tiny reorders

4. Six Cost-Saving Ordering Habits

  • Standardise specs: fix width and folding as brand standards — fewer specials means lower cost
  • Shrink jacquard areas: let the logo shine in jacquard, give large backgrounds to satin or plain
  • Cut colours: keep backgrounds and text in the same family sharing yarn lots — fewer changes, fewer stops
  • Combine orders: producing several styles in one run spreads set-up cost better than many tiny orders
  • Re-order approved designs: repeat a sealed original and skip re-sampling and revision risk
  • Get artwork right the first time: vector files, bleed and Pantone references complete — no hidden cost of revision loops

5. What to Send When Asking for a Quote

The accuracy of a quote depends on the completeness of your brief. Send the full list below and you can usually get a solid woven label quote the same day:

  • Finished size and fold: width × length, neck/side/waist position, folded or flat
  • Material and weave: polyester, cotton or blend; satin, plain or mainly jacquard
  • Colours: Pantone references or physical swatches, background and text separated
  • Quantities and rhythm: trial volume, bulk volume, and roughly how often you reorder
  • End use: hot washing, stone washing or other harsh processes — it affects yarn and finishing choices
