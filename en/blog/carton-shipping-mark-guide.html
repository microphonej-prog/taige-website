Carton Shipping Marks for Garment Export: Main Mark, Side Mark and Labels

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 19, 2026

The carton shipping mark is the identity card of an export carton: warehouses count by it, forwarders split by it, customs and the consignee check against it. In practice, wrong shipping marks and carton labels scrap whole carton runs, trigger warehouse rejections or force repacking at destination far more often than trims quality disputes do. This guide covers the main mark, side mark, carton size, barcodes and marking rules, plus six details to confirm before the order is placed.

1. Three Marks: Main Mark, Side Mark and Additional Marks

Carton markings work in three layers. The main mark goes on the largest face (usually the long side) and answers whose goods these are and which order they belong to; the side mark goes on the narrow face, repeating only the order number, style and carton number; the additional marks carry country of origin, gross and net weight, carton dimensions, destination port and handling symbols such as keep dry, this way up and fragile. Exporters who only print the main mark often find warehouse staff unable to route the cartons to the right order.

2. Main Mark: Required Fields and How to Write Them

Field Purpose How to write it
Consignee or brand mark Fast sorting in the warehouse Use the buyer's abbreviation or code; never substitute the full company name
PO or order number Line-by-line check against the order Copy it exactly as the buyer writes it, without prefixes or your own numbering
Style, colour and size Split by style, colour and size to avoid mixed cartons One style and colour per line; write size ranges in a readable form such as S-XL
Carton number Counting and short-shipment checks Use the 1/24, 2/24 format, with the total confirmed by the buyer
Destination port Routing and customs identification City name or port code; multi-destination orders must be distinguished
Country of origin A legal requirement in most markets Made in China or Made in P.R.C., legible and not removable

3. Side Mark and Carton Specs: Weigh It, Do Not Estimate

The side mark repeats the order and carton numbers and adds gross weight, net weight, carton dimensions (length x width x height, usually in cm) and units per carton. These numbers must be measured: gross weight after the finished goods are packed, including inserts, poly bags and tags, and dimensions measured on the closed outer carton rather than the board specification from the carton supplier. Buyers use these two figures for container and pallet planning, so an error changes both the loading plan and the freight cost.

4. Direct Print or Labels?

Method Best for Advantage Watch out for
Direct print at the carton factory Large runs, fixed fields, simple colours Low unit cost, cannot fall off, consistent look Artwork must be final early; a change means reprinting cartons
Self-adhesive carton labels Small runs, many SKUs, or late information Flexible, blank cartons can be ordered first, controlled cost Check adhesive residue and lifting in cold conditions
Hand writing Emergency single-carton use only Fastest in the moment Illegible writing is often rejected at the warehouse, so avoid it in bulk

5. Legibility and Position: Four Rules to Avoid Rejection

  • Type height: no less than 10 mm on standard cartons, 15-20 mm when the carton exceeds 40 cm; warehouse staff read from forklift distance.
  • Contrast: on dark cartons do not print dark text on the board; use white labels with black text or reversed-out large type.
  • Barcodes: place them on a flat area that tape will not cover, leave a quiet zone around the code and avoid corners; one primary barcode per label.
  • Do not cover the buyer's labels: if the carton already carries the buyer's logistics label, offset ours so nothing is hidden.

6. Additional Marks Buyers Commonly Require

  • Country of origin: markets such as the US require a legible, permanent and non-removable mark, usually on both the article and the outer packaging; missing it can mean re-marking or return.
  • Handling symbols: use the common ISO 780 pictograms for this way up, keep dry, fragile, stacking limits and temperature range; symbols travel better than text.
  • Retailer logistics rules: some EU and US retailers require SSCC-18 logistics labels matched to EDI data; the buyer supplies the format and we print and verify to that artwork.
  • Packaging sustainability marks: with EU packaging and packaging waste rules tightening, material and recycling marks on the carton matter more; keep the board grade and recycling symbol clear and avoid full-surface lamination that complicates recyclability.

7. Six Carton Marking Details to Confirm Before Ordering

  1. Main mark fields and layout: the field list, line order, and whether the buyer logo or a Pantone colour must be used.
  2. Carton spec and load: outer dimensions, units per carton, gross and net weight, and whether double-wall board or palletising is required.
  3. Print or label: direct print for large fixed runs, blank cartons plus labels for many SKUs and frequent changes.
  4. Barcode type and size: plain linear, Code 128 or SSCC-18, with size and quiet zone per the buyer's standard.
  5. Language and compliance wording: whether a language other than English is needed, and which form of origin wording applies.
  6. Approval route: sign off every carton marking artwork as a PDF; nothing goes into mass production without written approval.
💡 Tip: changing a carton mark costs more than the printing: reprinting cartons, repacking and amending customs paperwork all follow. The cheapest route is to print the artwork 1:1 at sampling, stick it on a real carton, photograph it for the buyer and lock the fields, type size and barcode position in one round. This article is practical guidance; destination regulations and the buyer's technical file prevail.
