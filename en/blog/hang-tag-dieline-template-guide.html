Hang Tag Dielines: Build a Template That Cuts Right the First Time

By TAIGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 11, 2026

Every hang tag that arrives with a ragged edge or a hole punched 2 mm off centre traces back to the same place: a dieline nobody ever drew. A hang tag dieline is the one-to-one drawing that tells the die cutter where to cut, where to crease and where to punch. It is the only file in the job that every other step depends on. This guide covers the five measurements on every hang tag template we issue, the standard sizes worth reusing, and the artwork mistakes that turn a seven-day job into a fourteen-day reprint.

A template without die lines is not a dieline

Most hang tag templates you can download are a coloured rectangle with a size caption. That is a dimension, not a dieline. A usable dieline carries at least three line types, each on its own layer and in its own colour: a cut line (solid, usually a magenta spot), a crease line (dashed) and a hole position (a circle with a stated diameter). Ask for the file with layers intact — a flattened JPG loses exactly the information the die cutter needs.

The five measurements every hang tag template carries

  • Trim size — the finished dimension after cutting, e.g. 50 × 90 mm, not the size before.
  • Bleed — 3 mm on every cut edge. Background colour or image must extend into it, or a 0.5 mm white sliver shows after trimming.
  • Safe area — keep text and logos 3 mm inside the trim line; on textured stock or a soft die edge, 4 mm is safer.
  • Fold line — on a bifold tag the crease sits exactly at the midpoint; full-coverage ink across it will crack on 350 g/m² board.
  • Hole position — centre the hole on the tag's centre line, typically 6 mm from the top edge, 4–5 mm diameter for standard string. Give it as a coordinate; 'near the top' is not a measurement.

Standard sizes worth reusing

Reusing a size the die already exists for removes a tooling charge and one approval round. These four cover most apparel programmes:

Size (mm) Typical use Hole and position Notes
50 × 90 mm Womenswear, mid-market 4 mm, 6 mm from top The default; fits a 3-line brand block plus a barcode
55 × 90 mm Denim, outerwear 5 mm, 6 mm from top Extra width for a size run in one pass
60 × 120 mm Premium lines, gift sets 5 mm, 8 mm from top Room for a GPSR address block without shrinking the logo
40 × 70 mm Kidswear, accessories 3.5 mm, 5 mm from top Any smaller and there is too little stock above the hole

Setting up artwork on top of the dieline

  • Supply vector (AI or PDF) at 1:1, CMYK, with any raster image at 300 dpi equivalent.
  • Outline or embed fonts. A substituted font changes line breaks and pushes copy past the safe margin.
  • Give foil, emboss and spot UV their own 100 % spot-colour layers, named exactly as the finish.
  • Keep the cut line out of the artwork: its own die layer, overprint off.
  • For two-sided tags, mark front and back with registration marks outside the trim, to be cut away.

Where hang tag files usually go wrong

Problem What the printer sees Fix
No bleed A white hairline on all four edges Extend the background 3 mm past the trim
Text outside the safe area First and last letters clipped Move it 3 mm inside, 4 mm on textured stock
Solid ink across the crease Cracking and white showing at the fold Keep solid coverage 1.5 mm clear of the crease, or pre-score
Hole diameter missing The die cutter guesses and the string will not pass State diameter and coordinates
Wrong colour mode A dull, shifted brand colour Convert to CMYK and confirm on a printed proof

Reserving space for mandatory information

Label content is now part of the dieline decision. Under the EU General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR, EU 2023/988) a product placed on the EU market must identify the manufacturer and, where applicable, a responsible person established in the EU, with a postal address. On small trims that information usually rides on the hang tag and needs roughly 35 × 15 mm at 6 pt. If the same tag will carry a QR code for a Digital Product Passport, add 15 × 15 mm plus a 3 mm quiet zone. Deciding this after artwork approval is how a small layout tweak becomes a new die.

How the requirement differs by market

The same dieline has to hold different information in different markets. In the EU, GPSR manufacturer and responsible-person details sit alongside fibre composition under (EU) No 1007/2011. In the US, the FTC textile labelling rules govern. In Japan and Korea, country-of-origin and care marking are mandatory in the local language. Decide the destination markets first, then decide whether the safe area needs 15 mm or 25 mm for text.

Approving the die-cut proof

  • Measure the cut against the trim size with a ruler, not by eye.
  • Check the hole on the actually punched sample, and pass a string through it.
  • Hold the fold to a light: a cracked crease shows as a pale line.
  • Confirm the grammage on the delivery note matches the approved sample (350 g/m², not 300).
  • Keep one signed sample; it is the reference for every repeat order.
