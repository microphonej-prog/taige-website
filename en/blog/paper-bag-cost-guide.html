How Is a Custom Eco Paper Bag Priced? GSM, Finishing and MOQ Explained

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 14, 2026

Two questions come up in every custom eco paper bag project: why does it cost this much, and can it be cheaper? A paper bag is not a single material — it is paper, printing, finishing, handles and bag-making labour stacked together, and changing any one of them rewrites the quote. Understanding the cost build-up is what makes negotiation possible.

This guide gives you a cost build-up table, the six variables that move a quote most, a checklist for reading quotations, and five ways to cut cost without cheapening the bag.

1. What Is Actually Inside a Paper Bag Quote

Paper bag cost splits into five blocks. The proportions shift with the specification, but the logic does not — knowing each block tells you where to negotiate and where not to touch.

Cost blockTypical shareMain variables
Paper materialroughly 30–40%GSM and paper type (white card, art, kraft, recycled, specialty)
Printing10–30%Number of colours, process (offset, flexo, digital) and coverage
Finishing10–20%Lamination, foil stamping, spot UV, embossing, die-cutting
Bag making & labour10–20%Handle type, bag style, size and folding steps
Packing & freightdepends on destinationCarton packing, container fill rate, shipping terms

2. The Six Variables That Move a Quote Most

  • GSM: from 120g to 250g the paper cost steps up and the bag feels stiffer, but folding gets harder and waste rises too;
  • Colours: each extra spot colour means another plate and another pass through the press; four-colour process is flexible but start-up cost is hard to absorb on small runs;
  • Finishing: foil, spot UV and embossing need tooling and extra machine time; more processes also mean a higher reject rate, so the quote climbs;
  • Handles: a die-cut hand hole is the cheapest, paper rope the next step up, then cotton rope, ribbon and non-woven handles — plus knotting or buckle work if required;
  • Style and size: a flat bag is the simplest build; gusseted bags, cardboard inserts and custom die-cutting add significant operations;
  • Quantity: plate-making and set-up are fixed costs, so the more you order the lower the unit price — at trial quantities, fixed costs can be half the quote.

3. Three Budget Tiers and How to Spec Them

Start from the budget tier and then choose paper and finishing — far more efficient than designing first and cutting cost later. These three specs work as a starting point with any supplier:

TierPaper & GSMPrint & finishingHandle
E-commerce shipping bag120–150g kraft or recycled paperOne or two colours, no laminationDie-cut hole
Retail store bag150–180g white cardFour-colour print plus matte laminationPaper rope
Premium gift bag200–250g specialty paper or white cardSpot colours, foil stamping, spot UVCotton rope or non-woven handle plus card insert

4. Six Questions to Ask Before Accepting a Quote

  • Is the GSM and paper type stated, and is lamination included;
  • How many colours, are spot colours used, and do they carry a surcharge;
  • Handle type and load capacity, and whether a load test was done;
  • Are plate-making and sampling charged separately or built into the unit price;
  • Price breaks at different quantities and the minimum order;
  • Lead time, packing method and freight terms (EXW, FOB or DDP).
💡 Tip: comparing unit prices alone is the classic trap. Of two quotes for the same spec and quantity, one including freight and sampling and one excluding it, the unit price gap looks large — yet total cost may reverse the ranking.

5. Five Ways to Cut Cost Without Cheaper Paper Feeling Cheap

  • Standardise the bag: let several lines share one size and style so volumes combine and fixed costs spread further;
  • Trade paper for process: matte lamination, one-colour print and a small foil detail often look more premium than a heavier sheet, for less money;
  • Trim the volume: different folding and carton counts raise container fill rate and cut freight directly;
  • Order early: placing the order before peak season avoids rush charges and express freight, and leaves room for a revision;
  • Simplify the handle: swapping non-woven for paper rope or a die-cut hole changes the look very little but moves cost noticeably.

6. Work With a Bag Supplier Who Can Explain the Cost

The difference in custom bag projects is rarely whether it can be made, but how best to make it. Send the artwork, target quantity and budget range together so the supplier can come back with two or three specs to compare before you lock in paper and finishing.
