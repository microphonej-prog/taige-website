Saree and South Asian Ethnic Wear Trims: Fall, Pico and Blouse Label Sets

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated October 17, 2026

A saree is the archetypal complete garment of South Asia, and a set involves far more than the fabric itself: the fall tape, the pico edge, a full label set for the blouse piece, a hang tag and packaging. Brands and importers working with India, Bangladesh and the Gulf are demanding about fabric yet often stumble on saree trims: fall tape that was never pre-shrunk, a care label with no MRP, a tag pinned straight through the embroidery. This article breaks the set down item by item.

Five parts: the four trims categories a saree set actually needs, the two most neglected operations (fall and pico), the label set for the blouse piece, marking priorities for India and the Gulf, and finally folding, kitting and packaging for the order.

1. The Four Trim Families in a Saree Set

  • Structural trims: the fall tape and the pico thread decide how the hem drapes and whether the raw edge stays neat — invisible, but they move the return rate.
  • Identification: neck label, size label and care label on the blouse piece or finished blouse — fibre content, care, MRP, country of origin — plus the brand hang tag for the saree itself.
  • Attachment: cotton string, elastic loop or plastic fastener. On heavily embroidered sarees avoid metal pins — the hole never closes.
  • Packaging: clear OPP or CPE bag, folding board, belly band or box. One set per bag is the norm in South Asian retail, and the set ratio must not drift.

2. Fall and Pico: Small Operations, Big Return Rates

The fall is a strip of tape sewn inside the hem, usually cotton or poly-cotton twill, roughly 5–8 cm wide. It weights the hem, improves the drape and protects embroidery and zari work from abrasion. Pico is the rolled hem that seals the raw ends of the saree so the yarns cannot unravel. Both are routine work for local tailors in India, but if you export semi-finished goods for local finishing, the contract must state who does it and with which material.

  • Pre-shrinking is non-negotiable: a fall tape that was never pre-shrunk shortens at the first wash or dry clean, puckering and flipping the hem — the single most common complaint. Ask for the shrinkage figure and whether pre-shrinking was done.
  • Colour mismatch: a natural-white fall under a dark saree shows when the wearer moves. Either dye to tone or get explicit sign-off on the contrast. Sew a sample onto the fabric before approving.
  • Match the thread: use polyester 40/2 or finer high-tenacity thread in a tone matched to the ground. Cotton thread fades first on bright grounds; a few washes later the seam reads lighter than the cloth.

3. The Blouse Piece Label Set: Where Inspections Land

The saree itself usually carries no label; identification lives on the blouse piece or finished blouse. Whether the set is complete decides whether the importer can put the goods on the shelf. The table shows the configuration we most often ship to South Asian clients — add or remove to the brand's brief.

4. India, Bangladesh and the Gulf: What Goes on the Tag

  • MRP: Indian retail expects the maximum retail price including tax. Follow the customer's template for format and currency symbol; never convert or add a discounted price yourself.
  • Fibre content and care: fibre names with percentages, care symbols in whatever system the client specifies (ISO symbols are common), and wording in the language of the destination.
  • Country of origin: India, Bangladesh and the Gulf states all require a visible origin mark, consistent across hang tag, care label and carton.
  • Language: the Gulf usually asks for Arabic or bilingual Arabic and English; India works mostly in English, with local languages added at retail in some states. Always confirm against current destination regulations and the client's brief.

5. Folding, Kitting and Packing: One Set per Bag, Right Ratio

A standard saree runs around 5.5 m with roughly 0.8 m of blouse piece in the set; longer 6.3 m versions exist. Folding decides how it looks on the shelf: fold twice along the length, bring the width to about 25–30 cm, insert a folding board and bag it — far crisper than rolling it up by hand.

  • Lock the ratio: one set equals one saree, one blouse piece, one label set, one hang tag, one bag. A missing blouse piece or a tag lost in transit can get the whole carton rejected.
  • Moisture first: monsoon humidity is high. One desiccant sachet inside the clear bag and a moisture-barrier liner in the carton cut down mould complaints on arrival considerably.
  • Tag position: attach on the seam allowance at the inner edge of the pallu or at a dedicated loop, away from embroidery and zari. Never pin straight through the cloth.
💡 Practical tip: South Asian buyers inspect differently from what we instinctively focus on. They care about fall pre-shrinking, pico neatness and MRP plus origin matching in three places — not how bright the foil on the tag is. Send a sample with the fall sewn on and the label set kitted per set; it clears approval far faster than a bag of loose parts.
