Hang Tag Paper Types: Art Paper, Kraft, Cotton & PVC — How to Choose

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated August 16, 2026

Seven-tenths of a hangtag's perceived quality comes from its paper. The first thing a shopper touches is the tag; its thickness, stiffness, gloss and texture all speak for your brand. With art paper, kraft, cotton, PVC and more to choose from, how do you pick the right hang tag paper? This guide breaks down each material's properties, weights and best uses.

1. Define Your Brand's Vibe First

Material is an extension of your brand language: minimal luxury suits thick cotton or textured paper, bright fashion suits high-grammage art paper, natural and eco suits kraft, and waterproof practical uses call for PVC. Set the tone first, then choose the paper — this saves endless revisions and re-sampling.

2. Art Paper: The Mainstream Standard

Art paper is smooth, takes ink evenly and reproduces colors vividly — the most common hangtag material. Standard weights run 250–400gsm: 250–300gsm suits high-volume fast fashion, 300–350gsm balances stiffness and value, and 350gsm+ feels like a card, ideal for mid-to-high-end brands. Matte lamination adds tactility; gloss lamination boosts color and durability.

3. Kraft & Recycled Paper: Natural and Eco

Kraft paper keeps the pulp's natural color with a rugged, organic texture — a classic match with cotton or jute string, ideal for organic cotton, outdoor and 'slow fashion' brands. Recycled paper is made from recovered fibers, slightly darker with a natural grain; paired with soy-based inks and no lamination, it tells a clear eco story. Note that kraft absorbs ink strongly, so dark prints can show mottling — plan your design accordingly.

4. Cotton & Textured Paper: The Premium Choice

Cotton paper contains cotton fiber — soft, warm and breathable to the touch. Paired with foil stamping or embossing, it is the classic choice for luxury brands. Textured paper (linen, canvas or leather embossed) gains a dimensional surface and a premium hand feel. Both cost more than art paper, but deliver the clearest brand premium.

5. PVC & Specialty Materials: Waterproof and Tough

PVC tags are waterproof, fold-resistant and hard to tear — suited to swimwear, outdoor gear, tools and kids' products. They are usually die-cut and printed with lamination, available in clear, frosted or holographic finishes. As environmental rules tighten, more brands switch from PVC to degradable PET or rPET.

There is no single 'best' hang tag paper — only the right one for your brand. Always sample before ordering: print the same design on each candidate paper, feel and inspect them under light, then confirm stiffness, color and print detail before committing.
