Knitwear Trims: Soft Labels That Survive Washing Without Snagging

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 18, 2026

Knitwear trims — the neck labels, care labels, hang tags and packaging used on sweaters, knit tops and sweatshirts — look identical to those on woven garments, yet the requirements are very different. Knitted fabric is built from loops: hard labels, coarse thread and sharp corners snag it easily, and washing brings shrinkage, pilling and distortion. Pick the wrong trim and the customer notices at the very first wash.

This guide works through the four parts that matter — neck label, care label, hang tag and string, packaging — and closes with a specification sheet you can copy straight into a request for quotation, plus a five-step sampling check.

1. Four Requirements Knitwear Places on Trims

  • Softness: loops hate hard edges, and label edges plus stitching are the first points of friction
  • Wash durability: knitwear sits against the skin and gets washed often, so labels must not fade, curl or stiffen
  • Matching shrinkage: if the fabric shrinks and the label does not, the label wrinkles and lifts
  • Low weight: heavy tags and fasteners pull on the neckline yarn, especially on fine-gauge knits

2. Neck Label: Soft, Thin, Kind to Skin

For knitwear, favour a light satin or plain-weave woven label; for premium close-fitting styles, a heat-transfer tag sits flat against the skin. On childrenswear, control stiffness and length so the label edge cannot scratch.

  • Material order: light satin beats plain weave, which beats thick jacquard — jacquard belongs on outerwear and denim, not next to skin
  • Edge treatment: a woven selvedge frays far less than a cut edge; cut-edge labels must be hot-cut and softened
  • Size: typically 20–35mm wide and 40–60mm long; on cashmere or silk blends keep it under 25mm wide to reduce pressure and show-through
  • Attachment: folding and stitching both sides is the most stable; stitching only top and bottom saves labour but the label lifts after washing and rubs the neck
  • Placement and colour: 5–10mm below centre back neck, clear of the rib stretch zone; on dark fine knits use the same shade or slightly lighter to avoid show-through

3. Care Label: Fibre Content, Wash Symbols and Wash Performance

On knitwear the care label is a compliance matter: wool, cashmere, viscose and blend percentages must match the tested composition, and the wash symbols must match the real care advice. A wool-cycle symbol paired with normal washing is a complaint waiting to happen.

4. Hang Tag and String: Avoid Snagging First, Then Design

  • String: fine cotton or polyester cord is safer than elastic, which rubs the yarn every time it retracts
  • Fastener: a plastic fastener is safer than a metal eyelet — no snagging, no corrosion, no pressure mark
  • Weight and thickness: on fine cashmere keep the tag thin and light so long hanging does not stretch the shoulder line
  • Where to attach: connect to the hanger tab or a dedicated loop rather than pinning the tag onto the knit surface
  • Shape and colourfastness: die-cut tags need rounded corners; dark cords must be colourfast so they do not mark pale knits

5. Packing and Shipping: Moisture, Pressure, Moths

  • Flat packing: add a sheet of tissue against the napped face to keep fold marks off the surface, then use a ziplock or flat bag
  • Hanger packing: hangers and hang tabs must support the shoulder so long hanging does not stretch the hem or cuffs
  • Moisture: on long sea freight add desiccant and a moisture-barrier inner bag, especially for natural-fibre blends
  • Moths: wool and cashmere can take a moth repellent strip, but check odour and destination compliance first
  • Avoid compression: vacuum packing flattens loft and hand feel, so trade a little freight cost for a lower compression ratio

7. Sampling and Acceptance: Five Checks

  1. Hand feel: press the neck label against the side of the neck and confirm no prickle or hard edge
  2. After-wash check: run one 30°C wash plus line dry per the symbols and look for fading, curling and pale print
  3. Shrinkage comparison: wash fabric and label together and compare dimensions for wrinkling or lifting
  4. Attachment strength: check string pull strength and that the fastener catches neither skin nor fabric
  5. Colour and show-through: pair dark knits with a matching label shade and check show-through in strong light
