VerpackG and LUCID: The Registration Is in the Buyer's Name, the Data Is in Yours

By TAGE Packaging · Updated November 26, 2026

A single export carton can hold a PE poly bag, a paper carrier bag, a sheet of tissue and a seal sticker, all travelling from Dongguan to a German warehouse together. Under the German packaging act, VerpackG, each of those four items carries an obligation, and that obligation sits with the company that places the packaging on the German market, not with the factory that made it. This is the part buyers most often get wrong. This guide sets out who has to register in LUCID, what the annual volume report actually weighs, which of your bags count as system-participation packaging, and the six data points a custom packaging supplier has to hand over so the brand can file without guessing.

1. Three Roles, Two Obligations

VerpackG has been in force since 1 January 2019, with a further amendment in July 2021, and it splits the duty in two: registration (Registerpflicht) and system participation (Systembeteiligungspflicht), plus two layers of volume reporting. There are only three roles, and the order in which you assign them matters.

  • Producer (Hersteller): the party that first places the packaging on the German market on a commercial basis. A brand importing from China and reselling is a producer, and so is a foreign distance seller shipping directly to German consumers.
  • Distributor (Vertreiber): the party that fills service packaging at the point of sale, or passes on packaging it did not itself place on the market.
  • Your factory: neither, in the legal sense. Yet across the whole chain only you know how many grams each bag weighs, which material it is and whether it carries a coating, and without that set of numbers the report cannot be completed.

2. Register Before the First Delivery (Section 9 VerpackG)

Under Section 9 VerpackG, a producer must register in the LUCID register kept by the ZSVR (Zentrale Stelle Verpackungsregister, the German packaging register) before placing packaging on the market, obtain a registration number, and pass that number to its dual system and to downstream customers. Packaging from an unregistered producer may not be circulated in Germany. That is a sales ban rather than a fine paid later, and large retailers and marketplaces check the number directly.

In practice there is a deadlock: registration needs German tax identification data, so a Chinese factory cannot register on the brand's behalf and should not be asked to. Buyers usually take one of two routes, either a German subsidiary or a fiscal representative, or an importer of record that carries producer status. Writing that sentence into the contract at quotation stage is a good deal less work than chasing bag weights afterwards.

3. The Volume Report Is a Weight Exercise, Not a Count

Reporting runs on two layers: one to LUCID, one to the dual system. The initial report (Erstmeldung) before market launch gives the categories and an estimated volume; the annual report is filed by 15 May of the following year; the figures handed to the dual system follow the cycle agreed in the contract, in practice monthly or quarterly. All numbers are in kilograms and must be split by material, because paper and board, plastic film and composites sit on three different fee scales. Dual-system fees are calculated by weight and material, so reducing film thickness or switching material does not only help the environment, it changes what the customer pays for recovery for a full year.

Do the arithmetic: one carton of 500 poly bags at 22 g each is 11 kg of plastic film. A buyer who writes down roughly a hundred kilos a year usually receives a reconciliation notice from the dual system and the ZSVR in year two. Putting the gram weight of each SKU on the shipping documents is the cheapest insurance available.

4. Which of Your Bags Is System-Participation Packaging

The dividing line is not whether the item is plastic but whether it ends up with a private household. Anything that reaches the end consumer is system-participation packaging, while transport packaging that only moves between factories and warehouses usually falls outside system participation. That line decides whether the buyer pays for the item at all, and for how many years.

Item you ship German treatment Weighed as
PE poly bag or mailer on a B2C order System participation: shipping packaging sent straight to a consumer plastic film, by kilogram
Paper carrier bag handed to a shopper System participation: service packaging paper and board, by kilogram
Tissue sheet plus seal sticker System participation: service packaging, sticker counted as composite paper or plastic composite, by kilogram
Master carton or inner bag on B2B delivery only Usually outside system participation, as it never reaches a household confirmed separately, usually recycled board

5. Six Data Points Your Supplier Has to Hand Over

These six are not a favour, they are the necessary inputs to the filing. The first four already appear on our shipping documents; the fifth and sixth depend on the wording the brand wants to use, and we add them per SKU. Miss any one and the brand can only report estimates, with the risk landing squarely on the brand.

  • Weight per item in grams, split by item and material rather than given as a carton total, for example 38 g for one carrier bag made of 60 g/m² paper.
  • The ratio inside a composite: 60 g/m² paper plus 8 g/m² PE coating, for instance, lands in two different lines of the report.
  • Recycled content and its evidence: any claim of a recycled share needs an FSC Recycled certificate or a GRS or RCS transaction certificate behind it.
  • Whether the item is mono-material: is there a barrier layer, is the sticker paper-based. This decides whether the design can meet recyclability requirements from 2030.
  • Print coverage and colour: a full-surface carbon-black print on PE film is invisible to near-infrared sorting, which effectively keeps the bag out of the recycling stream.
  • Annual quantity shipped per destination country: the same bag sent to Germany, France and Spain becomes three separate filings and cannot be merged into one.

6. Market Notes: One EU Layer over National Law

The EU packaging and packaging waste regulation, PPWR (EU) 2025/40, applies from 12 August 2026 and replaces the old Directive 94/62/EC; its Article 6 recyclability requirements and Article 7 recycled-content requirements bite from 2030. National registers do not disappear because of it, and VerpackG with LUCID remains the German entry point. The same bag sold into different countries meets a different set: France looks at AGEC law n° 2020-105 with Triman and info-tri marking, Spain at Real Decreto 1055/2022 and the plastic tax under Ley 7/2022, Japan at the Container and Packaging Recycling Law and the Plastic Resource Circulation Act with their labelling duties, Korea at EPR under the Act on the Promotion of Saving and Recycling of Resources with its marking obligations, and China assesses packaging and environment through the GB/T 16716 series.

One more line that is widely misread: biodegradable does not mean exempt. A bag labelled compostable is still packaging, and if it reaches a German household it must be counted in system participation; compostability needs proof through the EN 13432 industrial composting tests, and what that proves is compostability, not recyclability. The two certificates do not substitute for each other.

Two adjacent obligations get missed in the same file. The first is chemistry: the inks, adhesives and coatings on the bag sit inside the REACH (EC) 1907/2006 restrictions and the SVHC candidate list, so a bag that is perfectly recyclable on paper can still be refused because of what was printed on it. The second is wording: an environmental statement printed on packaging is advertising, governed in France by the Code de la consommation and the AGEC law, in Japan by the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations, in Korea by the Act on Fair Labelling and Advertising, and in Spain by Ley 7/2022 alongside general consumer protection rules. If the bag also carries a wash-care or fibre-composition line, Japan applies its Household Goods Quality Labelling Act and Korea the labelling standards of the Act on Safety Management of Electrical Appliances and Consumer Products, while China maps to GB/T 5296.4 and GB/T 16716. Print what you can document, and nothing else.
