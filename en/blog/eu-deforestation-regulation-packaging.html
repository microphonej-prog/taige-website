EUDR and Paper-Based Trims & Packaging: How to Build the Document Chain

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 28, 2026

Since the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) came into force, European buyers have added one question before ordering: where does the pulp in your paper bags and kraft hang tags come from, and can you document it? The logic is simple: EUDR does not judge whether a product is eco-friendly, but whether the land behind it was deforested. Paper bags, boxes, kraft hang tags and tissue paper mostly fall into the paper and paperboard category, so their origin has to be explainable. A supplier with a complete document chain is already ahead at the quotation stage.

1. What EUDR Really Targets: Origin, Not Appearance

EUDR covers seven commodities and their derived products: cattle, cocoa, coffee, palm oil, rubber, soy and wood, with wood including wood products, pulp and paper products. Applicability is decided by HS code, not by industry: a paper bag and a brochure from the same factory can carry different codes, one requiring traceability, the other not. So the first step is not arguing about whether you are in scope, but listing the HS codes and checking them one by one.

💡 In one line: EUDR asks for due-diligence evidence that the forest land behind this paper is legal and deforestation-free. Words such as recycled, recyclable or eco do not decide whether a product is in scope.

2. Who Carries the Duty: Brand, Importer, Supplier

The duty to file a due diligence statement (DDS) sits mainly with the operator that first places the goods on the EU market — the brand or the importer. A trims factory or printer in China is not the declarant, but if upstream documents are missing, the buyer simply cannot complete that statement. This is why paper-origin documents decide where an order lands: the lowest quote still fails the buyer's compliance gate if the paperwork cannot be completed.

3. How to Build the Document Chain for Paper Trims

You do not need a perfect chain on day one, but the order matters: confirm the HS codes, lock the paper sources, then link them to batches. Four document types cover most buyer questions.

Document Usually issued by Key content If missing
FSC or PEFC chain-of-custody (CoC) certificate Certified printer or paper merchant Certificate number, validity, product categories covered The buyer cannot confirm traceability and looks for a certified supplier
Mill or pulp supplier declaration Paper mill (may be passed on by the merchant) Product name, HS code, country of origin, fibre source Key DDS fields stay blank and the buyer cannot file
Order-to-batch mapping record The trims factory making the bags or tags Order number matched to roll lot and mill lot Once questioned, there is no path back to the paper source
Upstream geolocation data (if required) Usually held by the pulp mill, paper mill or certification body Plot coordinates, harvest or production date Met only indirectly through certification: agree the approach with the buyer early

4. Four Common Misunderstandings at Quotation Stage

  • An FSC logo equals compliance: FSC is forest management and chain-of-custody certification and strong evidence, but it does not file the EUDR due diligence for the buyer, who still has to submit the statement.
  • Recycled paper is automatically exempt: scope depends on the HS code and the fibre source; deciding by how green a material sounds is the wrong test.
  • Wait until the rules are fully clear: confirming sources and collecting certificates takes weeks; starting only when the order lands guarantees a delay.
  • Buy only on the lowest paper price: a mill that will not provide documents costs more than it saves, because the buyer gets stuck at the filing stage and the whole shipment slips.

5. Six Questions Worth Asking Your Supplier Before Ordering

  • Which HS codes apply to the paper in our bags and tags, and is each of them in scope?
  • Which mills supply the paper, and can you provide FSC or PEFC CoC certificates with transaction verification?
  • Can the order number be traced to the roll lot and the mill lot, supplied with the goods or on request?
  • Can upstream provide the country of origin and, if the buyer needs it, geolocation data?
  • If we change paper, grammage or construction, which documents have to be redone?
  • Do the FSC logo and environmental claim wording on the packaging have a valid licence and licence code?
💡 Note: EUDR scope and timing have been revised several times; this article covers the division of labour — who issues which document and how it travels. For specific codes, filing dates and enforcement details, follow the latest European Commission notices and your buyer's compliance team.
