GOH vs Flat Pack Shipping: Which Protects Your Trims Better

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 16, 2026

A garment-on-hanger (GOH) container and a flat pack carton move the same jacket under completely different stress on the trims. In GOH the garment hangs from a rail for four to six weeks and arrives floor-ready; flat packed, it is folded, bagged and compressed into a carton. The choice changes freight cost, the hanger loop, the hang tag position, the poly bag spec and the moisture risk, and it has to be decided before the hang tag artwork is approved, not after.

1. What GOH Actually Changes

A GOH container is fitted with rails on both walls and the garment hangs on a hanger from that rail. There is no carton, so nothing compresses the garment and nothing absorbs vibration. That protects a structured shoulder, but it means the hang tag, the hanger loop and any metal hardware swing for the whole transit. Flat pack does the opposite: the garment is folded into a poly bag and the carton absorbs the movement, but the fold line and the bag corners press on the trims. Neither method is safer in general; each moves the risk to a different part.

  • GOH: swing and rail vibration load the hanger loop and the tag hole
  • Flat pack: compression and fold lines load snaps, buttons and print
  • Both: moisture, because a container is a sealed steel box with a 15–20 °C day-night swing

2. The Freight Math Behind the Decision

A 40 ft high-cube container measures roughly 1,203 × 235 × 269 cm inside. Loaded flat, cartons fill that volume almost completely; loaded GOH, the rails and the air between hanging garments consume 30–40% of the usable volume. So GOH costs more per garment in freight, by a few cents on a low-value item and almost nothing on a premium one. It pays back only when the destination would otherwise spend on pressing, steaming, re-hanging and re-ticketing, which on a wool coat is far more than the freight difference.

Item GOH Flat pack
Volume per garment 30–40% more baseline
Arrival condition floor-ready needs pressing
Hang tag visible on the rail, faces the aisle folded inside the bag
Poly bag 40 micron, or none 40 micron, sealed
Main trim risk hanger loop tear, tag hole tear compression on buttons, print rub
Best for structured outerwear, suits, premium knits, denim, basics, low value

3. Trim Specs That Change With the Method

In GOH the single most important trim is the hanger loop. Specify the tape width (10 to 12 mm for a 5 mm hanger hook), the stitch density at both ends, and the loop length inside the collar. A loop sewn at 8 stitches per inch without back-tacking will pull open at the seam within two weeks of rail vibration. In flat pack the equivalent question is the fold line: if the garment is folded across a chest print or across a row of snaps, expect a permanent crease and a rub mark by the time the carton is opened.

  • Hanger loop: 10–12 mm tape, 8–10 stitches per inch, back-tacked at both ends
  • Hang tag: 350 g/m² board with a reinforced eyelet when it hangs free on the rail
  • Poly bag: 40 micron, perforated when the garment is bagged straight off the ironing table
  • Security tag: through the hanger loop, never through the care label

4. Humidity Is the Trim Killer Nobody Specs

A steel container crossing the Indian Ocean can swing 15–20 °C between day and night. As the air cools, moisture condenses on the ceiling and drips onto the top layer; the trade calls it container rain. A 350 g/m² paper hang tag takes up that moisture and curls within days, and a curled tag does not scan or read on the shelf. Two numbers belong in the spec: desiccant measured in grams per cubic metre of carton volume, not in units per container, and a target relative humidity of 50–60% inside the poly bag.

A 350 g/m² hang tag that leaves the factory flat can arrive with a 2–3 mm curl on any route over 25 days. Either add a 0.5 mm lamination or move up to a 400 g/m² board; both cost less than a return.

5. Floor-Ready Means the Label Is Read, Not Checked

GOH garments go straight to the rail, so the care label and the fibre content statement are read by the shopper, not by a quality inspector. That changes the cost of a wrong label: it is a consumer-facing error, not factory rework. Shipping into the EU, fibre names and percentages follow Regulation (EU) 1007/2011. Shipping into Japan, composition and care follow the Household Goods Quality Labelling Act. Shipping into Korea, quality labelling duties come from the Electrical Appliances and Consumer Products Safety Control Act. Print once, and verify the language set for every destination before the run starts.

6. Five Checks Before You Book the Container

  1. Hanger loop pull test on 5 pieces: 30 N for 10 seconds, no slippage at the stitch
  2. Hang tag curl: 24 hours at 65% relative humidity, then measure the lift at the corner
  3. Poly bag perforation count on a bag taken from the actual roll
  4. Label language set matches every destination on the purchase order
  5. Desiccant quantity recorded per carton, not per container
