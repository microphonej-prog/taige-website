Answering a Brand's ESG Data Request as a Trims Supplier

By TAGE Packaging · Updated November 25, 2026

Every autumn a brand's sustainability team sends an Excel sheet or a platform link asking for environmental data on woven labels, hang tags and packaging bags. Many trims factories treat it as one form to fill in, and thirty rows later the buyer still replies: how were these numbers calculated. The problem is not the workload. The questionnaire is really asking three things at once: how the factory performs environmentally, whether management systems are in place, and the carbon footprint of a specific batch. The three use different units and different verification routes, and answering them together answers none. We make custom trims in Dongguan; this guide separates the three and shows which document each number comes from.

1. Three Questionnaires, Three Different Questions

Higg FEM asks about the facility: energy, water, wastewater, waste and chemical management, scored item by item and verified by an approved verifier, with a score that belongs to a year. EcoVadis asks about management systems: is there a policy, a record, evidence of improvement, summarised in a percentile scorecard. ESRS E1 under CSRD asks for value-chain data: the emissions a brand puts into its annual report, traceable to the supplier and subject to limited assurance. The same factory can produce three completely different answers. A high FEM score does not mean a good scorecard, and a good scorecard does not prove that the carbon footprint of one batch of woven labels can be calculated.

  • Higg FEM = annual facility data in kWh, m³ and kg, third-party verified.
  • EcoVadis = a management-system scorecard on percentiles, built on policies and records.
  • CSRD / ESRS E1 = value-chain emissions, traceable to the supplier and open to assurance.

2. The Numbers Behind One Woven Label

Buyers usually want a figure per thousand pieces or per kilogram; the factory holds monthly bills. Reconciling the two starts with sub-metering: one electricity meter for weaving, one for dyeing and finishing, one for printing, at least one water meter, and waste weighed in kilograms. Without sub-meters you fall back on allocation, and allocation invites a verification question about method; numbers with a vague method tend to be dropped by the brand. Our suggestion for a first round is three tasks: measure grammage and weight per thousand pieces properly (ASTM D3776 for fabric, ISO 536 for paper), reconcile the electricity bill against the sub-meter, and tie recycled content to batch numbers on the GRS transaction certificate.

Data point Unit and basis Source document
Electricity kWh per 1,000 labels, kWh per kg printed Utility bill plus sub-meter reading, reconciled monthly
Substrate grammage g/m², ASTM D3776 for fabric and ISO 536 for paper Incoming inspection report, retained sample per lot
Recycled content per cent, by batch rather than by year GRS or RCS scope certificate plus transaction certificate

3. Facility Numbers and Product Numbers Are Not Interchangeable

Organisational carbon inventories follow ISO 14064-1, product footprints follow ISO 14067, and material averages come from databases such as Higg MSI. The three are not interchangeable: MSI is an industry average, not this factory and this batch, so using it to fill a product footprint field is returned at verification. Dividing facility emissions by output gives a per-unit figure that works as an internal management metric and fails as a product footprint. Our rule: MSI for early estimates and design comparisons only; formal replies carry an ISO 14067 report or measured supplier data, with the boundary stated in the notes — cradle-to-gate or cradle-to-grave.

4. Evidence That Survives a Verifier

A verifier does not read the deck; it reads three lines: certificate, batch, ledger. Recycled content needs a GRS or RCS scope certificate proving the factory is eligible, matched to the transaction certificate for that batch proving the material was actually used — with only one of the two, the claim breaks. Chemicals need a statement against the REACH Annex XVII restricted substances list, plus a notification record where an SVHC exceeds 0.1%. Energy needs a reconciliation sheet between sub-meters and bills, and a variance above 5% needs an explanation. These documents usually already exist in the factory; nobody has filed them one question to one document.

5. What Not to Write in the Reply

An ESG reply is treated as a public statement, held to the same standard as website copy. Three common overreaches: turning used recycled polyester into a sustainable product; turning a factory's renewable electricity share into a carbon-neutral product; turning design for recycling into 100% recyclable without the collection infrastructure or test basis. Japan's Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations (景品表示法) targets superior misrepresentation, the EU green claims rules target unsubstantiated environmental claims, and Korea's Act on Fair Labelling and Advertising (표시광고법) covers false marking — different jurisdictions, one logic: if you cannot evidence the sentence, do not write it.

A practical rule: a number that can carry the note ISO 14067, cradle-to-gate, paper and printing included, transport excluded, is a number that can enter a brand's annual report. If you cannot state the boundary, label it an estimate.

6. Time and Cost: How Long the First Round Takes

A first Higg FEM, from installing sub-meters to a verified score, usually takes more than a quarter: one month of data collection, two weeks of internal reconciliation, and four to eight weeks of verifier scheduling. A first EcoVadis round runs six to ten weeks because policy documents have to be written. An ISO 14067 footprint for a single product takes four to six weeks if the material factors are already sourced; if the supplier cannot provide them, sampling and testing double that. On cost structure, verification and consultant fees are fixed; the real variable is whether sub-meters exist — without them, everything starts with installing meters.

7. What to Send Us So the Data Is Usable

  • The buyer's original questionnaire, not only the summary fields — a wrong category means starting over.
  • Substrate g/m², weight per thousand pieces, and whether accessories such as the plastic fastener are included.
  • Copies of certificates you already hold: GRS or RCS scope, FSC chain of custody, OEKO-TEX reports.
  • Last month's electricity bill and output, so we can estimate allocation and give you a range to argue with.
