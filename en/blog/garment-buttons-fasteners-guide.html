Garment Buttons and Metal Fasteners: Resin, Metal, Snaps and Eyelets

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated October 2, 2026

Buttons and metal fasteners are the smallest parts of a garment and surprisingly often where complaints start: a button falls off, a snap will not hold, an eyelet pulls out, plating darkens after washing. This guide breaks garment buttons and metal fasteners into six parts — material choice, snaps and press studs, eyelets and rivets, matching the care method, child safety and inspection, and an enquiry checklist.

The principle in one line: fastener failures almost never come from the part itself but from three mismatches — material versus care method, holding strength versus fabric thickness, and plating versus destination regulation.

1. Start With the List: Which Fasteners, Failing Where

  • Buttons: the usual faults are thread failure and scratched faces; a four-hole button holds better than two-hole, but bad sewing undoes that advantage
  • Snaps and press studs: they fail as loosening holding power, a stud punching through the cloth, or flaking plating — all tied to stud size, fabric thickness and how often the piece is worn
  • Hooks and trouser hooks: once deformed they snag the cloth — on light and knitted fabrics choose rounded, gently tensioned versions
  • Eyelets and rivets: typical problems are pull-out, corrosion and edges biting the yarn — denim and workwear also have to survive stone and enzyme washing
  • Zipper pulls and cord locks: small hardware of the same family — check cycle life, surface finish and sharp edges, and on kidswear assess cord length too

2. Choosing the Button Material

MaterialStrengthsWatch out for
Resin (polyester / urea)Freest colours and shapes, controlled cost, survives home washingHigh-temperature drying can distort it, and some resins dull in aggressive dry-cleaning solvent — confirm the care method first
Imitation shell and pearlescent resinRefined look, small colour variation, stable across batches — good for shirts and premium linesThickness and weight decide the feel — too thin looks cheap; hold the hole-pitch tolerance or automatic button machines jam
Metal (brass, alloy, stainless)Strong premium signal and durable — right for denim, outerwear and workwearPlating and nickel release matter most: skin-contact items are usually held to a 0.5 μg/cm²/week limit, with the test method stated
Wood, horn and natural materialsGreat texture and a credible sustainability story — good for designer labels and winter outerwearDislikes alkali, long soaking and strong solvent; natural batch variation needs an agreed tolerance up front
Fabric-covered and self-cover buttonsExactly the body fabric in colour and texture — a unified look for formalwear and suitingNeeds a fabric swatch to make them, and the cloth may pucker or shift after washing — always run a wash test

3. Snaps and Press Studs: Setting the Holding Force

  • Force by application: close-fit and kidswear want a lighter feel (easy to close, not popping open), while outerwear, workwear and bags want a firmer one; never mix two forces on one style
  • Stud size versus fabric weight: light and knitted cloth takes a small, shallow stud, heavy cloth and denim can take a larger one; the prong must bite the fabric without coming through the face
  • Setting process: air pressure and die wear drive holding strength, so check opening force per batch in production — not only once at sampling
  • Concealed options: for a hidden placket you can use concealed snaps, covered studs or magnets — assess their effect on skin comfort and child safety, and confirm cycle life

4. Eyelets, Rivets and Metal Parts: Plating and Wash Life

  • Plating choice: nickel plating is cheap but can trigger skin allergy; for the EU prefer nickel-free, brass, gold-tone or coated finishes and keep test reports on file
  • Wash and chlorine: ordinary plated parts dull and corrode in chlorine bleach and salty perspiration — for swim and outdoor tell us the end use up front
  • Setting and safety: eyelet edges must be flattened without burrs and rivet prongs must stay hidden; on light fabric add a reinforcing patch to spread the load so it survives repeated washing
  • Denim finishing: stone wash, enzyme wash and sanding strip plating, so either set the metal parts after washing, or specify hardware already finished to look worn

5. Childrenswear: The Baseline for Fastener Safety

  • Tension and torque: kidswear fasteners are tested to the destination regulation and customer standard — a common expectation is that they stay put at roughly 70–90N; confirm the exact figure with your customer
  • Small-parts risk: a detached fastener is a small part; infant garments should avoid detachable small pieces, and decorative buttons need reinforced sewing plus pull checks
  • Chemical compliance: fasteners and plating must meet limits for formaldehyde, azo amines, lead and cadmium — typical references are formaldehyde by contact class, azo amines below 30 mg/kg and a 100 ppm lead limit for children's products, always per destination law
  • Sharp edges and cords: no sharp edges or burrs, and hood and waist cords must respect children's drawstring rules on exposed length to avoid snagging

6. Enquiry Checklist: These Items Are Enough

ItemWhat to fill in
Style and placementPlacket, cuff, pocket, waistband or decoration, and whether it touches the skin
Material and finishResin, metal or natural, with colour and finish (gloss, matt, antique, painted)
Size and strengthButton diameter or eyelet bore, pull and holding-force targets, and whether third-party reports are needed
