Hang Tag Artwork Guide: Bleed, Resolution & Color Setup

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 4, 2026

Hang tag artwork quality decides whether the finished tags look premium or defective: missing bleed means white edges after cutting; low-resolution images leave jagged logo edges; a wrong color mode makes brand colors come out completely different. These three issues cause most hang tag reprints, and nearly all of them can be avoided at the file stage. This hang tag artwork guide covers bleed, resolution, color and die-cut layers so your tags pass sampling the first time.

Whether you are a designer or a brand buyer — preparing files in-house or asking the factory to do it — mastering the specifications below saves rounds of communication and lifts your sampling pass rate.

1. Bleed and Safe Margins

Cutting tolerances mean the finished size never matches the design perfectly. That is why every artwork needs 3 mm of bleed — background colors and images extend 3 mm beyond the final cut line so no white edge appears after trimming. The safe margin is the distance from content (text, logos, barcodes) to the finished edge; keep it at least 2–3 mm so a slight cut shift never clips your content.

2. Resolution and Image Quality

  • Bitmaps at 300 dpi minimum: place images at 1:1 finished size; anything below 200 dpi shows visible jagged edges and pixelation when enlarged
  • Keep logos vector: illustrations and brand marks in AI/EPS/PDF vector format scale without any loss of quality
  • QR codes and barcodes: generate at 600 dpi or higher, keep at least 2 mm of quiet zone around them, and scan-test samples after printing

A common trap: screenshots and images saved from websites are usually 72 dpi — they blur when scaled and must not be used for print.

3. CMYK and Spot Colors: Why Colors Differ

Screens emit color in RGB while printing stacks CMYK inks, and the two gamuts differ — so screen color and printed color can never match 100%. If your brand color is critical, specify a Pantone spot color and proof it against the physical fan deck with the printer.

Color ModeBest ForKey Points
CMYK processPhotos, gradients, multi-color artworkJudge by digital proof and press sheet; screen color is only a reference
Pantone spot colorBrand logos, core VI colorsSmallest color difference; specify the exact number (e.g. PMS 872C)
Foil stamping, embossing, UVDecorative effects, not ink colorsMark position and effect with a single-color vector layer in the file

The same file also prints differently on different papers: coated art paper is vivid, kraft absorbs ink and looks darker. If brand color is essential, always confirm a physical sample before production.

4. Text: Outlines, Font Size and Proofreading

  • Outline your fonts: convert text to paths before delivery so a missing font cannot cause garbled or substituted type
  • Minimum font size: keep body Chinese text at 6 pt or larger; reverse-out and light typefaces need one size up
  • Proofread everything: brand name, website, size and origin — one wrong character on a hang tag voids the whole run

5. File Formats and Naming

  • Deliver PDFs with bleed (plus die-cut layer) or AI/CDR source files; package all placed images and fonts together
  • Naming suggestion: brand_model_size_version date, e.g. TAGE_HT001_54x90_v1_20260904
  • When several styles or sizes share one file, separate them clearly by layer or page to avoid production mix-ups

6. Pre-Press Checklist
