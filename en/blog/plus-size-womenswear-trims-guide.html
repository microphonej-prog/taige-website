Plus-Size Womenswear Trims: Sizing, Reinforcement and Packaging

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated October 9, 2026

Plus-size womenswear is one of the fastest-growing apparel segments in Europe and North America, but its trim requirements are nothing like standard sizes: size labels run all the way to 5XL, waistbands and side seams need reinforcement, care labels must explain how stretch fabrics behave, hang tags need a real garment measurement chart, and packaging has to scale up with the garment. Order standard-size trims and the three most common outcomes are incomplete size marking that gets delisted, care labels that wrinkle and stiffen at the waistband, and bags the garment simply will not fit into.

1. Four Requirements Plus Size Places on Trims

1) Size range: one style often runs from XS to 5XL, so label quantities and stock multiply, and a letter size means very different real measurements in different markets. 2) Load and reinforcement: plus-size garments put more tension on waistbands, side seams, tag cords and packaging bags, so stitching and tag-pin load must be checked at the largest size. 3) Care label comfort: plus-size customers notice a side-seam label more, so width, material and stitching position matter more than at standard sizes. 4) Packaging size: the same style needs wider, deeper bags and paper bags, and hanging styles need a longer hanger bag.

2. Laying Out the Size Range: S–5XL and Market Conversions

Whichever route you take, the size label has to agree with the fibre content, origin and care instructions on the care label. Cross-border sellers should also check each platform's size-hint format: many returns happen not because the garment fits badly but because the size information itself does not add up.

3. Woven and Care Labels: Reinforcing the Waistband and Side Seam

  • Neck label position: on plus size the main label often sits at the back neck or inside the waistband — use a narrow soft woven label inside the waistband so it does not press into the skin when the wearer bends.
  • Care label position and width: keep a side-seam care label within about 25–30 mm wide, use satin or a soft coated material, and bar-tack both ends so repeated wear does not pull it off.
  • Reinforcement stitching: double-needle or bar-tack the waistband, side seams and cord attachment points, and allow stretch allowance on elastic fabric so it does not pucker after washing.
  • Cords and tag pins: check tag-pin load at the largest size on plus-size outerwear and down garments, and choose a thicker cotton or nylon cord for long coats.

4. Care Marking for Stretch Fabrics

Plus-size womenswear leans heavily on elastane blends and high-stretch knits, and care requirements differ from ordinary cotton: the higher the elastane, the more it suffers from hot drying and long soaking, so the care label should specify a low wash temperature, no bleach, low ironing, and no tumble dry or low heat only. Coated care labels must be wash-tested to the strictest condition on the label, because a plus-size garment is stretched more often after washing and the print blurs and edges crack sooner.

💡 Tip: stretch one-size items cause the most trouble: one size label covers S–L, the customer is really an L, and if the fabric does not recover after washing the size feels mislabelled. Add a tear-away note and a height and weight range on the hang tag or care label.

5. What the Hang Tag Should Say: Size Chart, Fit Notes and QR Code

The front of a plus-size hang tag usually carries only the brand and the line; the back does the real work: print the garment's flat measurements (bust, waist, hip, length, sleeve), the height and weight range it suits and the size conversion (EU / US / UK). A QR code can point to a fuller size guide, the fibre composition and care instructions, cutting customer-service questions and giving the brand somewhere to put content. Use 350–400 g art paper or kraft and size the tag up to 55×90 mm or more so the information is not cramped.

6. Packaging and Hanging: Bag Types, Hanger Bags and Paper Bags

Once the garment grows, three packaging parameters have to be recalculated: bag width, bag depth and film thickness. Leave at least 20–30 mm of slack beyond the folded width, because a thin plus-size bag tears at the seal; for hanging styles switch to a longer, wider hanger bag and check the hook load against the whole garment weight; for retail paper bags, reassess the load and handle and consider a heavier board with a reinforced handle on plus-size items. Mailing bags and retail paper bags follow different logic — do not force one specification onto both.

7. Quote Request and Acceptance Checklist

  • Size range and expected quantity per size, including real order volumes at XS and 5XL
  • Fibre content and elastane percentage, plus the strictest care condition (water temperature, tumble dry or not)
  • Stitching position (neck, inside waistband or side seam) and tag cord or pin load at the largest size
  • Flat garment measurement chart per size (bust, waist, hip and length) and whether a QR code is needed
  • Bag type, width, depth and film thickness, and whether hanging packaging is required (hanger bags plus hanging cartons)
