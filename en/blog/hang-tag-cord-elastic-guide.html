Hang Tag Cords, Strings and Elastic Loops

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 19, 2026

The cord is the part of a hang tag that everyone treats as an afterthought. It gets pulled, cut, hung on a rail and rubbed for the life of the garment, and at the border it has to prove that this tag really belongs to this garment. A 0.3 mm difference in diameter, 20 mm in loop length or a different knot changes how the tag behaves at retail. This guide treats the cord as a real specification: material, diameter, how length is stated, how it is fastened and how it is accepted.

1. Material and Diameter: Five Options, Real Differences

Material Typical diameter Where it works, and the catch
Waxed cotton cord 1.0–2.0 mm Natural feel and a knot that holds; wax can bloom white in humid transit
Nylon monofilament 0.5–0.8 mm Clear, thin, visually neutral; low break strength and a sharp cut end
Polyester twisted cord 1.2–2.5 mm High strength and weather resistance; reads industrial, good for volume lines
Round elastic loop 0.8–1.5 mm Easiest to loop over a button; loses stretch under long-term tension
Satin or grosgrain ribbon 3–8 mm width Strongest brand impression; the edge frays unless heat-cut or folded

One threshold that is easy to miss is the hole. A 3 mm hole holds a 1.0 mm cotton cord well; with a 2.5 mm polyester cord there is almost no play, so every pull at retail transfers straight into the board and the hole tears within days. Keep hole diameter at two to three times the cord diameter.

2. Stating Length: Cut, Loop and Hang Length

One cord can carry three completely different numbers, which is where most cord disputes start. Cut length is the straight length before knotting. Loop length is half the circumference of the closed loop after knotting or welding, usually quoted as a folded length. Hang length is the visible distance from the tag hole to the fixation point once the garment is tagged. The PO has to say which one, with a tolerance — ±5 mm is normal.

Rules of thumb: 120 mm loop for e-commerce single-unit packing (fastest to tag, least tangling), 150–180 mm for department-store or premium rails so the tag hangs near the cuff, and the children's neck zone handled per EN 14682 as covered in section 5.

3. Fastening: From a Knot to a Metal Eyelet

  • Single knot plus glue: cheapest, fine for one-time retail tags, but the adhesive must be non-yellowing
  • Plastic barb or tag pin: re-closable, easy to re-tag on returns, good for try-on heavy categories
  • Safety pin: for hosiery and scarves that cannot take a hole; nickel release becomes a test item
  • Metal eyelet plus cord: durable and premium looking; watch the hole fit and plating wear
  • Stitched directly: merges tag and label into one operation and makes removal destructive

4. Colour Matching and Material Pairing

Cord colour is not a 'close enough' item. Dyed cord and printed board develop colour differently, and one colour code reads a step darker on cotton. The workable method: pick the cord against the tag's background, approve it on a physical dip sample, allow ΔE within 1.5, and test crocking and wash migration separately on dark cords, or pale garments will show a halo around the tag.

5. Childrenswear and Compliance: No Long Cords at the Neck

Children's hang tags must not carry a free-moving long cord in the neck zone — the core restriction of EN 14682; the Chinese market adds GB 31701. The safe answer is a cord short enough that it cannot pass over the head, or a plastic barb punched next to the neck label. Exporting to the EU also means meeting GPSR (EU) 2023/988 on traceability and the responsible party: the label or packaging must identify an operator, not just a brand name.

6. Acceptance: Three Measurable Checks

  • Break strength: pull at a fixed rate to failure and record the peak; scatter across ten consecutive pieces matters more than the absolute figure
  • Colourfastness: dry and wet crocking, plus 40 °C wash migration on dark cords
  • Metal parts: nickel release judged per EN 1811, and plating adhesion checked by tape peel

Written into the acceptance block of the PO, the cord stops being the item everyone argues about at goods-in. It also makes the supplier's material planning accurate: most cord waste hides inside the phrase 'about this long'.
