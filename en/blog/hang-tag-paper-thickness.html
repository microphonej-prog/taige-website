How to Choose Hang Tag Paper Thickness: A Complete Guide from 250gsm to 500gsm

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated August 29, 2026

Hang tag paper thickness is the first factor that defines the quality feel of your tag. Higher grammage means thicker, stiffer paper and a more solid hand feel — but higher cost and reduced folding and die-cutting performance. Understanding how to choose hang tag paper thickness helps you find the sweet spot between premium feel and budget, avoiding overspending on excess bulk or looking cheap with flimsy stock.

This guide explains grammage vs thickness, best uses for common weights, stiffness vs size matching, plus finishing compatibility and sampling checks.

1. Grammage vs Thickness Basics

Grammage (gsm or g/m²) is the weight of one square meter of paper — the most common spec unit in the industry. Within the same material, higher grammage means thicker paper. But different materials have different densities: art paper and kraft at the same grammage differ in thickness — always state both grammage and material when requesting a quote.

  • Grammage (gsm) — weight per square meter; drives the material cost——每平方米克重，决定纸张的用料成本
  • Thickness (microns/mm) — determined by grammage and material density; verify with a caliper——由克重与材质密度共同决定，可用测厚仪实测验收
  • Stiffness — the key to hand feel; depends on grammage, fiber structure and bulk——吊牌手感的关键指标，与克重、纤维结构和纸张松厚度相关

3. Stiffness vs Tag Size

The larger the tag, the more its own weight demands higher grammage to stay flat and crisp; otherwise edges droop and look limp. Custom die-cuts and long formats are also stiffness-sensitive — narrow tags bend more easily when hanging.

  • Standard sizes (35×70mm, 40×90mm, etc.) — 300gsm is enough
  • Large formats (60×120mm and up) — start at 350gsm, go to 400gsm if needed
  • Die-cut and narrow formats — assess stiffness at the narrowest point; lamination adds rigidity if needed

4. Finishing Requirements by Grammage

  • Foil stamping and embossing — 300gsm and above; pressure processes need a substantial base, thin paper can crack or crease——建议 300g 以上，压力工艺需要足够厚实的纸基，过薄的纸易压穿或起皱
  • Lamination — film adds significant stiffness; 250gsm laminated feels close to 350gsm unlaminated, a useful cost lever——覆膜本身会显著增挺，250g 覆膜后的挺度接近 350g 不覆膜，可据此优化成本
  • Holes and eyelets — the area around holes needs strength; thin paper tears when pulled——孔位与鸡眼附近需要足够强度，过薄纸张在拉拽时容易撕裂

5. Sampling Checklist

When the physical sample arrives, check each item on this list:

  • Thickness — measure with a caliper and match it against the agreed grammage
  • Stiffness — hold by one corner and check whether edges droop
  • Print and finishing — foil coverage, emboss depth, hole edges free of burrs
  • Color — compare with the artwork and confirm paper whiteness is acceptable
