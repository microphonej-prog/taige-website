Recycled Content Claims: What a Hang Tag Can and Cannot Promise

By TAGE Packaging &nbsp;·&nbsp; Updated November 24, 2026

A hang tag that says 60% recycled content is not a marketing line; it is a claim with a paper trail. The trail has three links: a scope certificate for the supplier, a transaction certificate for that specific shipment, and a specification that names which recycled stream the percentage refers to. Remove any one of the three and the number turns from an asset into a liability, because from 2026 onward buyers in Europe and North America ask for the certificate before they ask for the price.

This guide is written for brands and sourcing teams buying woven labels, hang tags, packaging bags and ribbon. It covers what GRS and RCS actually certify, which fields a transaction certificate locks down, how the same sentence is read under French, Spanish, Japanese and Korean consumer law, and the three wording habits that turn a sound sustainability programme into a greenwashing complaint.

1. Recycled Content Is a Document, Not a Sentence

Technically, recycled content is the mass of recycled material divided by the total mass of the product. What actually decides whether the sentence survives scrutiny is whether the stream is named: mill offcuts and spool waste (pre-consumer) and collected bottles or garments (post-consumer) are two different claims, with different costs and different levels of scrutiny. ISO 14021, the definition set for self-declared environmental claims, expects the claim to state pre-consumer or post-consumer and the percentage; a tag that says only eco material or contains recycled content carries no information for an auditor. For a trim supplier this means the specification must answer four questions: which stream, what share, calculated under which standard, verified by whom.

2. GRS, RCS and ISO 14021: Three Different Promises

GRS, the Global Recycled Standard (version 4.0), is a voluntary certification with a 20% recycled minimum to be certified and a 50% minimum to carry the GRS logo on a product. RCS, the Recycled Claim Standard (version 3.0), starts much lower at 5%, with an RCS 100 mark at 95% or above — which is why a woven label made with 30% recycled yarn usually runs on RCS rather than GRS. Both are owned by Textile Exchange and both rest on chain of custody: a scope certificate says what a facility is allowed to produce, and a transaction certificate proves, shipment by shipment, that it did. ISO 14021 is not a certification at all; it is the wording rule, and it governs how you phrase a claim, not whether anyone audited the factory.

Standard Minimum recycled Issued by What the buyer gets
GRS 4.0 20% (50% for the logo) Certification body Scope certificate plus a TC per shipment
RCS 3.0 5% (RCS 100 at 95%+) Certification body Same set, lower threshold
ISO 14021 No threshold, wording rules The company itself Internal evidence the buyer may request

3. What a Transaction Certificate Locks Down

  • Per shipment, not per style: the TC binds one invoice and one weight, not the statement that our labels are certified.
  • The buyer name must match: the TC should name your company or your trading partner. If it names an intermediary, ask for the next link in the chain.
  • Product, weight, percentage and custody model appear together: drop any field and the percentage can no longer be traced back to a calculation.
  • Ask about validity and re-issue rules up front: scope certificates are renewed annually and TCs are usually applied for within a window after shipment, which is painful to fix later.

4. Applying It to Real Trims: Labels, Tags, Bags, Ribbon

For woven labels the usual recycled route is rPET yarn in the 50–150 denier range, certified under GRS or RCS; below 20% recycled content only RCS is available. Paper claims on hang tags do not run through GRS at all — they run through FSC Recycled or FSC Mix chain of custody, which means asking the mill for an FSC invoice claim. Poly bags commonly use 30–80% recycled LDPE, with EN 15343 as the traceability standard. Recycled polyester ribbon and tape now feel close to virgin polyester; the real difference shows up in dye-lot consistency.

  • China: self-declared environmental claims follow GB/T 24021 and textile instructions follow GB 5296.4. Do not let one label artwork serve both domestic and export orders.
  • EU market: packaging recyclability is governed by PPWR (EU) 2025/40. Recycled content and recyclability are two different promises; do not let one sentence carry both.
  • Grammage and size belong in the same line: g/m² decides stiffness and mm decides the hole and the attachment, and both decide whether the tag survives to the consumer's hand.

5. Three Wording Failures That Get Noticed

  • Claiming 100% recycled with no TC. Rewrite it as made with X% recycled content (post-consumer PET) and commit to sending the TC within 24 hours of a buyer request.
  • Treating recycled as recyclable. Recycled content is about where the material came from; PPWR is about whether the item re-enters a recycling stream. A 100% recycled paper tag with a plastic barb is worse on the second count.
  • Converting the claim into bottle counts. The moment someone asks for the assumed bottle weight, the sentence collapses. Keep the mass percentage and drop the story.
A one-sentence audit: if the claim were removed from the tag, would any document in your files contradict it? If not, it holds. If yes, fix the document, not the tag.

6. Cost, MOQ and Lead Time

The cost of a recycled claim is never one number: rPET yarn carries a premium over virgin polyester, TC applications add administrative cost, and dye-lot variation in recycled board drives extra proof rounds. The real variable is order quantity, because certified yarn is often sold by the full case, so the certification premium spread over 3,000 labels is several times what it is over 30,000.

Item Typical range
rPET yarn premium +10–30% over virgin polyester
TC administration A fixed fee per shipment, unrelated to value
MOQ 3,000–5,000 per design for woven labels
Lead time add-on +5–10 working days to source certified yarn
