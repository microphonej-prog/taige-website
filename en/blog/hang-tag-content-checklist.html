What to Print on a Hang Tag: Nine Fields and Where They Belong

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 19, 2026

When a brand asks what to print on the hang tag, there are really three questions underneath: what the shopper looks for on the rail, what the law requires to travel with the product, and what a retailer or marketplace needs to scan. Of the nine fields that come up most often, six belong on the tag and three belong on a durable label. Put them in the wrong place and a beautifully printed tag is still a card with a compliance risk attached. This guide separates the three layers, then gives the type sizes, barcode grades and proof checks.

1. Four Fields the Shopper and the Sales Assistant Look For First

On the shop floor the first move is to find the size, the second is to confirm the style and the colour. None of these four is legally mandated, but together they decide whether the tag works: brand and collection on the hero side, style and colour code on the barcode side aligned with the barcode text, size at the largest type size on the tag.

Field Where it goes What to watch
Brand and collection Hero side Grouped with the logo, 5–8 mm type
Style and colour code Back, or above the barcode Aligned with the barcode text so both can be cross-checked
Size Large type, front or back 4–6 mm; the first thing staff look for
Retail price (optional) Better on a price ticket Printing it ties every markdown to one sheet of paper

2. Four Fields the Law Cares About, and Why the Tag Cannot Carry Them Alone

Fibre composition, care instructions, country of origin and, in the EU, the responsible person are the four fields with a real legal basis. Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 requires textile fibre composition to be indicated on the product and to remain visible through the whole chain. In the United States, the FTC rule at 16 CFR 423 requires care instructions to be permanently attached to the garment. Japan's Household Goods Quality Labelling Act makes composition, care symbols and the labeller mandatory items. Korea centres on quality labelling and country of origin. What they share is durability: a hang tag can be removed, so it cannot be the only carrier.

The practical answer is to treat the tag as a second copy rather than the only copy: one line saying see the care label, with the complete information on the durable label. It costs nothing extra and leaves the layout free for the brand story.

Information Allowed on the tag Required location
Fibre composition Duplicate only Durable label (Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011; Japan's Household Goods Quality Labelling Act)
Care instructions Duplicate only Durable label (16 CFR 423; EN ISO 3758 symbols)
Country of origin Yes Product or packaging, per market rules
EU responsible person Yes Product or packaging (GPSR (EU) 2023/988)
  • Printing it only on the tag: the moment the tag comes off the information is gone, and EU customs or a marketplace spot check will treat it as non-compliant.
  • Using a tear-off secondary card for mandatory information: a tear-off card comes off just as easily and is not durable.
  • Printing English only: language requirements follow the place of sale, and English is not a universal pass.

3. Barcode and Price: the Two Fields That Trigger Chargebacks

The barcode is a scanning interface, not decoration. The GTIN must match what the brand registered with GS1, and the print should be graded afterwards: retailers typically demand ANSI grade C, or 2.5, or better. Insufficient bar height, low contrast or a reflective laminate will each turn goods-in scanning into unreadable, and that category of defect almost always ends in a chargeback.

Price works the other way. Printing the retail price on the tag ties every markdown, and every market's price difference, to one sheet of paper. In cross-border and multi-channel selling, the usual approach is to keep the price off the tag and put it on a tear-off price ticket or in the system, so a price change costs one small label.

One rule worth remembering: barcode and style code on the tag, price on the price ticket, composition and care on the care label.

4. Layout Hierarchy and Type Size: Legibility Is Measurable

Tag area is small, so hierarchy has to come from type size and white space. Workable baselines: brand name 5–8 mm, size 4–6 mm, style code and barcode text 1.5–2 mm, other copy no smaller than 1.2 mm. Anything under 1 mm still prints at 300 dpi, but nobody reads it at 30 cm.

QR codes carry their own limits. Below roughly 0.3 mm per module, or below 12 mm overall, scan rates on mid-range phones under shop lighting drop sharply. On the back panel, leave at least a 2 mm quiet zone or the contrast at the edges gets eaten.

5. Language Sets and Version Control

More languages is not automatically better. The EU has 24 official languages and a hang tag cannot hold 24; the usual answer is a two-language set of English plus the local language, with a third in brackets where it matters. What actually needs control is versioning: one product sold into three markets is three tag versions, and each needs a traceable version number.

  • Print the version number on the back or under the barcode, matching the number on the purchase order.
  • The PO should name the version, the language set and the target market, not just one batch of hang tags.
  • When a version changes, segregate the old stock with a label so two versions never ship in one carton.
  • Do not run different market versions from one plate; mixed plates are the most common reason a proof passes and production fails.

6. Proof Checks: Five Items You Can Verify on the Spot

  • Copy proof: check against the approved text character by character, punctuation and accents included. This is the one that slips.
  • Barcode: scan it at least three times and ask for the ANSI grade report; reject anything below grade C.
  • Colour: ΔE ≤ 1.5 for brand colours and ≤ 2.0 for the rest, with a rub test on heavy dark solids.
  • Hole: at least 8 mm from the edge, diameter matched to the cord or fastener, no burr on the rim.
  • Material and GSM: measured within the 300–400 g/m² band and inside ±5%, on the paper grade that was approved.

A hang tag does not carry much information. Of the nine fields, six genuinely need to be printed and three belong on a durable label. Get the placement right, put type sizes and barcode grades into the specification, and the tag stops being a design file and becomes a trims item you can inspect on arrival.
