Poland Apparel Labelling: Polish Text, BDO Registration and Packaging EPR

By TAIGE Packaging  ·  Updated November 5, 2026

Poland is the largest apparel market in Central and Eastern Europe and one of the main gateways for Chinese garments and trims into the EU. Plenty of exporters ship beautifully made hang tags, woven labels and care labels, then see the goods rejected in Warsaw for just three reasons: fibre names in English, consumer information without Polish, and packaging never registered in the BDO system.

This guide works through four layers — regulation, language, packaging and the actual trim configuration — and closes with a specification list you can paste into a request for quotation plus seven pre-shipment checks.

1. Three Rule Sets Press on One Label

Selling apparel in Poland means meeting three requirements from three different sources at once; miss any one and the goods stop at retail.

  • EU level: Regulation (EU) 1007/2011 requires fibre composition in descending weight order, undeclared fibres normally below 3%, and no invented fibre names.
  • Polish level: the Polish Language Act requires consumer-facing information in Polish, which covers composition, care instructions, sizing and warnings on the label.
  • Packaging and product safety: packaging must be registered in BDO and reported for EPR by material and weight, while GPSR requires a traceable EU responsible person.

2. Polish Fibre Names: A Missing Diacritic Is a Non-Conformity

Every official language version of the EU regulation carries its own statutory fibre names, and the Polish version uses diacritics. A missing ogonek (ą, ę) or stroke (ł, ż) on the label is a different matter for market surveillance — and it is the most frequent cheap mistake in this market.

Fibre Statutory Polish name Common mistake
Cotton bawełna Written as bawelna, losing the ę
Polyester poliester Replaced by the English polyester
Viscose wiskoza bambus used instead of the legal name
Elastane elastan Written as spandex, not an EU legal name
Polyamide poliamid Swapped with poliester or omitted
Wool wełna Blend percentages missing or out of order
💡 Tip: Polish ą, ę, ł, ż and ś must be typeset as real glyphs, never substituted with lookalike Latin letters from an English keyboard. We check every character before artwork is finalised and ask the client to confirm the spelling once more before printing.

3. Care Symbols Are International, the Words Must Be Polish

ISO 3758 care symbols travel without translation; everything around them — written instructions, size marking, origin and responsible person — must be in Polish and must stay legible for the expected life of the garment.

  • Care: pair symbols with a short Polish line, for example pranie 30°C, rather than symbols alone.
  • Sizing: Polish retail works on EU sizes; adding a height range noticeably cuts size-driven returns.
  • Origin: garments normally carry Wyprodukowano w Chinach, which must match the origin on the customs declaration.
  • Responsible person: GPSR requires a name and address inside the EU; naming a country alone is not complete information.

4. BDO Registration and Packaging EPR: Who Files, and What

Polish packaging producer responsibility runs on registration, reporting and recovery targets. A company that first places packaging on the Polish market must register in the BDO system — the database of products, packaging and waste — filed through the regional marshal, then keep records by material and weight, and may use an authorised recovery organisation to meet targets.

  • Who registers: the company placing packaging on the Polish market or its Polish-side entity; in cross-border e-commerce this usually lands on the importer or the local seller.
  • What is filed: material category (plastic, paper, aluminium, glass and so on), weight, source and destination.
  • Reporting rhythm: an annual summary plus ongoing records, reconcilable with purchase invoices and shipping documents.
  • Cost of skipping it: beyond fines, marketplaces may delist the listing outright — far more expensive than registering.

Fee bands depend on material and weight and are revised yearly, so this article deliberately quotes no numbers — but a quotation must know the weight of a single bag, or the whole EPR cost disappears from the price.

5. Configuring the Four Trim Families for Poland

  • Hang tag: brand on the front, Polish composition and care summary on the back, with a QR code that lands on a Polish-language page.
  • Neck label (woven): brand and size; only a dense weave keeps diacritics clean, and we suggest a minimum cap height of 2 mm.
  • Care label: a multi-page folded label with Polish text and ISO 3758 symbols that survives five or more washes without losing print or curling.
  • Packaging: PE or CPE bags printed with the recycling mark and material code, with void space kept low in line with the staged PPWR requirements.

6. Seven Checks Before the Goods Leave

  1. Fibre composition in descending weight order, totalling 100%, with undeclared fibres under 3%.
  2. Check every Polish fibre name and diacritic one by one; no English substitutes.
  3. Care symbols match the real process: wash temperature, tumble drying, chlorine bleaching.
  4. Size label matches EU size and height range, with no conflict between neck label and hang tag.
  5. Origin wording matches the customs origin and traces back to a batch.
  6. The EU responsible person is named with a full address, not just a country or a platform.
  7. Packaging material and per-unit weight match the BDO filing and reconcile with invoices.
