Poly Bag Printing Methods: Printing, Labeling & Hot Stamping — How to Choose

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated August 17, 2026

Branding on garment poly bags is done mainly through three poly bag printing methods: direct printing, labeling and hot stamping. They differ sharply in color quality, MOQ and cost — choosing wrong affects both look and budget. This guide compares the four main options so you can pick the right fit.

1. Gravure Printing: The Choice for High Volume

Gravure uses engraved cylinders to transfer ink onto the film, giving saturated colors and fine detail — the most common way to print garment poly bags. It suits a single design at high volume, typically tens of thousands of pieces, where unit cost drops to its lowest.

  • High color fidelity, handles gradients and fine logos
  • Good ink adhesion, resistant to rubbing and scuffing
  • High minimum quantity, uneconomical for small runs
  • Cylinder engraving is costly and slow — best for few SKUs at scale

2. Flexo Printing: The Flexible Mid-Size Option

Flexo uses flexible photopolymer plates that cost far less than gravure cylinders, suiting small-to-mid runs and frequent design changes. Color quality trails gravure slightly but covers most one-color and multi-color brand printing needs.

  • Fast, cheap plate-making, great for frequent changes
  • Best for clean 1–4 color designs
  • Flexible MOQ — trial orders welcome

3. Adhesive Labels: The Savior for Small Runs & Many SKUs

Sticking adhesive labels onto clear poly bags avoids plate-making for the bag itself; the label can carry foil stamping, matte lamination or die-cut shapes. Ideal for e-commerce trials, mixed multi-SKU batches and promo packaging — the most cost-effective poly bag printing method at small volumes.

  • No plates, zero changeover cost, print on demand
  • Label finishes are controllable: matte film, foil, clear labels
  • Switch easily between styles, languages and sizes

4. Hot Stamping: Minimalist Premium Look

Hot stamping presses metallic foil (gold, silver, rose gold) directly onto the bag — best for clean logos and short text. The metallic sheen stands out on clear film; common in gift sets, boutique retail and premium lingerie brands.

  • One foil color per pass; multi-color means multiple passes
  • Suits refined logos on dark or clear bags
  • Low MOQ — a differentiator for premium brands
