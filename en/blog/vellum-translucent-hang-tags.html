Vellum Hang Tags: What Translucent Paper Can and Cannot Do

By TAGE Packaging &nbsp;·&nbsp; Updated November 22, 2026

A translucent hang tag is the quietest move in a tag range: light passes through the sheet, the customer sees the fabric texture through the paper, and the tag becomes part of the product display. Lingerie, bridal, swimwear and beauty brands ask for it first. It is also the easiest tag to ruin. A heavy ink build shows through as a stain on the reverse, lamination removes the translucency entirely, and a poorly placed hole tears within two weeks. This guide covers what the stock can hold, how ink behaves, and a one page spec you can send to a supplier.

1. What Translucent Paper Actually Is

Vellum on a hang tag means a translucent cellulose paper. It is not the parchment of medieval manuscripts, and it is not the opaque inkjet vellum sold for certificates. There is no coating doing the work: high beating and fibre arrangement scatter the light, and some stocks are passed briefly through a sulphuric acid bath, which is where the Chinese name for tracing paper comes from. Translucency, whiteness and stiffness pull against each other: the more transparent the sheet, the greyer it reads; the whiter it is, the more milky it looks; the heavier it is, the stiffer and the less see-through.

  • 90 to 110 g/m²: the most transparent, ideal for reading the fabric through the tag, but the die-cut edge curls easily
  • 120 to 140 g/m²: the balance point between translucency and stiffness, and the weight we ship most
  • 150 to 180 g/m²: closer to semi-opaque, stable when punched and folded, suited to higher priced brands
  • Two 120 g/m² sheets can look like cream paper or grey tracing paper depending on whiteness, so specify whiteness on the sample request

2. Where It Earns Its Place

  • Lingerie, swimwear and eveningwear: the customer sees lace, satin and embroidery through the tag
  • Beauty and fragrance apparel capsules: gift feel without a plastic tag
  • Monochrome minimal brands: with one logo on the sheet, the translucency is the design
  • Same stock as the paper bag and wrap: one material across the set makes the recycling message simple and honest

4. Printing: Ink Sits on the Surface

There is almost no coating to absorb ink, so every dot stays on the surface. The upside is a crisp edge and a faint raised feel. The downside is that a 100 percent solid will always show through to the reverse, where it reads as a stain. A white underlay lifts the colour back, at the cost of killing translucency in that area, so the white belongs only where the sheet should not be see-through.

  • For large dark areas, lay white first, or the reverse looks stained
  • Use single black or a spot colour for small type and hairlines; CMYK builds go grey and soft on translucent stock
  • When printing both sides, offset the dark areas so the two faces do not fight each other
  • Laser toner cracks along a score, so folded tags should be offset printed; keep digital for flat, unfolded formats

5. Finishing and Attachment

  • Skip lamination. Film cancels the translucency and you pay extra for what is now ordinary card
  • Score first, then fold. Translucent paper has short fibres and will fur and whiten on a raw fold
  • Punch 3 to 4 mm, add a clear or matching reinforcement patch below 120 g/m², and keep the hole at least 5 mm from the edge
  • Match the string to the sheet in cotton or waxed cord. A metal eyelet tears thin stock, so add a washer if you must use one
💡 Tip: approve translucent tags on top of the actual fabric, never on white paper. Judging on a white desk overstates the translucency, and it is the most common sample dispute on this tag type.

6. Keep the Sustainability Claims Honest

A translucent tag is a mono-material paper product, and that is a real end-of-life advantage over card plus plastic film. But 100 percent recyclable and fully eco-friendly are absolute claims, and EU environmental claim rules plus national advertising law treat them strictly. A defensible line is paper based, recyclable in local paper streams, with the FSC or recycled content certificate on file: a buyer who asks can be answered, which is worth more than a slogan on the tag.

  • Paper recyclability depends on the local collection system, and CEPI or 4evergreen assessment methods give you a technical basis
  • A hang tag is not packaging. EU packaging rules such as PPWR place obligations on packaging, so do not present them as tag obligations
  • Fibre composition, care and origin still belong on the sewn-in label (EU Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011); origin marking follows the destination market's own rules
  • A compostable claim needs a test report such as EN 13432. Without one, write paper based and recyclable instead
