In India, a Garment Poly Bag Is a Declared Material, Not a Free Extra

By TAGE Packaging · Updated November 27, 2026

Brands selling apparel into India are stuck on two questions: the importer wants EPR registration data for the packaging, and the marketplace wants a written material and thickness declaration for every garment bag. India's Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016 place producers, importers and brand owners (PIBOs) under one obligation; the 2022 EPR guidelines break it into registration, annual returns, recycling targets and recycled content. For a trims supplier shipping out of Dongguan, that turns a 30 µm LDPE bag into a material declaration somebody will check line by line.

1. Whose Obligation Is It, Really

The duty sits with whoever places the packaging on the Indian market: the producer, the importer, or the brand owner. A Chinese trims factory does not register on the CPCB EPR portal, but almost every number in that registration comes from the supplier's paperwork: material, film thickness, grammage, recycled content, and the evidence behind each. When a brand sells through an Indian distributor, the distributor is the importer of record and holds the registration; the brand still has to hand over the data, and the marketplace checks the brand's name. Write the split into the purchase order, because in a dispute only what was documented exists.

2. Which of Your Trims Count as Plastic Packaging

India's EPR guidelines split plastic packaging into four categories: rigid, flexible (films, bags, pouches), multi-layered, and compostable plastic or plastic sheets. Garment bags, mailers and shrink film all land in the flexible category; hang tags, woven labels and care labels are paper and textile and stay out of the plastic tonnage. The category decides where recycling targets and recycled content duties attach — not whether a material is good. Remember that and you avoid spending a quarter on a proposal to convert everything to paper.

  • Garment bag (LDPE or HDPE, 30-40 µm): flexible category, declared by material and weight
  • Mailer with bubble lining or paper laminate: flexible, but aluminium foil or multi-material builds can move it to the multi-layered category
  • Shrink and stretch film: flexible, and its weight is routinely underestimated because it is thin
  • Desiccant sachet (paper or PE laminate): usually counted as a packaging component, so name the material when the brand declares

3. The 120-Micron Rule and Where It Stops

The 2021 amendment, effective 30 September 2022, raised the minimum thickness of plastic carry bags from 50 to 120 microns, and of non-woven carry bags from 25 to 90 g/m². It governs carry bags — the bag a shopper takes home. A 30-40 µm garment bag wrapping clothing inside a carton is not a carry bag and does not become 120 µm because of this rule. Being precise pays both ways: when a buyer pushes a thicker film citing India, you can show the category logic; when the requirement comes from a marketplace packaging standard, follow the platform, because platform standards are routinely stricter than the statute. Measure thickness to ISO 4593 and put sampling points and tolerances in the spec.

4. Recycled Content: What You May Print, What You Must Prove

India's EPR guidelines set recycled content obligations for plastic packaging from 2025-26, stepping up through 2028-29, with flexible packaging on a looser schedule than rigid. The moment a percentage is printed, ISO 14021 requires the claim to be specific, verifiable and qualified as pre-consumer or post-consumer. GRS does not cover PE film — it is built for textile products — so do not hand a GRS certificate to support recycled content in a poly bag. Ask for the film maker's batch-level traceability statement, PCR purchase invoices, and material notes aligned with IS 14534. Take the current-year percentage from the CPCB portal rather than recycling last year's artwork.

5. Where the Cost Actually Lands

For a bag of the same size, the price gap comes from three places: film thickness (each 5 µm removed uses less resin but raises the burst rate), PCR share (higher haze affects print colour, and supply plus certification set the premium), and documentation (testing, traceability, and a return that is refiled every year). The real cost difference is usually not unit price but the fact that one bag must satisfy three different forms — brand, marketplace, importer. Duplicate testing and duplicate sampling often cost more than the resin itself. Merge the three forms into one spec first, then discuss unit price.

Three checks before you order: which Indian plastic packaging category the bag falls into; whether every number printed on it can be backed by a document within thirty seconds; and whether the 120-micron requirement is law, a platform standard, or a misunderstanding.
