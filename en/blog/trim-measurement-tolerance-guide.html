Trims Measurement and Tolerance: What to Measure, With What, and How Much

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 29, 2026

Most arguments about trim dimensions are not about bad parts — they are about two parties measuring different things: one measures the net size after cutting, the other the gross size including the fold; one estimates to the nearest millimetre with a tape, the other reads 0.02mm on a caliper; one measures straight out of the carton, the other after two days of rest. The same lot is then called out of tolerance by the buyer and perfectly in spec by the supplier. This guide sets out gauge selection, the state of the goods, tolerance ranges by product family, sampling and judgement, and how to write it all into the specification.

1. Three Sources of Disagreement: Datum, Gauge, State

Align these three points first and most dimension disputes disappear. None of them is about manufacturing capability — they are purely questions of definition, and all three can be fixed on paper.

  • Different datum: for a hang tag, do you measure the outer profile or the printed panel? For a woven label, the total length with folds or the net length? For a hole, edge to hole centre or centre to centre? Without a written rule, each side follows its own habit.
  • Different gauge: a tape reads to 1mm, a caliper to 0.02mm. Measuring a 20mm label with a tape already carries one or two millimetres of interpretation error; using it to judge a 0.5mm tolerance can only produce an argument.
  • Different state: paper trims grow and shrink with humidity, textile trims stretch under tension, freshly woven labels have not finished relaxing, and bags come off the machine still warm. Resting and conditioning before measurement are not optional.

2. Choosing the Gauge: Precision Must Match the Tolerance

A useful rule: the minimum reading of the gauge should be smaller than one fifth of the tolerance. To judge ±1mm you need a gauge that reads to at least 0.2mm; to judge a ±0.5mm die-cut dimension you need a caliper good to 0.1mm or better. 3. Conditions: When You Measure Matters as Much as How

  • Equilibrium: let paper trims rest several hours in stable temperature and humidity. In damp air paper swells and a long edge can gain 0.3–0.8mm.
  • Relaxation: woven labels and tapes need time to recover after weaving. Rest them at least 24 hours in normal conditions before measuring to get a stable dimension.
  • Tension and flatness: measure tapes flat under light tension, roughly 0.5–1kg, without stretching or letting them hang. Lay tags and paper bags flat so handling does not bow the edge you are reading.
  • Label the state: when a report contains both before and after wash figures, mark the state and wash method in every cell, or the data cannot be compared.

The ranges below reflect common industry practice rather than a formal standard. Higher-precision requirements — labels for automatic applicators, tags for automatic stringing — can be tightened, but cost and lead time will move accordingly.

5. Sampling, Judgement and Out-of-Tolerance Lots

A dimension verdict needs a sample size and a decision rule — saying eight out of ten passed means nothing on its own. The usual approach is AQL counting sampling: pick the sample size from the lot size, fix the acceptance number, and count each measured field as a separate inspection item.

  • Prioritise critical dimensions: features that affect assembly or appearance — hole position, fold position, print position, seal width — are critical and take the tighter tolerance; decorative dimensions can be relaxed.
  • Three ways to handle a miss: a minor deviation that affects neither fit nor appearance can be accepted by concession, with the range and the price adjustment in writing; a cosmetic-only deviation can be sorted or reworked; anything affecting assembly or compliance is rejected and replaced.
  • Retention samples and re-testing: each side keeps one approved sealed sample with the approval date and version. In a dispute, re-measure against the sealed sample and, if needed, send it to a third-party lab.

6. The Measurement Clause for Your Specification

Drop the paragraph below straight into your enquiry email or specification and most of the back-and-forth disappears:

Example clause: dimensions follow this specification. Tag outlines are measured on the outer profile; woven labels are measured net of folds; fold and hole positions are measured edge to centre. Goods rest 24 hours in normal temperature and humidity before measurement; tapes are measured flat under 0.5–1kg tension; paper grammage is sampled with a standard-area cutter and converted on a balance; thickness is read on a defined-pressure gauge with the pressure stated in the report. Tolerances are as marked per field; unmarked fields take ±1mm on length and width and ±10% on thickness. Sampling follows AQL 2.5 by attributes, with hole, fold and print position plus seal width as critical items. Out-of-tolerance lots are handled by minor concession, cosmetic rework, or rejection and replacement where assembly or compliance is affected, with method and cost agreed in writing before any handling.
