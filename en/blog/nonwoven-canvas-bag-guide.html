Nonwoven and Canvas Bag Guide: Custom Tote Bags for Clothing Brands

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated September 25, 2026

Nonwoven and canvas bags are the most underrated brand touchpoint a clothing brand owns: handed over in store, taken home from a trade show, used to pack a web order — a single bag can stay in a customer's hands for months. Four decisions drive the result: material, weight and size, printing method, and whether you may legally print environmental claims. This guide takes them one by one, with grammage and size tables, printing selection advice, and a sample approval checklist you can copy straight into a quotation request.

The article runs in this order: why an own-brand bag is worth it, four mainstream materials compared, how to set grammage, size and construction, printing methods and logo reproduction, environmental claims and where the legal line sits, then MOQ, lead time and inspection.

1. Why a Clothing Brand Should Own Its Bags

A bag is not a hang tag: the tag is thrown away the moment it comes off, while the bag tends to be kept. Its value is not on the cost sheet but in how many times it gets reused. Three settings repay the investment best:

  • Boutiques and pop-ups: every customer walking out with your bag is moving advertising. Mid-weight nonwoven keeps the cost predictable and can still be built as a side-gusset or flat-bottomed shopper.
  • Trade shows and wholesale: visitors rarely keep your business card but often keep the bag. A show bag must hold a catalogue and trim cards, so choose a bottomed, gusseted construction with a reinforced handle.
  • E-commerce and gifting: premium lines use canvas or cotton for dust and gift bags, replacing paper, with far higher reuse rates and better durability through returns.

2. Four Main Materials Compared: Nonwoven, Cotton, Canvas, RPET

Material Typical weight Hand and look Printing
PP nonwoven 80–140 gsm Stiff, slightly papery, widest colour range Screen, heat transfer and laminated full-colour all mature
Cotton 6–10 oz (about 200–340 gsm) Soft and natural, good for personal and gift use Mainly screen print; heat transfer for multi-colour graphics
Canvas (heavy cotton) 8–14 oz (about 270–470 gsm) The most substantial and premium feel, built for long reuse Screen print, embroidered patch or sewn leather patch
Recycled PET (RPET) Made to order, commonly 80–150 gsm Close to virgin polyester; available as nonwoven or woven Screen or heat transfer; recycled content must be stated

One-line selection rule: nonwoven for stores and events, canvas for gifting and premium lines, RPET when you claim recycled content — backed by a GRS transaction certificate.

3. Setting Weight, Size and Construction

Size follows contents first, feel second. A bag for one sweater and a bag for three folded shirts are different structures. The three sizes below work well as sampling starting points:

Size W × H × gusset (cm) Fits
Small 25 × 20 × 10 One tee, underwear, kidswear
Medium 30 × 25 × 12 Two shirts, knitwear, general boutique use
Large 38 × 35 × 12 Outerwear, down jackets, show catalogues
  • Handles: self-material handles cost least but carry little; for weight, switch to webbing and add bar-tacks or a reinforcement patch at the mouth.
  • Bottom and gusset: an 8–12 cm gusset covers most needs; for shoe boxes or gift boxes, increase both gusset and base width or the bag will bulge.
  • Lining and hardware: a lining (polyester, foil, EVA) lifts the feel and keeps dust out; zips, magnetic snaps and inner pockets depend on use, and each addition extends lead time.

4. Printing Methods and Logo Reproduction

  • Screen printing: cheapest for one or a few spot colours, with a thick, rub-resistant lay. Nonwoven and canvas are textured, so fine lines and small type fill in — keep text at 4–5 mm minimum.
  • Heat transfer: best for photo-level graphics, gradients and multi-colour logos, with the sharpest detail — but large transfers on dark fabric can show a pale edge, so sample first.
  • Laminated full colour: a printed film is bonded to the nonwoven, allowing full-bleed graphics and photos. It costs more than screen printing and suits campaigns and collaborations.
  • Trim-based branding: canvas bags accept woven labels, leather patches, embroidery or metal plates — the same trims used on garments, which gives the most consistent brand identity and is a common package request for trim suppliers.

5. Environmental Claims: What You May and May Not Print

A bag is the easiest place to make a green claim and the easiest place for a regulator to catch one. The EU Green Claims direction and the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) both require claims to be specific, verifiable and substantiated; vague wording such as "eco" or "green" is being phased out. Three practical rules:

  • If you print it, prove it: "made with 50% recycled polyester" needs a GRS transaction certificate or test report; "reusable" is stronger with a stated number of uses or a load test.
  • Certification logos are not free to use: OEKO-TEX, GRS and FSC are managed trademarks, usable only by the certificate holder, with authorisation and the official artwork, usually with the certificate number. See our guide to certification logos and claims.
  • Chemical and safety limits: bags exported to the EU or US should be checked against limits for azo dyes, phthalates and heavy metals (lead, cadmium); ink odour and migration also matter, and packaging linked to children carries additional small-parts and suffocation-warning duties.

6. MOQ, Lead Time and Inspection Points

  • MOQ: for nonwoven it is set by the shape and the number of print colours; canvas, with more sewing and material, usually starts higher. Adding one ink or one component to the same bag can push the minimum up.
  • Lead time: samples in 3 to 7 days, bulk in 10 to 25 days; linings, shaped sewing or embroidery add time. Before peak season, allow at least four weeks.
  • Six inspection points: measure actual grammage against the quote, check logo position and size tolerance, colour against the approved sample, handle strength and bar-tacks, stitch density and skipped stitches, plus odour and ink adhesion.
💡 Tip: for a first run, sample one size in one material, lock the ink colours, handle construction and logo size, then order in batches for store, trade show and e-commerce. That keeps the per-item minimum manageable and leaves only one sample to approve. We usually ship samples in 3 to 7 days and can send nonwoven and canvas swatches together for comparison.
