US Packaging EPR Has Arrived: Four Things Brands and Suppliers Must Do

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated October 27, 2026

Packaging compliance used to mean looking at Europe: PPWR, national EPR schemes, plastic taxes. From 2026 that reading is out of date — the United States is no longer a market without packaging law. California, Oregon, Colorado, Maine and Minnesota have passed packaging extended producer responsibility (EPR) statutes, and the three most ordinary items on a garment order — the inner poly bag, the branded paper bag and the mailer — all fall inside their scope.

For Chinese exporters and apparel brands this is a cost question and a data question at once: packaging has to be weighable, material has to be classifiable, structure has to be reportable. What follows covers the state of play in five states, who counts as a producer, what counts as packaging, the six data points a supplier must give, and how to land it in procurement.

1. Five States: One Skeleton, Different Timetables

The statutes differ in detail but share a skeleton: producers fund the system, a producer responsibility organisation (PRO) collects and disburses, and costs are allocated by material type and tonnage. What varies is scope, start dates and recycling targets.

StateLaw and yearScopeWhat it means for garment packaging
CaliforniaSB 54 (2022)Single-use packaging and plastic food service wareBy 2032: all single-use packaging recyclable or compostable, 25% source reduction, 65% recycling rate — inner bags and mailers included
OregonRecycling Modernization Act (2021)Paper, plastic, metal and glass packagingProducers report and pay through the PRO; paper and plastic bags are both in, and paper is not an exemption
ColoradoHB22-1355 (2022)Packaging and printing paperPhased rollout, with fees tied to recyclability; multi-material structures usually pay more

Note: fee schedules and reporting definitions are still being published state by state. Take exact figures from the state environmental agency and the PRO's formal notices. This article gives the logic, not legal advice.

2. Who Counts as a Producer: Cross-border Sellers Included

Most states define the producer as whoever brings the packaging onto the state market: brand owners, importers, first sellers. Chinese sellers shipping direct or selling a private label through a marketplace are usually captured too, because the obligation follows the brand, not the place of manufacture.

  • Brand owner: packaging responsibility follows the brand, even when a supplier manufactures the bags
  • Importer: a trader clearing goods in its own name can be treated as a producer too, sharing the reporting with the brand
  • Marketplace seller: for private-label goods sold through a platform, most states still point at the brand; some platforms already withhold fees or require packaging data in the seller portal
  • Small-producer exemptions: thresholds exist, but they vary by state, material and annual volume — never assume you sit below them

3. What Counts as Packaging: Weigh All Three Layers

EPR classifies packaging by layer, and a garment order is in scope at all three. What gets missed is not the carton — it is the first layer.

LayerWhat it is on a garment orderReporting note
PrimaryInner poly bag, dust bag, the small bag around each hang tagSmall unit weight but huge piece counts — the most commonly missed
SecondaryBranded paper bag, gift box, tissue, stickersMulti-material structures must be split by material
TransportMailer, master carton, carton tape, void fillThe biggest tonnage in e-commerce shipments

Hang tags and woven labels are normally treated as part of the product, not packaging; but the small poly sleeve around the tag and the poly strip used to attach it are primary packaging. That is the line most often missed in reporting, and the first one an auditor asks about.

4. Six Data Points Your Supplier Must Provide

The brand files the report, but the data can only come from the packaging supplier. Write these six into the specification and every later filing can pull straight from it instead of re-weighing and re-asking.

  • Material build: mono-material or multi-layer; for multi-layer, state each layer's material, order and share — not just PE or paper
  • Unit weight: per bag and per box to 0.1 g. This is the multiplier on every fee, so a rough estimate becomes a fee error
  • Size and gauge: decides whether it counts as small-format packaging and feeds both fee bands and recyclability calls
  • Recycled content: the PCR share and the paperwork behind it — claim 30% and you must be able to show where it came from
  • Recyclability call: mono-PE, paper-based and paper-plastic laminate are three different answers; do not wave it through with the word recyclable
  • Print and finishing: lamination, foil, windows and full-coverage ink all shift both weight and recyclability, so list them separately

5. Four Steps in Procurement

You do not have to wait for the full fee schedule. Four steps cut cost and risk at once, and none of them changes how the product looks.

  • Baseline: weigh all three layers for each SKU and total the per-unit packaging weight. Most brands find on the first pass that the inner bag is half of it
  • Reduce: drop what is not needed — tissue inside the paper bag, the sleeve around each tag, the plastic tray in the gift box. Reduction is the only move that cuts fee and material load at once
  • Go mono-material: swap paper-plastic laminate for mono-PE or pure paper, replace a window with a die-cut, replace metallised film with matte PE. The visual difference is usually smaller than expected
  • Put it in the spec: make material build, weight, recycled content and recyclability standing fields delivered with every order, not a one-off spreadsheet

6. Three Common Misreadings

  • We use paper, so we are clear: paper-based packaging is in scope and billed by tonnage too, and coated paper does not judge the same as uncoated
  • The platform will handle it: some platforms withhold the fee, but the data obligation stays with the brand, and penalties for misreporting land on the producer
  • The per-unit fee is tiny: it is, but multiply by annual tonnage, then add delisting and penalties for non-compliance, and the order of magnitude changes
What EPR really changes is not the fee but the fact that packaging weight and material become calculable for the first time. Brands that can count weight move fastest on reduction and mono-material; suppliers who cannot explain their material build get filtered out at every reporting cycle.
