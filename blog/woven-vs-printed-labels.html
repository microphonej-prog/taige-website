Woven Labels vs Printed Labels: Which Is Right for Your Garments?

By TAGE Packaging  ·  Updated 2026

Brand labels are a small detail with a big impact — they're on every garment you sell, and customers feel them against their skin. Two main options dominate the market: woven labels and printed (heat transfer / printed) labels. Both have strengths; the right choice depends on your product, budget and brand image.

What Is a Woven Label?

A woven label is created by weaving threads together — usually satin, polyester, or cotton — to form the design. The logo becomes part of the fabric itself, so it never peels, cracks or fades with washing.

  • Satin — smooth, glossy, premium feel; ideal for main labels and size labels
  • Polyester — durable and colorfast, great for activewear
  • Cotton — soft and natural, perfect for organic or baby clothing
  • Taffeta — fine and smooth, common for size labels

What Is a Printed (Heat Transfer) Label?

Printed labels are made by printing the design onto a film or transfer paper, then applying it to the garment with heat and pressure. Some are printed directly on fabric strips.

  • Heat transfer labels — printed on a thin film, pressed onto the garment; great for seamless sportswear
  • Printed fabric labels — ink printed onto woven or non-woven fabric, then folded and sewn

When to Choose Woven

  • Your brand logo is the star — you want it to feel premium
  • Main neck labels that will be washed repeatedly
  • Mid-to-high-end apparel, denim, formal wear

When to Choose Printed / Heat Transfer

  • Care labels with washing instructions (need fine text)
  • Seamless or performance sportswear where tags must be invisible
  • Small quantities or frequent design changes (new collections)
  • Budget-conscious startups testing the market

Many Brands Use Both

The most common combination: a woven main label for the brand name, plus a printed care label with washing instructions and composition. This balances premium feel where customers look, with cost efficiency and legibility where they don't.
